## Full concept for owner review: Cardio zones and talk test

**Hook:** A walk can feel easy while a run makes speech come in short bursts. Breath gives a clue to effort.  
**Rule:** The talk test sorts effort by speech. At a moderate pace, talking works but singing does not. At a vigorous pace, only a few words fit before a breath. These are broad effort bands.  
**Claim:** CDC uses talk but not sing for moderate effort, and only a few words before a breath for vigorous effort.  
**Misconception:** “One speed fits all” — the talk test gives broad effort bands that can differ between people.  
**Source:** CDC intensity page, linked above.  
**Quest:** In a made-up walk, ride and run, mark where speech is easy, short or in between.

1. **cardio-talk-test-1 — predict, level 2.** On a hostel walk, Mira can chat but cannot sing a line. Which effort band fits the talk test?
   - **a (correct):** Moderate effort. **Feedback:** Being able to talk but not sing is the CDC clue for this broad band.
   - **b:** No effort at all. **Feedback:** Speech has changed from rest, so the walk is asking the body to work.
2. **cardio-talk-test-2 — scenario, level 2.** During college cricket drills, Arjun can say only a few words before a breath. Which band is closer?
   - **a (correct):** Vigorous effort. **Feedback:** Short speech bursts before a breath match the CDC clue for hard aerobic work.
   - **b:** Easy rest. **Feedback:** Rest does not tend to cut speech to a few words at a time.
3. **cardio-talk-test-3 — estimate, level 2.** Three cardio cues are easy singing, talk but not sing, and only a few words. About how many fit the moderate talk-test band?
   - **a (correct):** One cue. **Feedback:** Talking but not singing marks moderate effort; the other cues point lower or higher.
   - **b:** All three cues. **Feedback:** The speech clues split broad effort bands, so they cannot all mean moderate work.
   - **Scored answer:** 1 cue (range 0.5 to 1.5).
4. **cardio-talk-test-4 — choose, level 1.** What does the talk test use as a clue to effort?
   - **a (correct):** How speech changes with breath. **Feedback:** As effort rises, talking and singing can become harder to keep up.
   - **b:** The colour of running shoes. **Feedback:** Shoe colour says nothing about how hard the heart and lungs are working.
5. **cardio-talk-test-5 — why, level 3.** Two friends use the same bike speed; one can still sing and the other can only talk. Why can their effort bands differ?
   - **a (correct):** The talk test tracks each person's felt effort. **Feedback:** The same outside pace can ask different amounts of work from different bodies.
   - **b:** A fixed speed gives all people the same effort. **Feedback:** CDC calls the test relative effort because fitness and breathing can vary by person.
6. **cardio-talk-test-6 — fill-the-step, level 1.** Fill the gap: at a moderate cardio pace, a person can talk but cannot ___.
   - **a (correct):** Sing. **Feedback:** That speech clue is the quick rule for moderate work in the CDC guide.
   - **b:** Walk. **Feedback:** Walking can be moderate work; the clue is speech, not whether feet move.
7. **cardio-talk-test-7 — true-not-quite, level 3.** A student says the talk test proves an exact heart-rate zone number for every runner. What is the gap?
   - **a (correct):** It gives a broad effort clue. **Feedback:** Speech sorts rough bands; it does not read a pulse or set one number for all.
   - **b:** Speech gives an exact pulse count. **Feedback:** A talk test can mark effort, but it cannot count each heart beat.
8. **cardio-talk-test-8 — explain-it-back, level 2.** Which line best explains the talk test to a friend?
   - **a (correct):** Speech gets shorter as aerobic effort rises. **Feedback:** Talking, singing and breath gaps offer a quick way to sort rough effort levels.
   - **b:** Speech stays the same at every pace. **Feedback:** Breath can limit words as effort goes up, which is why the test works.

## Claim limits and changes

- Dropped the claim that each person needs a bigger weight at every session. The ACSM stand allows several routes to a rising task and notes that a new challenge matters most for long-term progress.
- Dropped a fixed appetite hormone change after each short night. NIH gives the broad link; human study results vary.
- Dropped the claim that a static stretch always prevents injury or always harms output. The review reports mixed injury data and task-dependent output.
- Dropped a precise “zone 2” heart-rate number from speech alone. CDC calls the talk test a rough guide to relative effort.
- Dropped the claim that every Indian pack must print a per 100 g line. FSSAI also permits a per 100 ml or single-pack base, so the exercises compare two mock packs that both show per 100 g.
