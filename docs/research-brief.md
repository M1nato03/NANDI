# Ship One Offline App That Rewards Doing

Prime Pocket should become one Vite + React + TypeScript web app. It stores every record on the phone in IndexedDB through Dexie, deploys free as a static site, and runs on his Nothing Phone 3a Pro as a sideloaded Capacitor APK from the week the planner needs reminders. The reason is simple: a plain PWA cannot fire a reminder at a chosen time while it is closed, because Chrome abandoned the only API built for that. Nothing in the running app needs AI. The puzzle generators, workout progression, postpone rules and reward economy are all deterministic code. Claude sits at the edges as an optional author: it writes validated content packs into a repo the app pulls from, and it reads the JSON summary the app exports. All three modes pay into one append-only XP ledger. Tasks and workouts supply about 70% of a typical 250-XP day, and puzzles are capped at 80 XP. The ledger feeds a quadratic level curve, a 300-seat monthly season ladder, forgiving streaks with a weekly "Clean Week" as the headline, and a cosmetic currency with no randomness. "Firm but not too strict" comes down to one rule: **postponing costs a decision, never points**. Every postpone needs a new date and time. The second postpone asks for a first step of ten minutes or less. The third offers three choices: split the task, do ten minutes now, or move it to Someday. A fourth postpone retires the task, and after five free postpones in a week each further one needs a reason. Training pays for finishing the planned full-body session (three a week, with two as a legitimate minimum) and never for extra sets. Puzzles become more fun through a weekday difficulty arc, a season-long mystery, collectible technique cards and hard stopping screens, not through more puzzles. The anime-inspired "Proving Ground" theme borrows genre conventions, which copyright leaves free, and uses no characters, names or logos. That matters in India, where fan art has no clear safe harbour. Nearly every threshold here is a starting point, not a tested value. The app should log a one-week baseline before the economy switches on, and it should be judged by night-time Reels minutes and lights-out time. Time spent inside Prime Pocket is a guard metric that must not rise.

## A web codebase in an Android shell, because PWAs cannot keep time

### Chrome installs the app in seconds but will not wake it on schedule

Installing a web app on Android Chrome is now almost frictionless. Chrome needs only a manifest with a name, 192 px and 512 px icons, a start URL and a standalone display mode, served over HTTPS, after at least one tap and 30 seconds of viewing ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/install-criteria)). Since **Chrome 108 on mobile**, installing from the menu no longer requires a service worker ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/update-install-criteria)). The result is a WebAPK: a real launcher icon that also appears in Android settings. It "uses the current profile to store any data", meaning Chrome's own storage profile, and it has to ask for notification permission at runtime ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/webapks)). A service worker is still worth shipping, because offline caching and `registration.showNotification()` depend on it, and `new Notification()` throws a TypeError in nearly all mobile browsers ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Notification/Notification)). Nothing OS 4.0 (Android 16) reached the Phone 3a Pro on 2 December 2025 with a promise to tighten "background task management", but gave no technical detail ([Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/phones/nothing-phones/nothing-phone-3a-just-got-android-16-proving-budget-phones-dont-have-to-wait)). The research found no reports of that OS killing apps or delaying notifications, so the practical step is to set battery use to Unrestricted for whichever app delivers reminders.

The real constraint is time. A firm planner is useless if "start problem set at 21:30" never fires, and a PWA cannot fire it while closed. Chrome ended Notification Triggers, the only API designed for scheduled local notifications, because "it wasn't clear that we could provide consistent and reliable experiences across platforms" ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/notification-triggers)). Periodic Background Sync runs only for installed apps. Chrome sets its frequency from a site-engagement score ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/periodic-background-sync)), and one explainer puts that at about every 24–36 hours for most sites ([Gerschau](https://felixgerschau.com/periodic-background-sync-explained/)). Timers in a hidden page run once per second, then **once per minute** after five minutes hidden ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/timer-throttling-in-chrome-88)). Server-sent push works, but it moves his schedule off the phone. A Cloudflare Worker cron on the free tier could send Web Push (100,000 requests a day, five cron triggers per account) ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). Firebase's free Spark plan has no Cloud Functions to run a scheduler ([Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/pricing)). Free Supabase projects pause after seven days of inactivity ([Supabase discussion](https://github.com/orgs/supabase/discussions/37358)).

Capacitor solves the problem on the device. The same Vite build wraps into an APK whose Local Notifications plugin schedules through Android's alarm system. Android 13 and later need the `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` permission at runtime. Exact timing needs `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`, or on Android 14+ `USE_EXACT_ALARM`, which is meant "for apps where exact alarms are central". If the exact permission is denied, the plugin falls back to inexact alarms and returns only a warning, unless `isExactMandatory` is set ([Capacitor](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/local-notifications)). Android 14 stopped pre-granting `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` to most new apps. `USE_EXACT_ALARM`, by contrast, is a normal permission granted at install to calendar and alarm-clock apps ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/changes/schedule-exact-alarms)). Play limits that permission to those app types, but Play never reviews a sideloaded personal APK. That is a reading of the policy, not a verified rule. In Doze, Android allows `allowWhileIdle` alarms **once per nine minutes per app** ([Capacitor](https://capacitorjs.com/docs/apis/local-notifications)), which is fine for task reminders. A Trusted Web Activity built with Bubblewrap does not help, because it runs the same web code inside Chrome ([Bubblewrap](https://github.com/GoogleChromeLabs/bubblewrap/blob/main/packages/cli/README.md)). Sideloading is still open for now. Google's developer verification starts on 30 September 2026 in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand and expands globally "in 2027 and beyond". Apps installed over adb are exempt, and a free limited-distribution account covers up to 20 devices ([Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/06/android-developer-verification.html)).

| Capability | Installed PWA (Chrome WebAPK) | Capacitor APK (sideloaded) |
|---|---|---|
| Task reminder at an exact time, app closed | No; only with a push server | Yes; alarm-backed local notification |
| Rest-timer alert with screen off | Best effort; can arrive about a minute late, or never | Notification scheduled for the end of the rest period |
| Offline use | Yes (service worker + IndexedDB) | Yes |
| Where data lives | Chrome's profile | The APK's private WebView storage |
| What wipes the data | Clearing Chrome's storage or the site data | Clearing the APK's storage, or uninstalling it |
| Detecting Instagram during a focus block | Impossible | Possible later with a custom native plugin (unverified) |
| Install route | Chrome menu | `npx cap run android` or adb |

So the architecture is one web codebase with two shells. The Capacitor APK becomes the daily driver as soon as the planner can schedule reminders. The PWA URL stays as the development and preview channel. One trap sets the order of work. The APK's WebView and Chrome's PWA are different origins, so their IndexedDB stores are separate, and moving from one to the other means exporting and re-importing. Making that switch in week two or three, before months of history pile up, is cheap. Making it later turns it into a migration.

### Everything lives on the phone, so backup ships in week one

Chrome lets one origin use **up to 60% of total disk space**. By default that storage is best-effort and can be evicted when the device runs out of space, least recently used origin first ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/storage-for-the-web)). Calling `navigator.storage.persist()` asks Chrome to protect it. Chrome decides silently, without a prompt, from engagement, installation and notification permission. Persistent data is "only deleted if the user chooses to remove it using their site settings" ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/persistent-storage)). That last clause is the real risk. A WebAPK's data sits in Chrome's profile, so clearing Chrome's storage in Android settings plausibly wipes the PWA too. The research found no official Chrome statement on this, so it is an inference from the shared profile. In order of likelihood, the threats are: clearing Chrome or the site's data, uninstalling Chrome or resetting the phone, uninstalling the PWA, and storage eviction. `persist()` neutralises only the last.

Export and import therefore come before any feature. dexie-export-import turns the database into a Blob, keeps Dates and Blobs intact, and imports in chunks, with options such as `clearTablesBeforeImport` and `acceptVersionDiff` ([Dexie](https://dexie.org/docs/ExportImport/dexie-export-import)). The backup screen needs two buttons. The first is a Share button through the Web Share API, which must run from a tap and should be checked with `navigator.canShare` first ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/web-share)). Whether Chrome Android accepts `.json` files must be tested on the phone, with `.txt` as the fallback. The second is a Download button using `<a download>`, which always works. Restore should validate the file, preview record counts, and only then import. The app should store `lastBackupAt` and nag after a set number of days. Two version numbers must stay separate. One is the Dexie schema version, which migrates with `db.version(n).stores(...).upgrade(...)`; indexes "are dropped as soon as you don't specify them in a new version" ([Dexie](https://dexie.org/docs/Tutorial/Design#database-versioning)). The other is the backup `formatVersion`, with pure migrator functions so that any old backup file still imports. The same import path should bring in the existing claude.ai artifact's data on day one, which tests the restore code before it matters.

Four data-model rules make every later feature cheap. First, every record gets a ULID string as its key (not an auto-increment number), plus `updatedAt`, a `deleted` tombstone flag and a `deviceId`, so that sync can be bolted on later rather than migrated in. Second, every timestamp maps to a `dayKey` equal to the local date of the time minus four hours, so his 12:30 a.m. work still counts as "today" across all three modes. Third, rewards live in an append-only ledger whose entry IDs are built from the event, such as `task-done:<taskId>`. Re-running the award logic after a crash or an import can then never pay twice, and two devices' ledgers merge by simple set union. Fourth, timers are stored as absolute timestamps, never as tick counts.

```ts
// Every syncable row: id (ULID), updatedAt (ms), deleted (0|1), deviceId
// dayKey = local date of (timestamp − 4 h)  → 12:30 a.m. still belongs to "today"
db.version(1).stores({
  tasks:      'id, status, dayKey, scheduledStart, category, updatedAt', // size, firstStep, estimateMin, postponeCount
  taskEvents: 'id, taskId, kind, at',      // scheduled | postponed(reason) | started | switched | done | someday
  focus:      'id, taskId, startedAt',     // plannedMs, pauses[], overtimeMs
  exercises:  'id, pattern, source',       // free-exercise-db | custom; licence + attribution fields
  ladders:    'id, pattern',               // steps: [{exerciseId, sets:3, repMin:5, repMax:8}]
  programs:   'id, track',                 // gym | bodyweight; templates A/B; slots with progression rule
  exState:    'id',                        // loadKg, ladderStep, successStreak, failStreak, lastIncreaseAt
  sessions:   'id, dayKey, status',        // completed | minimum | partial | skipped; heatMode; readiness
  sets:       'id, sessionId, exerciseId', // type, loadKg, reps, durationS, effort, painFlag
  prs:        'id, exerciseId, kind',
  puzzleRuns: 'id, type, dayKey, seed',    // solveMs, hintsUsed, techniquesSeen[]
  cards:      'id, deckId, due',
  lessonProg: 'id, packId',
  packs:      'id, kind, version',         // validated content packs (lessons, puzzles, programs, rules)
  ledger:     'id, dayKey, mode, kind',    // append-only XP events; id = kind + ':' + refId (idempotent)
  dailyLog:   'dayKey',                    // reels bucket, lights-out time, weekly agency check
  settings:   'key'
});
```

Timers follow the same "store the truth, render the view" pattern. A rest timer persists `startedAt`, `durationMs` and its pauses, and recomputes the remaining time on every `visibilitychange` and `pageshow`. It therefore survives throttling, reloads and even the process being killed, and on return it can say "Rest finished 1:12 ago". The Screen Wake Lock API keeps the screen on during a workout. It is released automatically whenever the app is hidden, so it must be requested again on `visibilitychange` ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/web-apis/wake-lock)). Vibration needs a recent tap and can be suppressed by silent mode or Do Not Disturb ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Navigator/vibrate)). The APK adds the backstop: when a rest period starts, it schedules a local notification for its end, and cancels it if he skips or adjusts. A tempting hack is to play silent audio so the page stays awake. It fails, because Chrome's exemption requires audible sound and "a silent audio track doesn't count" ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/timer-throttling-in-chrome-88)).

### A codebase layout the agent cannot tangle

For an installed app, bundle size barely matters, because the code downloads once and is cached. What matters is how reliably Claude Code writes correct code. React 19 is about **45 KB** min+gzip, against about 5 KB for Svelte 5 and 7 KB for Solid. It also has about 190 million weekly npm downloads, against 2.7 million and 1.5 million ([PkgPulse](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/solidjs-vs-svelte-5-vs-react-reactivity-2026)). Preact offers the same API in about 3 kB ([Preact](https://preactjs.com/)). AI assistants are documented to "mix up the old reactive syntax with the new runes system" in Svelte 5 ([Khromov](https://khromov.se/how-and-why-i-built-an-mcp-server-for-svelte/)). The recommendation is React 19 with strict TypeScript, using `allowJs` so the existing plain-JS generators run unchanged until they are converted. Preact is an acceptable swap. vite-plugin-pwa generates the manifest and service worker. Its `injectManifest` strategy keeps a custom worker for `notificationclick` handlers, and `registerType: 'prompt'` means an update never reloads the app mid-workout ([vite-pwa](https://vite-pwa-org.netlify.app/guide/)). Valibot (about 1.4 kB) or Zod validate content packs at runtime ([PkgPulse](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/valibot-vs-zod-v4-2026); [Zod](https://zod.dev/packages/mini)). Vitest covers the pure logic. Playwright covers flows such as an offline reload or a backup round-trip, using mobile device profiles, permission grants and offline emulation ([Playwright](https://playwright.dev/docs/emulation)). Real-device testing over USB with `chrome://inspect` stays necessary for anything involving install or notifications.

```
src/
├─ core/        pure TypeScript, no framework, 100% unit-tested
│  ├─ time/       dayKey(), timer math
│  ├─ tasks/      postponeGate(), shrink/split rules, estimate calibration, capacity check
│  ├─ train/      progression engine, ladders, plate maths, XP eligibility
│  ├─ puzzles/    seeded generators, uniqueness counter, logic solver = grader = hint engine
│  ├─ economy/    award rules, level curve, season rank, streak + Clean Week, Shards
│  └─ rules.config.ts   ← every tunable number in ONE file (budgets, caps, curve constants)
├─ data/        Dexie schema, migrations, repositories, export/import, pack store + validators
├─ platform/    Notifier (WebNotifier | CapacitorNotifier), wakeLock, vibrate, share
└─ ui/          React screens: Today · Tasks · Train · Mind · Proving Ground (profile/ladder)
content/        JSON packs (lessons, handcrafted puzzles, programs) → validated in CI → /packs/
```

The `Notifier` interface (`schedule(id, at, title, body)`, `cancel(id)`, `listPending()`) is picked at startup with `Capacitor.isNativePlatform()`, so the UI never knows which shell it runs in. The app should reschedule all future reminders on every launch and after every edit. Whether the plugin itself restores alarms after a reboot is unverified and needs a test on the phone. Hosting the static build is free almost anywhere. Cloudflare now tells new projects to "start with Workers", and static assets cost nothing because "you only get charged when your Worker code runs" ([Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/full-stack-development-on-cloudflare-workers/)). Its header rules can mark `sw.js`, `index.html` and the manifest as `no-cache` and the hashed `/assets/*` files as immutable for a year. GitHub Pages is fine for version one, but the free tier needs a public repo and bans running a business on it ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits)), and it does not support custom headers ([GitHub community](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/60087)). That gap is tolerable because Chrome has bypassed the HTTP cache for service worker update checks since Chrome 68 ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/fresher-sw)). Netlify's free tier spends 15 credits per production deploy out of 300 a month and pauses the site when they run out ([Netlify](https://www.netlify.com/pricing/)). Vercel Hobby is "restricted to non-commercial personal use only" ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines)).

### Claude stays optional: packs flow in, summaries flow out

He won't always have a Claude plan, so Claude can never be a runtime dependency. The way to meet that is to make Claude an **author** of data, never a service the app calls. Generators, progression and economy rules run locally, and once a content pack is downloaded it lives in Dexie for good. When a plan is active, Claude Code writes new lesson packs, handcrafted puzzle packs, workout programs, or a revised rules pack (for example, a different postpone budget) into the `content/` folder. CI validates each pack with the same schema the app uses, then deploys the packs to `/packs/` on the app's own origin. In the other direction, the app exports a weekly summary JSON that he attaches to a Claude chat for a review. The research flagged one optional upgrade: the app could write that summary to a private repo. That needs a GitHub fine-grained token scoped to that one repo, kept only on the device with a "forget token" button, or held as a secret behind a tiny Worker. Fine-grained token details were not verified.

```
 CLAUDE (optional, only when he has a plan)              PHONE (always complete on its own)
 ──────────────────────────────────────────              ───────────────────────────────────
 Claude Code writes content/*.json ──▶ git push ──▶ CI: validate-packs ──▶ deploy /packs/
   (lessons · puzzle packs · programs · rules)                              │
                                                                            ▼
                                            fetch /packs/index.json (no token, same origin)
                                            → sha256 check → schema check → minAppVersion
                                            → store in Dexie (keep old version until new passes)
                                                                            │
 Claude chat ◀── weekly-summary.json  (Share / Download) ◀──────── export ──┘
```

Packs should not be served from `raw.githubusercontent.com`, because GitHub tightened its rate limits on unauthenticated raw downloads in May 2025, citing scraping, without publishing the numbers ([GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-05-08-updated-rate-limits-for-unauthenticated-requests/)). jsDelivr caches tagged versions "effectively forever" but caches branches for 12 hours ([jsDelivr](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jsdelivr/jsdelivr/master/README.md)), so a catalog hosted there would discover new content slowly. Serving from the app's own origin avoids CORS and keeps control of headers. An `index.json` catalog lists each pack's `id`, `kind`, `version`, integer major `schema`, `minAppVersion`, file URL, `sha256` and size. Pack files are immutable and versioned by filename, and only the catalog is `no-cache`. User progress is keyed by pack ID plus stable item IDs that are never reused, so updating a pack never wipes progress. Packs are untrusted input: they are never `eval`'d, and lesson text renders as plain text or sanitised markdown.

Syncing between devices is optional and can wait. For one user on one or two devices, CRDTs are overkill. Per-record last-write-wins, with tombstones and a server-issued checkpoint so device clocks don't matter, needs about 150 lines of Worker code on Cloudflare D1. D1's free tier allows 10 databases of 500 MB each and never pauses ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits/)). Dexie Cloud's free tier allows **3 production users and 100 MB** and plugs straight into the same schema ([Dexie](https://dexie.org/pricing)). PocketBase is "NOT recommended for production critical applications yet" ([PocketBase](https://pocketbase.io/docs/)). The zero-infrastructure first step is "sync by file": export on device A, then import on device B with a merge by `updatedAt`, reusing the backup code. One security rule matters for a public repo: nothing secret goes into `VITE_*` variables, which Vite inlines into the bundle. Keystores and `.env` files belong in `.gitignore`, because a leaked signing key would let anyone sign "updates". The backup file is plaintext personal data, and the UI should say so.

## One ledger pays all three modes, tilted toward real life

### Rewards help when they inform and hurt when they pay for fun

The reward evidence sets the shape of the economy before any number does. Across 128 studies, expected tangible rewards reduced free-choice intrinsic motivation (**d = −0.36**), and completion-contingent rewards did the most damage (**d = −0.44**). Performance-contingent rewards where people got less than the maximum were worst of all (**d = −0.88**). Unexpected rewards had **no effect (d = 0.01)**, and positive informational feedback *raised* intrinsic motivation in college students (**d = +0.43**) ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2001_DeciKoestnerRyan.pdf)). Puzzles are already fun, so paying for them risks spoiling them. Tasks and workouts are often not fun, so they can carry most of the XP. Gamification works, modestly. One meta-analysis found **g = .49** on cognitive outcomes, **.36** on motivation and **.25** on behaviour. Game fiction (narrative) and competitive-plus-collaborative play were the moderators that improved behaviour. The motivational and behavioural effects lost significance in the most rigorous studies ([Sailer & Homner](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)). The effect also moves over time. In a 14-week study of 756 students it "started to decrease after four weeks" and recovered between weeks six and ten ([Rodrigues et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41239-021-00314-6)). For physical activity, it shrank from **g = 0.42** during interventions to **0.15** about 14 weeks after they ended ([Mazeas et al.](https://www.jmir.org/2022/1/e26779)). The economy should therefore express XP as feedback ("3 of 3 planned tasks, your best Tuesday this month"), keep surprises deterministic but unannounced, and plan a fresh content arc for weeks four to five.

The second constraint is ethical, and it is specific to this user. The app replaces a feed, so it must not become one. In Lukoff and colleagues' study of YouTube users, recommendations, ads and autoplay *lowered* people's sense of agency. Playlists, search and watch stats, which let people plan ahead and see stopping points, raised it ([Lukoff et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.11778)). Zach Gage caps one of his games at five hands a day because "I never want people to be playing one of my games and go, 'What am I doing?'" ([Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/post/2024/08/interview-game-developer-zach-gage-on-pile-up-poker-and-resisting-dark-patterns/)). Wordle's one puzzle a day kept play to about three minutes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle)). The economy therefore has **no variable-ratio rewards of any kind**: no loot boxes, spin wheels or random chests. The variable-reward loop is exactly the mechanism he is trying to escape.

### The numbers: one XP table, one curve, one ladder

Every reward is an event written to the ledger. Five views are derived from it: level, season rank, Shards, streak state and a six-stat hexagon. Because every view is recomputed from the same events, tuning a constant in `rules.config.ts` and replaying the ledger can re-score history consistently.

```
 task done ──┐                                 ┌─▶ LEVEL    total XP; step L→L+1 costs 100·L       identity (months)
 set logged ─┤      award rules                ├─▶ SEASON   SP = XP capped 300/day; rank #300→#1   near goal (4–5 weeks)
 puzzle ─────┼─▶ (sizes, caps, night = 0) ─▶ LEDGER ─▶ SHARDS   1 per 10 XP + level/season bonuses     cosmetics, freezes
 lesson ─────┤                 (append-only)   ├─▶ STREAK   daily mark + weekly Clean Week          consistency
 lights-out ─┘                                 └─▶ HEX      28-day rolling stat per mode            self-image
```

The XP weights below put a typical good day at about **245 XP** and a maximum day at about **425**, with real-world tasks and workouts supplying roughly 70%. Most rows come from the gamification research team's spec. The Train rows deliberately depart from it. That spec paid 1 XP per workout minute, but per-minute XP rewards longer sessions. The resistance-training evidence (next sections) says longer sessions don't help a beginner. So Train pays for completing the *planned* session instead, as the workout research recommended, rescaled to fit this economy. Every number in the table is a starting value, chosen by judgement, to be retuned from his first weeks of data.

| Mode | Event | XP | Cap or anti-gaming rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tasks | Small (≤15 min) / Medium (15–45) / Large (45–90) | 10 / 20 / 35 | Size locked at planning time; **×1.5 if done on its first scheduled day**; tasks beyond the 8th of the day earn ×0.5 |
| Tasks | Quick win (created and finished within 30 min) | 5 | Max 5 a day, which stops create-and-tick farming |
| Tasks | "Clutch" surprise: finish a task postponed twice or more | +10 | Never announced in advance; max 1 a day |
| Tasks | Daily cap | 200 | Splitting a Large into three Smalls pays 30, not 35, so fragmenting doesn't pay |
| Train | Planned session completed | 60 at full plan; partial = max(40, 60 × share of planned working sets done) once every exercise has a set | One session a day counts; unplanned extra sets earn 0; doing more than the minimum always pays at least as much |
| Train | Minimum session (1 set each) or safety stop (pain flag) | 40 | A safety stop is never punished |
| Train | Rest-day recovery check (≥10-min walk or mobility) | 10 | Fills the "Body" slot of the Hat-trick |
| Train | Load PR, rep PR or ladder unlock | +15 each | Max 2 a day; warm-up and pain-flagged sets excluded; plausibility bounds |
| Train | Weekly target met: 3 of 3, or 2 of 3 | +60 / +30 | Once a week; 2 of 3 still meets the guideline floor |

The level curve is quadratic. The step from level L to L+1 costs **100·L XP**, so reaching level L takes **50·L·(L−1)** in total. That gives fast early levels and a steady slowdown with no wall. An exponential curve with a similar total at level 50 would demand about 9,700 XP for the final level alone, around 39 days. A linear curve levels up every two days forever and drains levels of meaning. Practitioner guidance says to model levelling time explicitly: invert the curve, then divide by expected XP per day ([Aversa](https://www.davideaversa.it/blog/gamedesign-math-rpg-level-based-progression/)). At 245 XP a day:

| Level | Total XP | Reached around | Days per level at that point |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 100 | first session | under 1 |
| 5 | 1,000 | day 4 | ~1.6 |
| 10 | 4,500 | day 18 | ~4 |
| 20 | 19,000 | day 78 | ~8 |
| 30 | 43,500 | day 178 | ~12 |
| 50 (soft cap, then cosmetic Prestige stars) | 122,500 | day 500 | ~20 |

From about level 15, levels arrive weekly at best, so the season ladder takes over as the short-term goal. A season runs from the first Monday of a month to the day before the next month's first Monday, which makes it 4 or 5 weeks long. That anchors each season on a temporal landmark. Gym visits rose **33.4% at the start of a week** and **47.1% at the start of a semester**, and goal commitments on a commitment-contract site rose **62.9% on Mondays** ([Dai, Milkman & Riis](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf)). Season Points equal XP, **capped at 300 a day**, so the top seat rewards consistency rather than binges. Rank is computed as follows:

```
target = 250 × days_in_season            (7,000 for 28 days; 8,750 for 35)
rank   = 300 − floor( 299 × min(1, SP / target) ^ 0.7 )
seed   = 10% of last season's SP (max 10% of target) carried in on day 1
tiers  : #300–201 Bench · #200–101 Challenger · #100–51 Contender · #50–11 Elite · #10–2 Ace · #1 Apex
```

The concave exponent gives the fast early climb that goal-gradient research predicts will pull people forward. In a 28-day season, day one's 250 SP lands him at **#271**. Half the target puts him at #116, three-quarters at #56, 90% at #23, and the full target at #1. A full previous season's seed starts the next one at about #241 rather than zero. That is endowed progress: in the classic car-wash study, **34%** of customers completed a card with two of ten stamps pre-filled, against **19%** for a card of eight empty slots needing the same eight washes ([Silicon Canals summary](https://siliconcanals.com/t-car-wash-loyalty-cards-endowed-progress/)). The ladder never shows a defeat state, because the less-than-maximum pattern carries the largest undermining effect (d = −0.88). Rank changes read as personal progress ("+14 places this week"). The other 299 seats are original ghost rivals. One named rival sits "just above" him, with the SP gap shown. From Season 2 onward, the target can be re-fitted so that matching his own previous median pace lands around #50, making the ladder a contest with his past self. Competition produced the most durable gains in the STEP UP trial: **+920 steps a day** during the intervention and **+569 three months after** ([Penn CHIBE](https://chti.upenn.edu/step-up)). Pure competition did worse than competition plus collaboration in the meta-analysis, though, so a co-op "duo goal" with a friend is worth adding once there is a way to share codes.

### Streaks bend so they never snap

Streaks are powerful and fragile. Duolingo learners who reach a 7-day streak are **3.6 times** more likely to finish their course, which is correlational ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)). Its retention PM described loss aversion kicking in around day seven. Two streak freezes at the start of a streak beat one, and **three added nothing**. Earning a lost streak back beat paying to repair it. Lowering the daily bar from one lesson to one exercise gained **zero** daily users ([Lenny's Podcast transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/jackson-shuttleworth/transcript.md)). When streaks break, people tend to drop the activity rather than restart it, a "double failure" of the behaviour and the streak. Self-blame makes the drop worse, and a repair option softens it ([University of Colorado](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions); [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2024-03-streaks.html)).

The rules follow from that evidence. A day counts with **one meaningful action**: a planned task done, a workout or rest-day recovery check, or the mystery, a lesson, or at least 10 due cards. Opening the app doesn't count. He starts each streak with **2 freezes**, never holds more than 2, gets 1 free freeze at each 7-day milestone, and can buy extras for 50 Shards. One pre-chosen rest day a week never uses a freeze. Within **48 hours** of a break, two Hat-trick days restore the streak. A gold "perfect" streak appears only after day seven, and only for weeks with no freezes used. The daily mark is the secondary layer. The headline is the **Clean Week: at least 4 Hat-trick days from Monday to Sunday**, tracked as its own weekly streak. Here the draft design had a hidden flaw that the rest-day rule fixes. With three workouts a week, a Hat-trick that required a workout would cap him at three Hat-trick days, making a Clean Week impossible. Counting the 10-minute rest-day recovery check as the Body slot keeps four or more within reach, and it nudges him toward the aerobic minutes the WHO guideline asks for. Copy on a break stays neutral and puts the cause outside him ("Streak paused. Life happens. Two Hat-trick days in 48 h restores it"). The symbol should not be a flame. A secondary, AI-generated summary of Duolingo's research says the flame didn't resonate with Indian users ([podcast summary](https://www.getrecall.ai/summary/lennys-podcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-or-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team)), so something from the theme, such as a jersey stripe or stamina pip, is safer. Duolingo's 10 p.m. streak-saver must not be copied. For someone whose problem is night-time phone use, the single streak reminder must arrive at least 90 minutes before his chosen bedtime, and never after it.

### Shards buy looks and puzzles, never rank

The soft currency closes the loop without inflation. He earns **1 Shard per 10 XP**, +20 per level-up, and a season-end award by tier (Bench 50, Challenger 100, Contender 150, Elite 200, Ace 250, Apex 300). That comes to roughly **800–1,100 Shards a season**. Prices are fixed: a streak freeze costs 50, a card back or banner 100–150, a handcrafted puzzle pack 150–250, a sound pack 300, and a full UI theme 400–800. A season of steady play buys about one theme plus one pack. Supply is bounded by the XP caps. Every purchase is a one-time cosmetic or content unlock. There is no Shard-for-XP conversion, no buying rank, and no randomised purchase, and the catalogue rotates each season. These rules are standard game-economy practice rather than findings from a study.

Notifications are part of the economy's budget too. Engagement pushes are capped at **two a day**, both opt-in: a planning reminder and the single early streak reminder. There are no social, store or "new content" pushes. Finch, a deliberately gentle app, still burned out a reviewer at about six weeks through notification volume ([Slate](https://slate.com/technology/2026/09/finch-app-self-care-wellness-review.html)). Reminders for task blocks and rest timers are different. He schedules them himself, so they sit outside the cap, and the three-Must limit bounds them naturally.

"Juice", the animation and sound feedback, also has an evidence-based budget. In a study of **3,018 players**, both no juice and extreme juice significantly worsened experience, performance and play time compared with medium or high juice ([Kao 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1875952118300879)). Three tiers follow, with timings set by judgement. *Micro* feedback (every check-off or cell placement) is a 100–200 ms tick with a light haptic. *Meso* feedback (task done, puzzle solved, workout logged) runs 400–800 ms: the XP counts up and a bar fills with a slight overshoot. *Macro* moments (level-up, tier promotion, season end) are a 1.2–2.5 s full-screen "stat reveal". They are always skippable, at most one or two a day, and attached to logging real-world actions rather than to browsing. Motion must be switchable off: WCAG 2.3.3 asks that interaction-triggered animation can be disabled, using `prefers-reduced-motion` plus an in-app toggle ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). In reduced mode, fades replace slides, shakes and speed-line bursts.

### The Proving Ground: an original ladder built from genre, not franchise

The theme can feel like a sports anime without touching anyone's property, because copyright protects expression, not ideas. WIPO states that copyright extends "only to expressions, and not to ideas, procedures, methods of operation or mathematical concepts" ([WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/protection)). India's Supreme Court held in *R.G. Anand v. Delux Films* that "ideas, themes, plots" are unprotected, and a key test is whether viewers clearly see copying ([Khurana & Khurana](https://www.khuranaandkhurana.com/2024/04/25/idea-expression-dichotomy-in-copyright-judicial-rulings-and-merger-doctrine)). "Style alone is not usually considered the subject matter of copyright" ([Creative Commons](https://creativecommons.org/2023/03/23/the-complex-world-of-style-copyright-and-generative-ai/)). The limits are just as clear. A recognisable franchise character "almost always qualifies as a derivative work", and redrawing it in a new style doesn't avoid infringement ([Odin Law](https://odinlaw.com/blog-fanart-copyright-trademark-risks/)). Japanese law protects specific character illustrations and allows characters to be registered as trademarks ([Monolith Law](https://monolith.law/en/internet/character-copyright-law)). India specifically "has no clear judicial precedent protecting fan art, and Section 52 (fair dealing) does not squarely exempt it" ([Selvams](https://selvams.com/blog/character-merchandising-in-india-licenses-fan-art-part-two/)). Existing level-up fitness apps already work this way, such as ARISE SOLO, which calls itself "inspired by classic RPGs and fantasy webtoons" and uses no franchise characters ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=arisesolo.com&hl=en_US)).

The original fiction places him as candidate **#300 in "The Proving Ground"**, a 300-seat selection programme. Each season is an "Arc", and an original coach persona narrates with its own name and look. A hexagon chart shows six stats, each computed from 28 days of ledger history: **Drive** (tasks), **Body** (workouts), **Mind** (puzzles), **Recall** (flashcards), **Wisdom** (lessons) and **Discipline** (bedtime and consistency). Game fiction is the one gamification moderator that significantly improved behaviour in the meta-analysis ([Sailer & Homner](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)), so this layer does real work. The visual language draws only on genre conventions, and the list of things to avoid is just as specific:

| Use freely (genre convention, idea or style) | Avoid (franchise expression or trademark) |
|---|---|
| Near-black or deep-navy field, one electric accent (acid-lime or cyan), one red-orange accent for rank-ups | The words "Blue Lock", "Ego" and "Egoist"; any team or character names |
| Diagonal slashes at 12–20° as the layout motif | The series logo or its letterforms |
| Halftone screentones and radial concentration lines for big moments | Silhouettes, hairstyles or jerseys that echo a character |
| Heavy condensed italic all-caps display type | Screenshots or traced panels |
| Giant rank numerals (#300 → #1), jersey-style level badges | The series' signature colour pairing used to evoke it |
| "Matchday" scoreboard cards and a hexagon stat-reveal | The in-show ranking layouts; AI imagery prompted to resemble the series |

The two research teams disagreed on "ego". One called the concept generic; the other listed the word as risky. Since it is the series' signature term and avoiding it costs nothing, it should stay out. Fonts should be SIL OFL families, which can be embedded in apps and need no attribution ([OFL FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/)). Candidates include Anton, Bebas Neue, Oswald, Barlow Condensed and Big Shoulders Display, but each one's licence file must be checked. A private app on one phone carries little practical risk. The original theme is what keeps a later Play Store release open.

## Postponing costs a decision, never points

### Specific plans beat willpower, and punishment breeds quitting

The best-evidenced anti-procrastination tool is also the cheapest to build: forcing a specific plan. A meta-analysis of 94 studies found that if-then implementation intentions improved goal attainment by **d = .65**, with **d = .61** on failing to get started ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement:-A-Gollwitzer-Sheeran/c4deb3507fe725ce6363c1735f1ba83bab20d665); [Gollwitzer chapter](https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/sites/default/files/2020-06/goal_intent_attain.pdf)). Precision matters. In a field study, flu vaccination was **33.1%** with a reminder alone, **34.6%** when people wrote a date, and **37.3%** when they wrote a date *and time* ([CHIBE](https://chibe.upenn.edu/news/converting-intention-into-action-prompting-people-to-write-down-the-date-and-time-they-planned-to-get-a-flu-vaccine-increased-vaccination-rates/)). A specific when-where-how plan also removed the intrusive pull of unfinished goals; merely considering several approaches did not ([Masicampo & Baumeister](https://users.wfu.edu/masicaej/MasicampoBaumeister2011JPSP.pdf)). Concrete framing nearly halved delay: people returned a survey in about **12.5 days** after concrete priming versus **20.5** after abstract priming ([PsyBlog](https://www.spring.org.uk/2009/01/how-to-avoid-procrastination-think.php)). Evenly spaced external deadlines beat self-set ones, and self-set ones beat a single final deadline ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/self-imposed-deadlines-dont-stop-procrastination-heres-what-might.html)). Estimates also need correcting. Honours theses predicted at **33.9 days took 55.5**. Only a manipulation that linked past completion times to the new estimate erased the bias (predicted 7.0 days, actual 7.0) and lifted on-time completion to **60% from 29%** ([Buehler et al.](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/biases/67_J_Personality_and_Social_Psychology_366,_1994.pdf)).

"Not too strict" has equally firm evidence behind it. In a two-week field study of Habitica, the task app that punishes missed dailies, **all 45 participants** experienced counterproductive effects. They were punished during genuinely productive periods and relabelled tasks to dodge damage ([Diefenbach & Müssig](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1071581918305135)). Students who forgave themselves for procrastinating before a first exam procrastinated *less* before the second, because they felt less guilt ([Wohl et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191886910000474)). In a 525-person experiment, giving people a choice improved how they rated an intervention (b = .12, p = .003), while controlling versus autonomy-supportive wording made no difference ([JMIR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6914245/)). Money-stake commitment devices fail this user. Only about **11%** of people offered a smoking-cessation contract signed it, and about **66% of signers forfeited** their deposit ([J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/cares-commitment-savings-smoking-cessation-philippines)). Pomodoro-style structured breaks lowered fatigue but did not change task completion in a small study ([Wikipedia summary of Biwer 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomodoro_Technique)). A scoping review found only **3 RCTs (n = 87)** in the whole literature ([BMC Med Educ](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12909-025-08001-0)). Timers are therefore a comfort feature, not a productivity claim. The synthesis is that the *friction* stays firm and the *emotion* stays soft.

### No mainstream app rations the postpone button

Competitors leave the gap wide open. Todoist offers a bulk "Reschedule" button for overdue tasks ([Todoist](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/introduction-to-dates-and-time-q7VobO)). TickTick lets "all overdue tasks… be postponed to today with one click" ([TickTick](https://x.com/ticktick/status/1432727380070699016)). That removes exactly the decision the research says matters. Todoist's machine-learning Smart Schedule, built for people who "keep snoozing and deferring", was withdrawn because it "was not accurate enough to be helpful" ([Todoist](https://x.com/todoist/status/1260030543556603904?lang=en)). Sunsama rolls unfinished tasks over at midnight and auto-archives them after a default of **four consecutive rollovers** ([Sunsama](https://help.sunsama.com/docs/usage-guides/archive/)). It costs **$22 a month with no free plan**, and reviewers call its mobile app "near unusable" for editing ([usecarly](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/sunsama-review/)). Its users have asked to move the end of the day past midnight ([Sunsama roadmap](https://roadmap.sunsama.com/improvements/p/option-to-change-rollover-time-end-of-day-time-to-after-midnight)), which matters for a 1–2 a.m. sleeper. Llama Life's per-task countdown keeps counting as **"Overtime"** so users see "how long things actually take versus how long you had planned", and it shows list totals and an estimated finishing time ([Ness Labs](https://nesslabs.com/llama-life-featured-tool)). A Todoist reviewer asked for what Android apps rarely deliver: "Once daily in a crowded notification panel is not a reminder. I want full screen 'do this now'" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.todoist&hl=en_IN)). With Android at **93.89%** of India's mobile market ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/india)), the bar for him is free, offline, Android-first and reminders that actually fire. No mainstream app combines a visible postpone counter, a weekly postpone budget and a forced shrink-or-split step. Prime Pocket's planner can be the first.

### The postpone ladder, step by step

```
 Task scheduled for a time slot, not done
   │  4:00 a.m. day boundary → "Unplaced" tray, triaged in the next plan or shutdown
   │  (no silent rollover · no "postpone all" button)
   ▼
 POSTPONE #1 ─ must pick a new DATE + TIME (3 suggested free slots or "pick")   uses 1 of 5 weekly
 POSTPONE #2 ─ same, plus SHRINK IT: "What's a first step that takes ≤10 min?"  uses 1
 POSTPONE #3 ─ choice sheet, never a block:
                 [ Split into 2–3 ]   [ ▶ Do 10 min now ]   [ Move to Someday ]
                 small link: "Postpone anyway"  → needs a reason                uses 1
 POSTPONE #4 ─ moves to Someday automatically; weekly review lists it
 UNTOUCHED 14 DAYS ─ moves to Someday automatically

 Weekly budget: 5 free postpones, reset Monday. From the 6th: one-tap reason
   (underestimated · blocked · low energy · avoided it) + the Shrink step. No XP lost.
 Real-reason exception (class moved, illness, travel): 2 a week, uses no budget.
 The only economic cost of postponing: losing the ×1.5 "on first scheduled day" bonus.
```

Each rung maps to a finding. The date-and-time requirement applies the planning-prompt evidence directly. The shrink step applies the concreteness and first-step findings. The three-way choice gives him choice, the one autonomy lever with experimental support. The automatic Someday move borrows Sunsama's four-rollover default, which is an industry convention, not evidence. It prevents the backlog wall that one developer described hitting at **247 tasks** before quitting app after app ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/vithanco/i-built-a-task-manager-that-automatically-kills-tasks-youre-ignoring-49dk)). The 14-day untouched rule is shorter than that developer's 30-day "graveyard" because a student's tasks are more time-bound. The specific numbers are judgement. No study gives an optimal postpone budget, an optimal trigger count, or a maximum task size. The budget should be set from the baseline week: if he averages 15 postpones a week, start the budget at about 10 and step it down, rather than failing him on day one. One safeguard makes "not too strict" automatic. **If he exceeds the budget in more than half of recent weeks, the app raises the budget by itself.**

The rest of the planner's numbers work the same way, with evidence for the structure and judgement for the values:

| Mechanic | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Max task size before a forced split | 90 min estimated; subtasks 25–60 min | Judgement; larger tasks overrun more (about 1.6–1.8× in Buehler) |
| First-step line | Optional at creation, required at the 2nd postpone | Evidence (concreteness, first steps) |
| Multi-day work (assignment, exam prep) | App proposes 3–4 evenly spaced checkpoints | Evidence by analogy (spaced deadlines) |
| "Must" tasks per day | Max 3, plus optional "Could" tasks | Judgement (practitioner norm) |
| Capacity warning | Estimates × his overrun ratio > free hours (default 4 h on class days, 6 h on weekends) | Concept from Sunsama's workload warning; numbers by judgement |
| Focus block | 25/5, long break 15–20 after four; +10 extend; 50/10 preset | Weak–moderate evidence on fatigue; 25/5 is convention |
| Live timers | One at a time; starting another pauses the first and counts a "switch" | Judgement from switching-cost research |
| Estimate calibration | At completion: "Planned 30m · Took 48m (1.6×)"; after ≥5 timed tasks in a category, suggest an adjusted estimate | Evidence (Buehler recall-relevant fix); ≥5 by judgement |
| Temptation bundle | Optional: he names a reward (reels, music) that the app suggests only after a completed block | Evidence: +51% gym visits at first, fading ([Milkman et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v60y2014i2p283-299.html)); +10–14% at scale for up to 17 weeks ([Kirgios et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S074959782030385X)) |
| Streak in Tasks | None; shows "Planned 5 of the last 7 days" and focus minutes this week | Evidence by analogy (broken streaks cause drop-off) |
| Money stakes | None | Evidence (11% uptake, 66% forfeiture) |

The core screen is a **Now card**: one task, a shrinking ring countdown, its first-step line, and an overtime counter in a different colour once time runs out. Beneath it sit a **Next strip** with the next one or two blocks and their start times, and a day-horizon line such as "3h 40m planned · 2h 50m left before 11 p.m." These patterns come from Llama Life, Structured and Tiimo, and reviews are their only evidence. The research found no controlled trials of visual timers in adults.

### Rituals sized for a 1 a.m. sleeper

Rituals survive only when they are short. Cal Newport's daily shutdown takes about **five minutes** and ends with a spoken closure line ([Cal Newport](https://calnewport.com/drastically-reduce-stress-with-a-work-shutdown-ritual/)). David Allen's GTD weekly review takes one to two hours at first, which is why practitioners push a 20-minute version ([loggd.life](https://loggd.life/blog/weekly-review-gtd)). For him the limits are a morning plan of at most three minutes, a shutdown of at most three minutes, and a weekly review of at most ten. All are skippable without penalty, and a skipped ritual shrinks to a one-tap minimum version. The shutdown matters most, and its timing is the point. It should fire at a time he chooses **before his scrolling window**, by default about 11:15 p.m., right after his nightly 11 p.m. learning session. It closes today, sets tomorrow's first task with a specific time and place, and offers one if-then for the night cue, such as "If I'm in bed with the phone after 12:30, I put it on the charger across the room." Mental contrasting with implementation intentions moved bedtimes about **33 minutes earlier versus 14 for controls** over three weeks. In other research, heavy bedtime procrastinators used their phones **79.5 minutes** in the three hours before bed, against **17.6** for light procrastinators ([Wikipedia: Bedtime procrastination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedtime_procrastination)). The day boundary sits at **4:00 a.m.**, so 12:30 a.m. work still counts as today. After a missed day, the app opens on a two-tap fresh-start screen ("Yesterday didn't go to plan. That's data, not a verdict"), not a red overdue pile. It never sends failure-framed pushes ("You broke…").

Two Android features need verification before they are promised. Full-screen "do this now" alarms use Android's full-screen intent, whose restrictions on recent Android versions were outside the research scope. A focus-block guard that notices Instagram opening would need Usage Access permission through a custom native plugin, which was not researched. That guard should be opt-in in any case, since evidence on choice favours opt-in friction over a hard block.

## Training pays for the finished plan, not the extra set

### Twice a week, every muscle group, short of failure

The new ACSM resistance-training position stand (April 2026), the first major update in 17 years, distils to "train all major muscle groups 2x per week and build gradually over time". "Moving from no resistance training to any resistance training represents the greatest single gain", and "training to failure, choice of equipment and complex periodisation did not consistently impact outcomes for the average healthy adult" ([Physio-pedia summary](https://members.physio-pedia.com/members-news/new-acsm-resistance-training-guidelines-what-physiotherapists-need-to-know/); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/03/260319074552.htm)). Elastic bands, bodyweight exercises and home routines all produced meaningful gains. The chair, Stuart Phillips, put it plainly: "The best resistance training program is the one you'll actually stick with." WHO's 2020 guideline asks adults for muscle-strengthening work on **2 or more days a week** plus 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity, and says inactive people should start small and build gradually ([WHO via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7719906/)). A meta-analysis of 15 studies found no significant difference between training to failure and stopping short, for either strength or size. When volume was not equated, stopping short gave *better* strength gains ([Grgic et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2095254621000077)).

These findings set three defaults. Full-body sessions three times a week clear the twice-a-week floor with one session to spare, so a missed day still meets the guideline, and a **2-day "minimum mode" is a first-class option**, not a failure state. Every set targets "solid" effort, meaning one to three reps left in the tank. **Failure sets earn no extra XP.** The game must never pay for soreness or for "going harder".

### A progression engine that fits in fifty lines

Progression is pure rule logic, so it runs offline with no AI. For gym lifts, the engine uses double progression with a confirmation step modelled on the "2-for-2" rule that the US military's Human Performance Resource Center credits to NSCA and ACSM. The same source gives NSCA's increments for less-trained people: **+2.5–5 lb upper body and +5–10 lb lower body**, roughly 1–2.5 kg and 2.5–5 kg ([HPRC](https://www.hprc-online.org/physical-fitness/training-performance/guidelines-progress-your-physical-training-over-time)). For bodyweight work, the r/bodyweightfitness Recommended Routine supplies a rule that translates straight into code: "Start every workout trying to do 3 sets of 8 reps. Hit 3×8 with good form → move to harder exercise (start at 3×5). Can't hit 3×5 → move to easier exercise." It also supplies the ladders themselves: wall, incline, full, diamond, then pseudo-planche push-ups; assisted squat through shrimp squats; Romanian deadlift through Nordic curls; vertical through archer rows ([RR gist](https://gist.github.com/sgup/f10f1d57e54b7876495f4bafb6d697eb)).

```
 GYM LIFT (rep range e.g. 8–12), after each session:
   every working set ≥ repMax  AND  no pain flag  AND  effort ≠ near-max ?
       → successStreak += 1
       → successStreak == 2 : load += smallest increment the user's equipment allows
                              (upper ≈ 1–2.5 kg, lower ≈ 2.5–5 kg); reps reset to repMin; streak = 0
                              (max 1 increase per exercise per week during weeks 1–6)
   any working set < repMin ?
       → failStreak += 1 ;  2 = hold ;  3 = load −10% and reset   (deload heuristic, unverified)

 BODYWEIGHT LADDER (3 × 5–8):
   3 × 8 clean (optionally in 2 sessions) → next step, start at 3 × 5
   any set < 5                            → previous step
   timed holds: 3 × 20–60 s, advance at 3 × 60 s
```

Two defaults suit a hostel and a gym equally. **"Gym Foundations"** is a full-body A/B plan, 3 times a week, about 45 minutes. Day A is goblet squat or leg press, dumbbell or machine chest press, cable or one-arm dumbbell row, dumbbell Romanian deadlift and plank. Day B is lat pulldown, split squat, seated dumbbell overhead press, hip thrust or back extension, and Pallof press or side plank. Main lifts are 3×8–12 and hinges 2×8–12. The warm-up is five minutes of easy cardio plus auto-generated ramp sets at about 50% and 75% of working load. **"Hostel Bodyweight"** takes about 35–45 minutes. It pairs squat and push-up ladders, then hinge and row ladders, with 90 seconds' rest within pairs, and finishes with a core triplet on 60-second rests. Rows appear only if he confirms a sturdy anchor, such as a table edge for inverted rows. Otherwise the app substitutes Y-T-W raises or a backpack row and flags "limited pulling"; a resistance band or doorway bar would widen the options. Each ladder starts with a one-time placement test: maximum clean reps, then he begins at the step where 3×5 is achievable. That test doubles as the theme's "rank assessment" on his first day. The plate calculator must handle Indian gym realities: 20 kg or 15 kg bars, fixed dumbbells in 2.5 kg steps, and sometimes no small plates. The "smallest increment" comes from his equipment settings, never from a constant.

### Guardrails for a hostel in Ahmedabad heat

Safety rules can lean on published guidance without making medical claims. A one-tap pain button ends that exercise, records it, suggests the easier ladder step or a rest day, and shows "if pain persists, consult a qualified professional". A safety stop earns the same 40 XP as a minimum session, so stopping is never the costly choice. No load increase ever follows a pain-flagged session. A planned week may add no more than 10–20% hard sets over the previous one. That cap is a heuristic adapted from HPRC's cardio rule, which carries no specific citation. Heat needs its own mode in Ahmedabad. ACSM says acclimatising takes **10–14 days** of repeated exercise in the heat. It advises training during cooler times of day such as early morning or late evening, and moving indoors when it is above about **27°C with humidity above 75%**. It also lists warning signs such as dizziness, confusion and pale skin ([ACSM](https://acsm.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Exercising-in-hot-and-cold-environments.pdf)). An offline "hot day" toggle cuts volume by 20–30% for the first 10–14 days of a routine or a hot spell, suggests late-evening or indoor sessions (which suit a night owl), and shows ACSM's warning-sign checklist. Tapping "dizzy/confused" shows stop-and-seek-help text. The toggle can link to the city's own Heat Action Plan, the first in South Asia ([Exemplars in Global Health](https://www.exemplars.health/stories/ahmedabad-indias-heat-action-plan)). An optional two-tap readiness check (slept OK? sore?) steers poor days to the minimum session rather than a skip. The first-run screen and settings carry "General fitness information, not medical advice."

### Log in one tap, and give away what rivals paywall

The market leaders converge on one logging pattern. Hevy shows previous performance on every set and supports warm-up, drop and failure set types, rest timers, a plate calculator and estimated one-rep maxes. Its free tier caps users at **4 routines, 7 custom exercises and 3 months of history**, and Pro sells the warm-up calculator and per-muscle set counts ([Hevy](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/33106320824727-Everything-You-Need-to-Know-About-the-Hevy-App-2025-Features-Guide)). Strong's free tier allows three custom routines and keeps its plate and warm-up calculators in Pro, according to a competitor's snapshot ([Push/Pull](https://push-pull.app/blog/push-pull-vs-strong)). In Prime Pocket, every set row arrives pre-filled with last session's weight × reps, and one tap completes it unchanged. Completing a set starts the rest timer automatically: 90 seconds for paired compound work and 60 for core. After the last set comes an optional three-way effort tap (easy, solid, near max), which suits beginners better than a 1–10 RPE scale, though novices' accuracy at gauging reps in reserve was not verified. Live PR badges appear when a set beats a stored best. Unlimited routines, full history, plate and warm-up calculators and per-muscle weekly set counts all ship free, because they cost nothing to compute offline. Metrics appear in priority order: consistency first (sessions against target, a calendar heatmap, and consecutive weeks with at least two strength sessions), then progression (ladder step per pattern and a PR feed), then volume as hard sets per muscle. Tonnage never becomes the headline.

The exercise library should ship inside the app. **free-exercise-db is released under the Unlicense (public domain)** with 800+ exercises in structured JSON with images ([GitHub](https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db)). The licence of its upstream image source was not independently verified, which should be checked before relying on it. Bundling only the 100–150 exercises the programs use, converted to WebP, keeps the APK small. Ladder steps the library lacks, such as wall push-ups, archer rows, shrimp squats and scapular pulls, become custom entries with original text and original stick-figure illustrations, which fit the theme anyway. wger's exercise data is licensed Creative Commons per entry, so it needs per-item attribution and share-alike tracking ([GitHub](https://github.com/wger-project/wger)). ExerciseDB's GIFs need a paid licence, and the licence forbids redistributing the raw files ([ExerciseDB](https://exercisedb.io/faq)). That would bite if the repo were public.

### Game rules that survive week four

Fitness gamification has measurable but decaying effects, and the trials show which elements last. Across 16 RCTs with 2,407 participants, gamified interventions raised physical activity by **g = 0.42**, and by **0.23 against equivalent non-gamified programmes** ([Mazeas et al.](https://www.jmir.org/2022/1/e26779)). In a three-week field experiment with 80 participants, step counts decayed week by week in every arm, and gamification did not raise intrinsic motivation ([IJHCS 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581923002148)). STEP UP's durable design combined commitment, points given up front and lost for missed goals, five levels with participants starting in the middle, weekly fresh-start resets, and a social frame ([EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/901675)). The Train mode takes the parts that fit a solo, "not too strict" app. It sets weekly rather than daily targets, so rest days never break anything. Ladder unlocks serve as the core progression, which is skill-based and finite, unlike points. Monday resets and season seeds apply the fresh-start effect. Every eighth week, at the end of every second season, comes a re-test week: placement tests plus lighter volume, which builds in a periodic easy week. Three things are left out: punitive "penalty quests", XP for raw volume, and ads. Ads are the top complaint in the "level-up system" fitness apps ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=arisesolo.com&hl=en_US)).

STEP UP's loss framing and the BE FIT trial deserve a fair hearing. BE FIT gave **70 points a week that were lost when goals were missed**, plus **five forgiveness "lifelines"**, and held a 1,400–1,900-step gain over 12 weeks ([AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/using-gamification-and-social-incentives-to-increase-physical-activity)). Loss framing is part of why those designs worked, and the default here gives that up. The Habitica and broken-streak evidence argues against forcing it on someone who asked for "not too strict". The honest compromise is to offer a BE FIT-style weekly pot with lifelines as an **opt-in season experiment** once the base habits exist, and to judge it against his own previous season.

## Puzzles get a story, a weekday arc and a hard stop

### Fun comes from decisions, mystery and scarcity, not volume

The puzzle mode doesn't need more content. It needs structure that makes each three-minute board matter. LinkedIn's daily games, each under five minutes and refreshed once a day, report that **84% of players return the next day and 80% within a week**. LinkedIn ranks players only against connections and alumni, never anonymous global players ([Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/linkedin-bets-on-games-to-build-professional-connections-daily-habits/)). Those are company figures with no stated base. A 2025 meta-analysis of 37 trials found that the best-performing combination of game elements for learning was **"Rules/Goals + Challenge + Mystery"** (overall d = 0.566) ([ETR&D](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-025-10493-y)). Duolingo's Gardenscapes-style moves counter was "completely neutral" because lessons involved no strategic decision ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)), which means mechanics must attach to real choices. Gage designs each game as "a sandbox" that stays fun stretched across a year of daily play, and asks "how do I support the player who has nothing?" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/puzzmo-co-creator-zach-gage-on-building-newspaper-games-that-can-last-forever)). Honesty about benefits keeps the mode credible. Brain training improves the trained tasks, with "little evidence that training enhances performance on distantly related tasks" ([Simons et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1529100616661983)). Lumosity paid the FTC $2 million over its claims ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges-its-brain-training-program)). The in-app line should be: "You get faster at what you practise; the real win is an active three minutes instead of a passive scroll."

### The daily drop, redesigned around the 80-XP cap

The Mind cap and the time budget turn out to be the same number. A mystery (25 XP), the featured board (10), one lesson (15) and about 20 due cards (20) total 70 XP and take about 10–15 minutes. One optional side board brings the total to 80. So the **Daily Drop** consists of the logic-grid mystery, a featured board that rotates by weekday (Mini Sudoku 6×6, Crowns, Sun & Moon, Number Path, Calcudoku), the day's lesson and the due cards, with the other board types available as side boards. Everything beyond the cap is Free Play at 0 XP and 0 Shards, reachable only from a secondary menu. The drop unlocks at a morning hour he chooses, by default 7:00. After completion comes a stopping screen ("Today's training complete. Next drop: 7:00") with one primary button that leaves the app, "Back to planner". Difficulty runs from easy on Monday to a **Sunday "Boss"** board, timed to the Monday fresh start. This weekday ramp is a crossword convention, not a tested design.

Four additions make it far more fun without making it longer. First, the **mystery meta-arc**: daily logic-grid cases chain into the season's story inside the Proving Ground. Each solve reveals a clue or a panel, and the Sunday Boss closes a chapter. That is the "Mystery" in the winning element combination, and the game fiction that improved behaviour in the meta-analysis. Second, **technique cards**: every puzzle type has named techniques, such as Sudoku pairs, Crowns region confinement and Sun & Moon adjacency. The first correct use of one unlocks a collectible card with a 20-second explainer. That builds competence rather than paying for time, and an unannounced unlock carries no undermining risk. Third, **mastery tracks**: personal bests and par times per type and difficulty, "no-hint clear" stamps and a per-type mini streak, all feeding the Mind stat on the hexagon. Fourth, **spoiler-free share cards**: a themed tile showing rank badge, solve time and hint count, never the solution, as with Wordle's emoji grid, which drove its spread from 90 players to millions ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle)). Boards are seeded from the date, so a friend running the app gets the identical puzzle and can compare without a server. The default target should be WhatsApp or plain text, not Instagram Stories, and sharing should switch off during wind-down. Opening Instagram to post a result at 1 a.m. reopens the very feed the app exists to replace.

Fairness keeps all of this from collapsing. Every board comes from one generator recipe. It plants a random valid solution, derives clues from it, then strips clues one at a time. A removal is kept only if a counter that stops at two solutions still finds exactly one, *and* a human-style logic solver still finishes without guessing. Uniqueness and a no-guess path are different properties, so both checks are needed ([Tatham](https://www.chiark.greenend.org.uk/~sgtatham/puzzles/devel/writing.html)). Clue count barely predicts difficulty (r = 0.25–0.27 with solve time), while constraint-propagation metrics reach r = 0.68–0.86 ([Pelánek](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/sudoku-arxiv.pdf)). The logic solver therefore does triple duty: it is the fairness check, the difficulty grader (`maxLevel × 100 + step costs`) and the hint engine. Hints climb three rungs: a nudge ("check row 3"), then the named technique shown on a 4×4 mini example, then a single move. A new puzzle type opens with a small, intuitive board and no tutorial, and the technique is taught afterwards. Problem-solving before instruction improved conceptual understanding across 53 studies ([ETH Zurich](https://ethz.ch/en/news-and-events/eth-news/news/2021/09/produktives-scheitern.html)). Generation runs in a Web Worker, and the app checks his grid against the puzzle's rules rather than the stored solution, so a correct alternative answer is never rejected. Handcrafted puzzle packs, which Claude Code can author, are the Shard sink. They are finite and themed, and they **earn no XP beyond the daily cap**. Names should stay neutral: Crowns not Queens, Sun & Moon not Tango, Number Path not Zip, and Calcudoku, because "KenKen" is a trademark ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KenKen)).

### Lessons open with a guess and close with a plan

Lessons keep a 12-screen, 3–5-minute shape that already fits the theme's "film session" feel. It runs: a one-line scene; a guess with a confidence tap; an immediate reveal with one labelled picture; the term named only after the picture; the source line; a two-card story beat; a one-sentence principle; a transfer check in a new situation; a "why?" pick; an if-then builder; and a Done screen with no auto-advance. The guess carries much of the weight. Pretested items were recalled at **75% versus 56%**, even though only 5% of the pretest answers were right ([Richland, Kornell & Kao](https://learninglab.uchicago.edu/Pre-Testing_files/RichlandKornellKao.pdf)). The if-then plan is the real payload, at d = 0.65 ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/10973)). "Did your plan fire yesterday?" at the next session gives him a reason to return that is not a streak. Each takeaway card saves automatically into the spaced-repetition deck, which bounds the supply of flashcard XP naturally. The source tracks (Gita lessons framed as a performance-psychology dialogue, Charaka lessons graded against modern evidence, and money and bias-training tracks) arrive as content packs. Scripture cards should keep juice to the micro tier. Speed lines and badge slams belong to logging achievements, not to a verse.

### Night mode turns the app off on purpose

The single biggest design risk is that a better app still wins the 1 a.m. slot. In **45,202 Norwegian students aged 18–28**, each hour of screen use after going to bed was associated with **59% higher odds of insomnia and 24 fewer minutes of sleep**, and "the type of screen activity does not appear to matter as much as the overall time spent using screens in bed" ([Hjetland et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1548273/full)). A meta-analysis of 125,198 young people linked bedtime device use to inadequate sleep with an **odds ratio of 2.17** ([Carter et al.](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/fullarticle/2571467)). Both studies are observational. Night behaviour therefore has two stages. **Wind-down** runs from shutdown until his chosen bedtime. It offers only leftover drop items and reviews, with a **10-minute hard cap**, no new lessons, no sharing, quieter juice and no engagement pushes. **Lights-out** starts at bedtime. A calm screen offers the lights-out check-in (+15 XP if it happens before bedtime), and mind activities earn 0 XP, with no sound and no animation. He can always override, because it's his app and autonomy matters, but overrides are logged as information. The bedtime setting should start at his real 1:30 a.m. and move about 15 minutes earlier every few days, rather than demanding 11 p.m. from day one.

## Judge it on Reels minutes, and build it in five phases

### Measure the life outside the app, with app time as the alarm

Success means less scrolling and more tasks done, not more time in Prime Pocket. The measurement layer is built in from the first week, and a **one-week baseline** is logged before any XP is shown. The economy can run silently in that week to calibrate XP per day and the postpone budget. Gamified effects shrink once the game is removed, and novelty dips after about four weeks before recovering by weeks six to ten. So the system is judged at weeks 8–10, not week two, and only one variable changes per season. Lukoff's agency findings inspire a weekly two-question check. The metrics and their healthy directions:

| Metric | How it's captured | Healthy direction |
|---|---|---|
| Night-time Reels minutes | Nightly one-tap bucket (0, <15, 15–30, 30–60, 60–120, 120+); weekly Digital Wellbeing figure typed in | Down from the ~6 h/day baseline |
| Lights-out time | Self-logged check-in; share of nights before his chosen bedtime | Earlier |
| Start adherence | Focus blocks started within ±15 min of plan | Up; the primary planner metric, since starting is where procrastination happens |
| Tasks done on their first scheduled day | Percentage per week | Up |
| Postpones | Per week, share needing a 3rd postpone, mix of reasons | Down; budget auto-raises if exceeded in >50% of weeks |
| Estimate ratio | Median actual ÷ estimate per category | Toward 1.0–1.2 |
| Workouts and Clean Weeks | Sessions per week; weekly streak | At least 2 strength sessions every week |
| **Guard: time in Prime Pocket** | Automatic, excluding workout and focus timers | Flat or falling; rising app time with flat task completion means the game is competing with life |
| Agency check | "Did time in the app feel well spent?" (1–5); "Did you open it when you meant to do something else?" (Y/N) | Up / No |
| Streak recovery rate | Broken streaks repaired or restarted within 7 days | High; low means the system is too strict |

After about three months, one **no-XP week** tests whether tasks and bedtime hold without rewards. That is the durability question the physical-activity meta-analysis raises. Habits took about **66 days** on average to become automatic, with a range of 18–254, and missing a single day did not derail them ([Lally et al. via BJGP](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3505409/)). A three-month horizon is therefore the earliest point at which the test is fair.

### A day in Prime Pocket

```
 07:00   Daily Drop unlocks: mystery + featured board + lesson + due cards  (≈10–15 min, any time before wind-down)
 ~09:00  Morning plan ≤3 min: ≤3 Musts, each with a start time; capacity bar vs free hours
 day     Focus blocks 25/5 or 50/10 — one live timer; Now card + Next strip; exact alarms from the APK
 evening Train 35–45 min on Mon/Wed/Fri (screen kept awake, rest alarm backstop) · rest days: 10-min walk check
 23:00   Learning session with Claude on the laptop (optional; the app doesn't depend on it)
 23:15   Shutdown ≤3 min: close today · tomorrow's first task with time + place · bedtime if-then
 23:20→  Wind-down: leftovers + reviews only, 10-min cap, no new lessons, no sharing, no engagement pushes
 bedtime Lights-out check-in (+15) → after this: 0 XP, no sound, no animation
 04:00   Day boundary (dayKey rolls over)
```

All times are defaults he can change. Excluding workout and focus timers, the intended attention inside the app is about 20–30 minutes a day, a small fraction of the six hours it competes with.

## Conclusion

The hardest technical constraint and the hardest behavioural one turn out to point the same way. Exact reminders force a native shell, and the 1 a.m. problem forces an app that limits itself. Both say Prime Pocket should be a small, deterministic rules engine on the phone, not a service or a feed. That framing also settles the Claude question more cleanly than any sync protocol could. Once Claude is an author of validated data rather than something the app calls at runtime, the app is complete without a subscription, and better whenever one exists. The second insight comes from the caps. Because XP is capped per mode, sized at planning time and zeroed at night, XP per day becomes a measurement instrument rather than a score: it rises only when real-world output rises. That makes the ledger the natural place to detect the failure that matters most, the game competing with life.

The remaining uncertainty is large and should be stated plainly. No study gives an optimal postpone budget, split trigger, level curve or season target. No study shows that replacing short-form video with a learning app improves sleep. Several Android behaviours (alarm restoration after reboot, full-screen intents, Usage Access, Nothing OS background policy) await testing on the device, and sideloading rules may tighten globally in 2027. The design answers this by making every number a single line in one config file and every outcome a logged metric. The first two months then become an experiment he runs on himself, and the verdict comes from his lights-out times, not from the app's opinion of itself.
