## 1. Paid to like, then asked to pay (task scams)

### Evidence

| Claim | Source quote | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Task scams start with online ads for part-time work at home. | "Targeted digital advertisements are launched on platforms like Google and Meta using key words like"Ghar baithe job", "Ghar baidhe kamai kaise karen" etc" | MHA, PIB, 6 Dec 2023. https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1982936 |
| Young job seekers are a main target. | "Targets are mostly retired employees, women and unemployed youth looking for part time jobs." | Same |
| The pattern: small tasks, a small payout, then a request to put in more money. | "an agent using WhatsApp / Telegram starts conversation with the potential victim, who convinces her to perform some tasks like Video Likes and Subscribe, Maps Rating, etc." ... "victim is given some commission, initially and is asked to invest more to get more returns against given task." | Same |
| Then the money is frozen. | "when victim deposits larger sum, deposits are frozen and thus victim is duped." | Same |
| Check who receives the money. | "Verify the name of receiver mentioned in UPI App. If receiver is any random person, it may be a mule account and scheme may be fraudulent." | Same |
| Report numbers and handles to the national portal. | "Citizens are also advised to promptly report phone numbers and social media handles used by such fraudsters to the NCRP www.cybercrime.gov.in" | Same |
| 1930 can help freeze money if reported fast. | "A dedicated cybercrime helpline number 1930provides immediate assistance to victims of online financial frauds by facilitating prompt reporting and, where possible, freezing of fraudulent transactions." | PIB backgrounder "Curbing Cyber Frauds in Digital India", 7 Oct 2025. https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/oct/doc2025107659501.pdf |
| 1930 and the portal are still the channels in 2026. | "Cyber frauds and suspicious communications of this nature should be reported immediately on the National Cyber Crime Helpline number 1930 or on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal www.cybercrime.gov.in" | MHA, PIB, 7 Aug 2026. https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2295889 |

**Grade: strong.** A direct government description of the pattern. Setting: complaints to 1930 and NCRP,
national. The "youth" target is stated, not measured by age.

### Step 1 · Spot the sign

**Hook.** Ravi in Patna gets ₹150 for liking 3 videos. Next, the "HR" on Telegram offers a "premium task".
What usually comes next in this pattern?
- a) More small tasks at the same pay.
- b) **A request to deposit money to unlock bigger tasks.** (correct)
- c) A formal offer letter by email.

**Teach card (29 words).**
> In task scams, the first payout is real and small. Then you are asked to pay in to unlock bigger tasks.
> Once the deposit is large, it gets frozen.

**Show me.** Ask: "Tap the message where it turns."

| Part | Weak offer (scam pattern) | Good offer (real gig) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Like 3 videos, get ₹150." | "Translate 10 product pages, ₹40 a page." |
| 2 | "Paid! You're a top performer." | "Contract and GST invoice format attached." |
| 3 | **"Deposit ₹2,000 to unlock premium tasks."** (spot) | "Payment 15 days after you submit." |

Feedback: "In the weak column, part 3 asks you to pay in. In the good column, money only flows to you, after
the work, with a contract."

(Editor note: ₹150 and ₹2,000 are story amounts, not source figures. Keep them small and plain.)

**Guided try.** Ayesha in Hyderabad has done 5 rating tasks and got paid twice. The group says: "Pay ₹1,000 to
join the VIP round." Which reading fits the pattern?

| Option | Correct | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| a) The payouts were real, so the VIP round is safe. | No, **misconception: "If they paid me, they are genuine."** | The early payout is part of the pattern. It builds trust before the bigger ask. |
| b) Being asked to pay in is the sign. Stop here. | Yes | A real employer pays you. A request to deposit is where task scams turn. |
| c) It's fine if the group has hundreds of members. | No | Big groups can be full of fake members. Size says nothing about who gets paid. |

### Step 2 · What to do

**Hook.** Ayesha has already paid ₹1,000. The group says one more payment will "release" her money. What gives
her the best chance?
- a) Pay once more to get it all back.
- b) **Stop paying and report quickly.** (correct)
- c) Wait a week to see if it unfreezes.

**Teach card (27 words).**
> Paying more does not release frozen money in this pattern. Stop paying. Save the chat, the UPI IDs and the
> receipts. Then call 1930 the same day.

**Show me.** Ask: "Tap the first useful step."

| Part | Weak response | Good response |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pays the "release fee". | Stops paying. **"Taking screenshots of the chat and UPI receipts."** (spot) |
| 2 | Deletes the chat out of embarrassment. | Calls 1930 the same day. |
| 3 | Tells no one. | Tells one friend she trusts. |

Feedback: "Proof helps the report, and 1930 can sometimes freeze money if called fast. Being scammed is not a
personal failing. These groups are run by organised networks."

**Guided try.** Ravi notices the UPI receiver name is a random person, not a company. What does that suggest?

| Option | Correct | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| a) It may be a mule account, so the scheme may be a fraud. | Yes | MHA flags a random receiver name as a sign of a mule account. |
| b) Small firms often use staff accounts, so it's fine. | No, **misconception: "Personal accounts are normal for start-ups."** | A real firm pays and gets paid in its own name. A stranger's account is the warning. |
| c) It only matters above ₹10,000. | No | The receiver name matters for any amount. |

### Step 3 · Where to report

**Hook.** Which number is the national cyber fraud helpline in India?
- a) 100
- b) **1930** (correct)
- c) 1800

**Teach card (23 words).**
> Lost money online? Call 1930 or file at cybercrime.gov.in. Calling fast matters: where possible, the money
> can be frozen before it moves on.

**Show me.** Ask: "Tap the better report."

| Part | Weak report | Good report |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "I got scammed on Telegram." | "Telegram handle, group name, 2 UPI IDs." |
| 2 | No time given. | **"Paid ₹1,000 at 9:40 pm today, UTR number attached."** (spot) |
| 3 | Filed 3 weeks later. | Called 1930 within the hour. |

Feedback: "The good report has who, when and the transaction ID. That is what helps trace and freeze money."

**Guided try.** Ayesha only got the Telegram ad and did not pay. Where can she flag the number?

| Option | Correct | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| a) Chakshu on sancharsaathi.gov.in. | Yes | Chakshu takes reports of suspected fraud calls and messages. If money is lost, use 1930 instead. |
| b) Nowhere. Nothing happened, so it doesn't count. | No, **misconception: "Only losses can be reported."** | Suspected fraud messages can be reported, and MHA asks people to report scam numbers and handles. |
| c) Reply to the scammer to warn them off. | No | Replying confirms your number is active. Report, block, and leave the chat. |

### Field try

Save **1930** in your phone as "Cyber fraud helpline". Takes 20 seconds.
- Cues: "On the bus", "After class", my own.
- Smaller version: say "1930" out loud once and tell one friend.
- Next-day line: "Did it / Partly / Didn't come up / Not this time." Fixed sourced line: "1930 can help freeze
  money if called fast." (PIB, 7 Oct 2025)

### Later-day question

"In a task scam, what usually happens after the first small payout?" (Answer: a request to deposit money to
unlock bigger tasks.)

## 2. A real job does not charge you (fake jobs and pay-to-work)

### Evidence

| Claim | Source quote | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Never pay for a job offer. Check the posting and company first. | "Never pay for job offers. Verify job postings and companies before applying or providing personal data." | CERT-In Advisory CIAD-2024-0050, "Preventing Online scams", 24 Oct 2024. https://www.cert-in.org.in/s2cMainServlet?pageid=PUBVLNOTES02&VLCODE=CIAD-2024-0050 |
| The government job portal charges nothing, and job seekers need not pay employers. | "NCS does not charge any fee at any stage of registration, job application, or interview processing. Jobseekers should not pay fees to employers." | National Career Service FAQ, live page opened 24 Oct 2026. https://www.ncs.gov.in/ |
| NCS helpline and fraud number. | "You may contact NCS at support.ncs@gov.in or on the toll-free helpline 1514. Cyber fraud can be reported on 1930." | Same |
| Jobs abroad: unregistered agents use WhatsApp and fake offers. | "It is reported that many illegal agents operate through Facebook, Whatsapp, text message and other such mediums." | MEA advisory, 14 Dec 2023, still linked on the MEA Overseas Employment page (opened 24 Oct 2026). https://www.mea.gov.in/images/pdf/05-List-2024.pdf |
| A valid job abroad uses a work visa, not a tourist visa. | "Valid job offers must allow the worker to emigrate on strength of Employment or Work visa or other similar visa except tourist visa." | Same |
| Check the agent on eMigrate. | "Prospective emigrants are advised to cross check the genuineness of the RA by visiting the government website www.emigrate.gov.in and click the link "List of active RA"." | Same |
| Help line for jobs abroad. | "Toll Free No: 1800 11 3090 (Accessible from India only)" | Same |

**Grade: strong.** Three current official pages say the same rule. Setting: national advisories. Note: the
MEA advisory caps a registered agent's service charge (₹30,000 + GST), so for jobs abroad the rule is "no fee
to an unregistered agent", not "no money ever". Editors keep that difference.

### Step 1 · Spot the sign

**Hook.** Gurpreet in Ludhiana gets a WhatsApp offer: "Selected for work-from-home data entry." What is the
biggest warning sign to look for next?
- a) The salary is mentioned.
- b) **A fee to confirm the seat.** (correct)
- c) The message has a company logo.

**Teach card (24 words).**
> A real employer pays you. A fee for registration, training, a kit, or a "refundable deposit" is the sign of
> a fake job offer.

**Show me.** Ask: "Tap the line that gives it away."

| Part | Weak offer | Good offer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Congrats, you're selected! No interview needed." | "Shortlisted. Interview on Thursday, 11 am." |
| 2 | **"Pay ₹1,499 registration to block your seat."** (spot) | "No fees at any stage." |
| 3 | HR chats only on WhatsApp. | Mail from the company domain, role on its careers page. |

Feedback: "Selected without an interview, then a fee. The good offer has a process and costs nothing."

**Guided try.** Meenakshi in Madurai is asked to pay ₹999 for "training material", refundable after joining.
Which is right?

| Option | Correct | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| a) Refundable means safe. | No, **misconception: "A refundable fee is not a real cost."** | The promise of a refund is part of the pitch. CERT-In's rule is simply: never pay for job offers. |
| b) A fee for any job offer is the warning sign. | Yes | Real employers do not charge applicants. The government job portal says the same. |
| c) Fine if the company is famous. | No | Scammers use famous names. Check the role on the company's own site. |

### Step 2 · What to do

**Hook.** Kavya in Mysuru likes an offer but is unsure. What is the quickest real check?
- a) Ask the recruiter if it is genuine.
- b) **Find the same job on the company's own website.** (correct)
- c) Look at how many likes the job post has.

**Teach card (25 words).**
> Check the job where the scammer can't reach: the company's own careers page, or ncs.gov.in. Talk only to
> email from the company's domain. Pay nothing.

**Show me.** Ask: "Tap the check that can't be faked by the sender."

| Part | Weak check | Good check |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Asks the "HR" for proof. Gets a stamped PDF. | **Opens the company site herself and finds the role.** (spot) |
| 2 | Trusts the logo on the offer letter. | Checks the sender's email domain. |
| 3 | Pays to "hold" the offer while checking. | Pays nothing while checking. |

Feedback: "Proof sent by the recruiter can be forged. A page you open yourself is harder to fake."

**Guided try (jobs abroad).** Tenzin in Gangtok is offered a job in South-East Asia. The agent says: "Go on a
tourist visa, we'll convert it there." What fits MEA's advice?

| Option | Correct | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| a) A valid job abroad needs a work visa. Check the agent on emigrate.gov.in. | Yes | MEA says valid offers use a work or employment visa, and registered agents are listed on eMigrate. |
| b) Tourist visas are a normal shortcut. | No, **misconception: "Everyone converts the visa later."** | MEA says tourist visas are only for tourism. This is a stated warning sign. |
| c) It's fine if the agent has a big Instagram page. | No | MEA says illegal agents often work through social media and WhatsApp. |

### Step 3 · Where to report

**Hook.** Gurpreet already paid a "registration fee". Where does he report it?
- a) Only to the fake company.
- b) **1930 or cybercrime.gov.in.** (correct)
- c) Nowhere, the amount was small.

**Teach card (24 words).**
> Paid a job fee online? Report on 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in. For a job abroad, MEA's helpline is 1800 11 3090.
> Small amounts count too.

**Show me.** Ask: "Tap the better next step."

| Part | Weak | Good |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "It was only ₹1,499. Let it go." | **Calls 1930 with the UPI receipt.** (spot) |
| 2 | Pays a "processing fee" to get the refund. | Reports the WhatsApp number on Chakshu too. |

Feedback: "A 'refund fee' is a second scam. Reports also help block the number for others."

**Guided try.** Which helpline is for job problems abroad, not cyber fraud?

| Option | Correct | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| a) 1800 11 3090 (MEA). | Yes | MEA's toll-free line for overseas job complaints, from India. |
| b) 1930. | No | 1930 is the cyber fraud helpline. It is right for online money loss. |
| c) 1514. | No, **misconception: "All job helplines are the same."** | 1514 is the National Career Service helpline for the NCS portal. |

### Field try

Pick one company you'd like to work for. Find its official careers page and bookmark it.
- Cues: "While job hunting", "With a friend in placements", my own.
- Smaller version: look up ncs.gov.in once and close it.

### Later-day question

"Which of these is a sign of a fake job offer?" (Pick from: a fee to block the seat / an interview date /
an email from the company domain.)

## 3. Loan apps: check before you tap

### Evidence

| Claim | Source quote | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Unauthorised lending apps charge hidden fees and misuse phone data. | "These reports also refer to excessive rates of interest and additional hidden charges being demanded from borrowers; adoption of unacceptable and high-handed recovery methods; and misuse of agreements to access data on the mobile phones of the borrowers." | RBI press release 2020-2021/819, 23 Dec 2020. https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=50846 |
| An app that lends for a bank or NBFC must name it upfront. | "digital lending platforms which are used on behalf of Banks and NBFCs should disclose name of the Bank(s) or NBFC(s) upfront to the customers." | Same |
| Do not share KYC copies with unverified apps; report to police or Sachet. | "consumers should never share copies of KYC documents with unidentified persons, unverified/unauthorised Apps and should report such Apps/Bank Account information associated with the Apps to concerned law enforcement agencies or use Sachet portal ( https://sachet.rbi.org.in ) to file an on-line complaint." | Same |
| RBI lists apps that regulated lenders report. It is a check, not a guarantee. | "the list of DLAs is being made available on the website for the limited purpose of aiding the customers in verifying the claim of a DLA's association with a RE. The list shall be based on data submitted by the REs on the CIMS portal of RBI on as is basis, without any further validation check by the Reserve Bank." | RBI press release 2025-2026/288, 8 May 2025. https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=60403 |
| The list is live. | RBI home page links "DLA's deployed by Regulated Entities" (to data.rbi.org.in), seen 24 Oct 2026. | https://www.rbi.org.in/ |
| The RBI booklet covers fake loan apps. | "The booklet elaborates on safeguards against commonly used fraudulent techniques, such as, SIM swaps, vishing/phishing links, lottery, etc., including fake loan websites and digital apps." | RBI press release on BE(A)WARE, 7 Mar 2022. https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=53372 (booklet itself not opened, see §Could not open) |

**Grade: strong** for the check (name the lender, RBI list). **Moderate** for the warning signs, which come from
a 2020 release. Setting: complaints to RBI; national.

### Step 1 · Spot the sign

**Hook.** Imran in Lucknow needs ₹5,000 for exam fees by Friday. An app promises money "in 5 minutes, no
documents". Before he installs it, what is the one thing to find?
- a) Its star rating.
- b) **The name of the bank or NBFC giving the loan.** (correct)
- c) How fast it pays.

**Teach card (26 words).**
> An app that lends for a bank or NBFC must name that lender upfront. No lender named, or only a brand name,
> is the warning sign.

**Show me.** Ask: "Tap what the weak app is missing."

| Part | Weak app | Good app |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Instant cash! No documents!" | "Loan by XYZ Finance Ltd, an RBI-registered NBFC." |
| 2 | **Lender: not shown anywhere.** (spot) | Key fact statement with total cost before you accept. |
| 3 | Asks for contacts and gallery access. | Asks only for what the loan needs. |

Feedback: "The weak app hides who lends and wants your contacts. Misuse of phone data is a sign RBI warned about."

**Guided try.** Pooja in Indore sees an app with 4.6 stars and lakhs of downloads, but no lender name. Which is
right?

| Option | Correct | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| a) Popular apps are safe. | No, **misconception: "Ratings prove an app is legal."** | Ratings can be bought. The rule is: the lender must be named upfront. |
| b) No lender named is the warning sign. | Yes | RBI requires platforms lending for banks and NBFCs to name them upfront. |
| c) It's fine if the loan is small. | No | Small loans from unauthorised apps carry the same hidden-charge risk. |

### Step 2 · What to do

**Hook.** Imran found the lender's name. What is the next check?
- a) Ask friends if they've heard of it.
- b) **Look for the app in RBI's list of lending apps.** (correct)
- c) Read the app's own "About us".

**Teach card (29 words).**
> RBI's website lists lending apps that regulated lenders report (Citizen's Corner, "DLA's deployed by Regulated
> Entities"). Not on the list? Step back. On it? Still read the full cost.

**Show me.** Ask: "Tap the stronger check."

| Part | Weak check | Good check |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reads the app's own claim: "RBI approved". | **Searches the app name on RBI's lending-app list.** (spot) |
| 2 | Shares Aadhaar and PAN photos to "see the offer". | Shares no KYC until the check is done. |

Feedback: "An app can write 'RBI approved' about itself. RBI's own list is the place to check. RBI says the list is
not a guarantee, so the full cost still matters."

**Guided try.** The app is on RBI's list. Does that make every term fair?

| Option | Correct | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| a) No. RBI says the list is not validated, so read the cost. | Yes | RBI built the list from lenders' own reports, without further checks. |
| b) Yes, RBI checked it fully. | No, **misconception: "Listed means RBI-approved in every way."** | RBI says the list is for checking the lender link only. |
| c) The list doesn't matter at all. | No | It is a useful first filter for the lender link. |

### Step 3 · Where to report

**Hook.** An unnamed app keeps calling Pooja's contacts about a loan. Where can she complain?
- a) Only the app's support chat.
- b) **Police or cybercrime.gov.in, and RBI's Sachet portal.** (correct)
- c) Nowhere, she agreed to its terms.

**Teach card (27 words).**
> Report an unauthorised loan app to the police, on 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in, or on RBI's Sachet portal. Save
> the app name and the bank details it uses.

**Show me.** Ask: "Tap the better report."

| Part | Weak | Good |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pays extra to make the calls stop. | **Files a report with the app name and its UPI ID.** (spot) |
| 2 | Uninstalls and deletes the screenshots. | Keeps screenshots of the calls and messages. |

Feedback: "RBI asks people to report the app and the bank account linked to it. Proof makes that possible."

**Guided try.** Which portal is RBI's own for complaints about unauthorised lending?

| Option | Correct | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| a) Sachet (sachet.rbi.org.in). | Yes | RBI named Sachet for online complaints about such apps. |
| b) SEBI SCORES. | No | SCORES is for securities market complaints, not loan apps. |
| c) The app store review box. | No, **misconception: "A bad review is a report."** | A review warns users. It does not reach the regulator or police. |

### Field try

Open rbi.org.in, find Citizen's Corner, and look up one lending app you have heard of.
- Cues: "When I next see a loan ad", my own.
- Smaller version: save the path "RBI > Citizen's Corner > DLA's deployed by Regulated Entities" in notes.

### Later-day question

"An app that lends for a bank must show the bank's name (true / false)." (Answer: true.)

## 4. The PIN only sends money (UPI)

### Evidence

| Claim | Source quote | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Receiving money never needs a PIN or a QR scan. | "Transactions involving receipt of money do not require scanning barcodes / QR codes or entering MPIN. Thus, exercise caution if asked to do so." | RBI press release 2021-2022/1630, 28 Jan 2022. https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=53185 |
| Fraudsters misused UPI "collect requests". | "Misuse the 'collect request' feature of UPI by sending fake payment requests with messages like 'Enter your UPI PIN' to receive money." | Same |
| Same rule from CERT-In. | "Always remember, you don't need a UPI PIN or OTP to receive money." | CERT-In CIAD-2024-0050, 24 Oct 2024 (link in idea 2) |
| Check the receiver's name before paying by QR. | "Verify the sender's banking name before making payments using QR codes." | Same |

| Accounts for rent are scouted on social media. | "Current accounts and saving accounts are scouted through social media; majorly from Telegram and Facebook." | Same |
| Short fraud lessons helped in one US trial, but faded without a reminder. | "Individuals who saw only the video or text treatment at baseline were no better at identifying fraudulent investment opportunities six months later than individuals assigned to control. However, we find that effects persist if consumers are provided reminders." | Burke, Kieffer, Mottola & Perez-Arce (2021 working paper; published *J. Econ. Behav. Organ.* 198, 2022, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2022.03.028). Read from the open working paper: https://gflec.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Burke-Kieffer-Mottola-Perez-Arce-Can-Educational-Interventions-Reduce-Susceptibility-to-Financial-Fraud-CB2021.pdf |

**Grade: strong** for the checks (CCPA, I4C). **Moderate** for "lessons help": one US randomised trial of
investment fraud, 1,976 adults with an average age of 54, run in 2019. Not Indian, not 18-25. Use it only to
justify spaced reminders, not as a promise.

### Step 1 · Spot the sign

**Hook.** Divya's senior pitches a "business": buy a ₹6,000 starter kit, then earn on everyone you bring in.
Which of these is the warning sign?
- a) It sells a real product.
- b) **Earnings mainly from recruiting, plus a large joining fee.** (correct)
- c) It has a registered office.

**Teach card (27 words).**
> Three signs of a pyramid scheme: a large joining fee, pay for recruiting people, and no right to return the
> goods. Earnings should come from real sales.

**Show me.** Ask: "Tap where the money comes from."

| Part | Weak offer | Good offer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Starter kit ₹6,000, compulsory." | "No joining fee." |
| 2 | **"₹500 bonus for every friend who joins."** (spot) | "Commission only on products sold to customers." |
| 3 | "No returns on kits." | "30-day return and buy-back." |

Feedback: "In the weak offer, money comes from recruits, not customers. That's the CCPA's main sign."

(Editor note: ₹6,000 and ₹500 are story amounts, not income claims. Keep them as costs and pay-per-recruit,
never as "you can earn ₹X".)

**Guided try.** Sameer in Bhopal is told: "You don't need to sell. Just add 2 people under you." Which is right?

| Option | Correct | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| a) Easy, because no selling is needed. | No, **misconception: "Recruiting is just networking."** | Pay for recruiting rather than sales is a listed sign of a pyramid scheme. |
| b) Pay that depends on recruiting is the warning sign. | Yes | CCPA lists compensation for recruiting as a major sign. |
| c) It's fine if the person is a friend. | No | Friends often pitch these in good faith. The structure is what matters. |

### Step 2 · What to do (three checks for any offer)

**Hook.** Kavya is offered a monthly payment to "let a company use" her bank account for payments. What is the
risk?
- a) None, it's passive income.
- b) **Her account could carry crime money, and she could face arrest.** (correct)
- c) Only a tax issue.

**Teach card (29 words).**
> Three checks. Do I pay first? Is the pay for real work or sales? Does it use my bank account, SIM or ID?
> A bad answer to any: stop.

**Show me.** Ask: "Tap the check that fails."

| Check | Weak offer ("account job") | Good offer (tutoring a Class 10 student) |
|---|---|---|
| Do I pay first? | No | No |
| Pay for real work? | No, just "access" | Yes, teaching hours |
| **Uses my account or ID for others?** | **Yes** (spot) | No |

Feedback: "I4C says renting your account can lead to legal action, including arrest. The good offer pays for work
you do."

**Guided try.** Imran is offered money to open a new bank account and hand over the debit card and SIM. Which is
right?

| Option | Correct | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| a) It's his account, so it's legal. | No, **misconception: "Lending my own account is my choice."** | I4C warns that crime money in such accounts can lead to arrest. |
| b) This is renting out an account. Say no. | Yes | I4C advises citizens not to sell or rent bank accounts to anyone. |
| c) Fine if he deletes UPI after a month. | No | The risk comes from what flows through while others control it. |

### Step 3 · Where to report

**Hook.** Sameer wants to complain about a direct-selling firm that won't refund his kit. Where?
- a) 1930.
- b) **National Consumer Helpline, 1915.** (correct)
- c) SEBI.

**Teach card (29 words).**
> Refund or product problems with a selling firm: National Consumer Helpline, 1915 (8 am to 8 pm) or
> consumerhelpline.gov.in. Asked to rent your account, or lost money online? 1930.

**Show me.** Ask: "Tap the right channel."

| Part | Case | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kit not refunded by a direct-selling firm | **1915 or consumerhelpline.gov.in** (spot) |
| 2 | Telegram offer to rent his account | 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in |

Feedback: "Consumer problems go to the consumer helpline. Fraud and account renting go to cybercrime."

**Guided try.** Kavya's account was used and is now frozen. What helps?

| Option | Correct | Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| a) Talk to the bank and file on cybercrime.gov.in with what happened. | Yes | Being open early helps. Many people are drawn in by offers that look like jobs. |
| b) Close the account quietly. | No, **misconception: "If I close it, it's over."** | The record stays. Reporting and talking to the bank are better. |
| c) Ask the "company" to fix it. | No | They are the ones who used it. |

(Editor note: frozen accounts can need legal help. Add a neutral line pointing to the District Legal Services
Authority only after an editor checks the current NALSA helpline.)

### Field try

Take one earning offer you've seen (an ad, a forward, a senior's pitch). Run the three checks on it in your
head. That's all.
- Cues: "Next time I see a 'work from home' ad", my own.
- Smaller version: write the three checks in your notes app.

### Later-day question

"An offer pays you for every person you recruit. Which sign is this?" (Answer: compensation for recruiting, a
sign of a pyramid scheme.)

## Cross-cutting risks for editors

| Risk | What to do |
|---|---|
| Shame for people who were scammed. | No "careless", "gullible" or "fell for it" in learner-facing copy (several sources use "gullible"; do not copy it). Every report step says that fast reporting helps and that these are organised networks. |
| Fear-selling. | No loss totals in hooks. No sirens or red screens. Copy names the sign and the next step, then stops. The big figures (₹8,690 crore saved etc.) are kept out of lessons. |
| Income promises. | No "earn ₹X". Story amounts are costs or fees only. Idea 8 teaches checks, not ways to earn. |
| Rules change (e.g. UPI collect requests). | Each claim gets a `checkedOn` date. Review the track every 6 months. Teach rules that survive changes (PIN only sends). |
| Naming firms. | Never name real companies, apps or groups as scams (the CCPA list includes firms under investigation, not convicted). Use made-up names. |
| Advice to engage scammers. | No "scam-baiting" or "test the caller". Every step ends the contact. |
| Stereotypes. | Victims and helpers vary by gender, age, region, village and city. Elders are not shown as the "easy target"; young people are not shown as greedy. |
| Distress (digital arrest, frozen accounts). | Keep a calm "tell someone you trust" line. No therapy claims. If a support helpline is added, an editor must verify it first. |
| Phone pull. | Field tries take under a minute and end with "close the app". None after bedtime (spec 21 Part B). |
| Evidence type. | Grades here rate the source, not an effect size. Teach cards state plain rules; the regulator's name goes in `sources[]`, not in teach text, because the validator bans authority words ("says", "according to") there. Feedback lines above sometimes say "RBI says" or "MHA lists"; editors rewrite those if the editorial check flags them. |

## Myths not to teach

| Myth | What the sources say | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "If they paid me first, they're genuine." | The first payout is step 3 of the task-scam pattern. | PIB 6 Dec 2023 |
| "A refundable fee is safe." | Never pay for job offers. | CERT-In 2024 |
| "Listed on RBI's site means fully approved." | The list is not validated by RBI. | RBI PR 8 May 2025 |
| "You need the PIN to receive money." | Receipt of money needs no PIN or QR scan. | RBI PR 28 Jan 2022 |
| "Collect requests are gone, so UPI scams are over." | Person-to-person collect was set to end; merchant requests and QR codes remain. | PTI/The Week 14 Aug 2025 |
| "Police can put you under digital arrest." | No agency arrests or investigates on a video call. | I4C via PTI 2024; TRAI via PIB 2025 |
| "One fraud lesson protects you for good." | In one US trial the effect was gone at 6 months without a reminder. | Burke et al. 2021 |
