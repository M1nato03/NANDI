## Problems that run through both topics

These cause most of the low "fresh" scores, so fixing them in the writing rules fixes many items at once.

1. **The step shape itself creates repetition.** Each step's hook, spot, both guided tries, takeaway and 2–3 practice items all ask the same one idea in the same direction. That is 6–8 hits per idea, often with the same numbers. This is the owner's "exact repetition" complaint, now inside one lesson instead of across drops.
   *Rule to add:* within a step, guided tries stay close to the teach card. Each practice item must then do one *different* thing with the idea:
   - use it in reverse (find the input from the output);
   - catch a misconception;
   - apply it in a new life area;
   - use it together with the previous step;
   - handle an edge case where it doesn't apply.

   No practice item may use the same numbers or story frame as a guided try.
2. **Every match item has only 2 pairs** (12 of 12). Once one pair is placed, the other is forced, and in most of them the pairs share a word ("Twelve firms in many industries" ↔ "A real spread"; "Index fund" ↔ "Follows a list, lower fee"). Use 3–4 pairs that don't share words, or drop the type.
3. **Fill-the-step items mostly ask for the concept's own name**, which is the topic title on screen: diversification-11, emergency-fund-10, compounding-15, free-sugars-14, protein-foods-14, upi-scams-13, shares-12, fad-diet-claims-14. Turn each one into a fill-in of a *reason* or a *number worked out*, not a label.
4. **Explain-it-back items repeat the takeaway** (index-funds-9, rule-of-72-15, risk-return-16, upi-scams-16). Make the learner pick the explanation that fits a *new* case.
5. **Correct answers look different from wrong ones:**
   - The correct option is the single longest in 69/158 money questions and 55/126 health questions (44% overall; chance with 3 options is 33%). It is worst in fad-diet-claims (11/16), food-label-reading (10/15) and risk-return (9/16).
   - Distractors use absolute words (always / never / exactly / nothing / only / all) in 18% (money) and 28% (health) of cases, against 7–8% for correct options.
   - So a test-wise learner wins by picking "the long, hedged one". Balance the lengths and give some correct answers absolute wording.
6. **Hints restate the answer.** 27 guided hints share a key word with the correct option. Examples: shares-s2.g2 hint "It is a meeting held for the owners" for the answer "At the general meeting"; fad-diet-claims-s3.g2 hint "The guide sets a floor of 1,000 kcal". Spec 14 §7 aims for 85–95% first-try correct; these will score about 100%, which the spec counts as too easy. A hint should point to *where to look*, not give the answer.
7. **Health teach cards cite an authority instead of explaining.** 12 of 24 health teach cards rest on "WHO/CDC/NHS/NCCIH/the guide says" (money: 2 of 30). A tired reader learns who said it, not why it is true. Keep the source in the source panel and use the card for the *because*.
8. **Untaught content is tested.** These items are tagged to a step whose teach card does not contain the idea:
   - inflation-14 (nominal/real), risk-return-9 (average outcome), risk-return-10 ("dividends"), diversification-13 (fees), diversification-9 ("portfolio");
   - shares-10 (dilution), loan-emi-9/-11 ("principal", "tenure"), index-funds-s1.g2 ("weight"), index-funds-s2.hook ("active funds"), upi-scams-s3.g2 (what to do after fraud);
   - protein-foods-s1.g2 (enzymes, haemoglobin), protein-foods-12 (food sources), free-sugars-13 (why the limit), free-sugars-15 (limit scales with intake);
   - hydration-cues-14 (children and elders), sleep-recovery-11 (REM brain activity), sleep-recovery-12 (REM timing), sleep-recovery-15 (bedroom);
   - exercise-heart-15 (3 in 10 adults), iron-and-anaemia-9 (prevalence), iron-and-anaemia-10 (daily mg), fad-diet-claims-16 (why slow).

   This is the "a quiz on facts you were never told" problem from spec 14 §1, back again.
9. **US/UK sources shown to an Indian learner.** These are named on cards: CDC (sleep, exercise), NHS (hydration, iron), NCCIH (detox), "a US investor guide" (diversification), "US big-company shares" (risk-return-s3.g1). Where an Indian source exists, use it. Otherwise say "health researchers" and keep the citation in sources.

## Money & Markets

### inflation — Taught 3 · Fresh 2
**(a) One idea per step.**
- s3 packs two ideas: "idle cash shrinks" and "the RBI aims for 4%".
- The first of those repeats s2 (prices outrun your money). s3.spot (Dadaji's mattress), s3.g1 (₹10,000 in a cupboard) and -10 (Riya's cash at home) are all s2's idea again.
- *Fix:* make s3 only "a little inflation is normal; the RBI aims for 4% (2–6%)". Move "idle cash" items to s2, or cut them.

**(b) Teach explains?**
- s1 says what inflation is but not *why a basket* (one item can jump while others don't). g1 (onions) then tests exactly that.
  Rewrite: "Inflation is how much prices rose in a year. India tracks a big basket of everyday goods, because one item, like onions, can jump while others stay flat."
- s3 never says why the target isn't 0. Then -15 asks "any inflation means trouble: what's wrong?" and the answer is "India even aims for 4%". That is an appeal to authority, not a reason.
  Rewrite s3 teach: "A little inflation is normal. If prices fell every year, people would wait to buy and businesses would slow. So the RBI aims for about 4%."
  Rewrite -15 correct option: "Mild, steady rises are normal; the RBI even aims for about 4%."

**(c) Same-feeling questions.**
- "Fixed rupees, rising prices, buys less" appears in s2.hook (₹2,000 pocket money), s2.g1 (₹100 basket), s3.spot, s3.g1 (₹10,000 cupboard) and inflation-10.
- "Pay vs prices" appears in s2.spot (Kavya), s2.g2 (Arjun/Meena/Ravi), -9 (fee vs income) and -13 (pension).
- "Same month last year" appears in s1.hook, s1.spot, s1.g2 and -16 step a.
- "4%" is recalled in s3.hook, s3.g2 and -15.

  Keep one of each and replace the rest with transfer items:
  - "Your phone plan stays ₹299 but data drops from 2 GB to 1.5 GB a day. Is that inflation?"
  - "Rent went up 8% and your stipend 8%. Better off, worse off, or the same?"

**(d) Giveaways.**
- -12: the correct "50,000+ shops" is the first node of the s1 diagram. "It guesses from a few big cities" is a distractor nobody picks.
- -10 "Both lose the same": nobody picks it.
- -15 b and c agree with the friend, so only (a) answers "what is wrong".
- s1.g2's hint "Look for a gap of one full year" gives the answer.

**(e) Plain words.**
- -14 brings in "nominal pay" inside the stem with no teaching. Rewrite stem: "Pay counted in rupees is your *rupee* pay. Pay counted by what it can buy is your ___ pay." Options: real / extra / gross.
- s1.hook "retail inflation": say "price rise (inflation)".

**(f) Step fit.**
- -11 ("a toy's inflation rate") and -16 compute "inflation" from one item. That goes straight against s1's point (misconception inflation-m1: one price is not the index).
- Reword -11 to "How much did the toy's price rise, in percent?" and -16 to "work out a price rise in percent".

**(g) India / tone.**
- -9 (college fee vs family income) can hit a student who feels guilty about fees. Swap in "your mobile recharge rises 10% a year; your stipend 5%".
- Dadaji's mattress reads a little like a joke about elders. "Cash kept at home for years" is enough.

### rule-of-72 — Taught 3 · Fresh 1
**(a)**
- s2 ("debt doubles too") is the same rule applied to a new case. It is fine as a step, but s1 never teaches "it is only a rough guess", which is misconception m1.
- The hook card mentions that, but no step does. Add "It is a quick guess, close but not exact" to the s1 teach.

**(b)** s2 teach: add the why: "because unpaid interest is added to what you owe, and then that earns interest too".

**(c) Same-feeling questions.**
- s1 is seven divisions: hook (72 ÷ 6), spot (72 ÷ 4), g1 (72 ÷ 9), g2 (which rate gives 6 years), -9 (72 ÷ 2), -10 (72 ÷ 6 = 12) and -11 (72 ÷ 8).
- **-10 is s1.g2 word for word in idea and numbers**, and s1.hook is its mirror (6% → 12 years).
- **-13 is s2.g2** (6% savings vs 18% debt).
- s3: -15 repeats s3.g1 ("when does it work"), and -16, s3.hook and s3.spot are all "jumpy rate".

  Replace -10 with a debt-plus-saving question: "Your ₹5,000 savings earn 6%, and your ₹5,000 card debt costs 36%. In 2 years, which has doubled?"
  Replace -11 with a mixed-step question: "Your money must double before you are 30, 9 years away. What steady rate do you need?"

**(d)**
- -10's stem prints "72 ÷ 6 = ___", so it is a division drill, and "432%" is a distractor nobody picks.
- -15 b/c, s3.g2 b ("divide 12 by 72") and c ("Nothing is wrong" when the stem asks for the flaw): nobody picks these.
- -11 frame "On a train, Arjun's uncle says": cut the padding.

**(e)**
- s3 teach "for them it only answers a what-if question" needs a second read. Rewrite: "For shares it can only say: *if* it grew 12% every year, it would double in 6. Real years go up and down."
- -16 mixes two flaws (rough rule, and average vs steady). A tired reader can't tell which one is being asked about. Make it one flaw: "A fund went +30%, −10%, +20%… Can 72 ÷ its average give an exact doubling date?"

**(g) India.**
- 18% card interest is low for India. Most Indian credit cards charge about 3–3.6% a month, roughly 40% a year, which doubles in under two years.
- Instant loan apps are often higher. A learner told "card debt doubles in about 4 years" may relax.
- Ask the fact-checker for a typical Indian card APR and use it.

### upi-scams — Taught 3 · Fresh 1
