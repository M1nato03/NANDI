# CJ4 · What Indian 18-25s want to learn (demand review)

Job 1 of batch 4 ("useful now" life-skill topics). Date: 23 Oct 2026.
Question: which practical skills do Indian 18-25s seek out, and which of Jenil's example topics does the data back?

Scope note: this is **demand** evidence (what people look for, buy, enrol in, or what employers ask for).
It does not say whether a skill can be taught well. That is Job 2 (`cj4-evidence.md`).

---

## 1. Bottom line

- The clearest seeker demand is **work-and-money skills**: spoken English, side income, getting a job, government paperwork and exams, share market, business, and AI tools.
- Several of Jenil's examples (sleep, focus, detective reasoning, puzzles, hackathons) are **need-driven or entertainment-driven**, not search-driven. The data shows the problem exists. It does not show 18-25s searching for a fix.
- The three biggest seeker categories ("part-time income", "share market", "Instagram growth") are also the **most scam-prone**. A safe NANDI version must teach how to judge offers, not promise income.
- "Communication" is the one soft skill that shows up in every employer survey I opened. It is the best-supported bridge from Jenil's "be more fun to talk to" and "sell anything" ideas.

### 3.5 Jobs and employers

- Youth (15-29) unemployment 2025: "improved to 9.9%"; "urban youth still face a high unemployment rate of 13.6% compared to 8.3% in rural areas"; "Approximately 25.0% of youth (15-29 years) fall into the NEET (not in employment, education or training) category." (S14, secondary)
- Employers' top hiring priority: "Communication skills" (37%), then "Technical expertise relevant to the industry" (33%). (S15)
- Employers on freshers: job-ready skills gap, "Nearly 38% of employers cited this as a key concern"; "Soft skills such as communication, teamwork, and time management are also in short supply, with 27% of employers" reporting trouble. (S16)
- India Skills Report 2026: "AI, data analytics, cloud computing, and cybersecurity have emerged as the most in-demand skills"; gig workforce "projected to reach 23.5 million by 2030". (S17)
- LinkedIn Skills on the Rise 2025 India top 5: creativity and innovation, code review, problem solving, pre-screening, strategic thinking. The list also includes communication, adaptability, AI literacy, customer engagement, prompt engineering, market analysis. (S18; I saw this list in news coverage only.)

### 3.6 Money and markets

- "93% of over 1 crore individual F&O traders incurred average losses of around ₹2 lakh per trader (inclusive of transaction costs)". (S10)
- "The proportion of young traders (below 30 years) in the F&O segment rose from 31% in FY23 to 43% in FY24." (S10)
- "Despite consecutive years of losses, more than 75% of loss-making traders continued trading in F&O." (S10)
- "Younger cohorts exhibit higher awareness of securities market products, with Gen Z at 66 percent". (S11)
- "56 percent turn to financial influencers on social media"; "62 percent of investors make some of their investment decisions based on finfluencer recommendations"; YouTube is the top platform at 91 percent. (S11)
- A quoted respondent: "I watch a lot of financial videos on YouTube. Can learn a lot from there" – Lucknow, Investor, 18-25 years. (S11)

### 3.7 Scams aimed at job-seekers

- Task scams: "Targets are mostly retired employees, women, and unemployed youth looking for part-time jobs." Ads use phrases like "Ghar baithe job"; tasks like "Video Likes and Subscribe" or "Maps Rating"; once a larger sum is deposited, it is frozen. (S12)
- MLM: under the 2021 rules, "direct selling entities and direct sellers are prohibited from promoting a pyramid scheme" and are "banned from participation in money circulation schemes in the garb of doing direct selling business". (S13)

### 3.8 Creating, focus, sleep, projects

- Creators: "over 2–2.5 million monetized content creators" in India. (S20)
- YouTube: "93% of viewers report using YouTube to gather information and/or expand their knowledge". (S19, platform-commissioned)
- Smartphone use: "The prevalence of smartphone addiction was 39.9%" among 1,067 Kerala college students aged 18-23 (MUST screening tool). "a delay of over 30 minutes between phone use and sleep" had aOR 3.38. (S22)
- Sleep: "between 25- 72% of Indian university students reported poor sleep quality" and "17-44% experienced EDS" (excessive daytime sleepiness). (S23)
- Hackathons: Smart India Hackathon 2025 had "72,165 idea submissions" from "68,766 student teams". (S24)

## 4. Ranked skill tracks (by seeker demand)

How I ranked:
- **Seeker signal** = people choose it (platform categories, enrolments, searches, downloads).
- **Need signal** = surveys show a gap or harm, but not that people look for a fix.
- **Employer signal** = recruiters ask for it.
- Rank uses seeker signal first, then employer, then need. More independent sources = higher.
- "Fit" = how well 2-minute offline lessons plus a real-world try can carry it. A first guess; Job 2 tests it.

## 6. Scam-prone tracks and how a safe version differs

| Track | Scam pattern in India | Evidence | Safe NANDI version |
|---|---|---|---|
| Part-time income / online earning | Task scams: like videos, rate places, small payout, then "invest more", money frozen | S12 (MHA/I4C, Dec 2023) | Teach the pattern and the red flags: pay-to-work, Telegram/WhatsApp "HR", early small payouts. Point to real routes (internships, tutoring, freelance basics) with no income figures. Report path: cybercrime.gov.in or 1930 (per a bank advisory found in search; I did not open an I4C page). |
| Share market / trading tips | Tip groups, finfluencer calls, F&O "strategies" | S10: 93% of F&O traders lost; young traders 43%. S11: 62% of investors act on finfluencer advice. | No tips, no stock names, no live prices, no return claims. Teach base rates (S10), costs, and how to check if an adviser is SEBI-registered. SEBI's own rules limit performance claims in education content (reported, not opened by me). |
| MLM / "be your own boss" | Recruit-to-earn, buy-in kits | S13: pyramid and money circulation schemes banned under 2021 rules | Teach how to tell a product business from a recruitment chain: who pays you, for what, and does income depend on recruiting. |
| Instagram growth | Paid follower packs, "guaranteed reach" courses | Seekho category demand only (S1). No fraud data found. | Teach making and feedback loops, not hacks. No follower or income targets. Never frame success as numbers. |
| "Earn with AI" | Paid courses promising income from AI tools | No India data found | Teach AI as a study and work tool. No earning claims. |
| Fake job offers | Job ads that ask for fees | S12 (same scam family) | Inside the job track: "a real employer never asks you to pay". |

General rule for all money tracks: no income promises, no "fast", no countdowns, no screenshots of earnings. This matches NANDI's hard rules (no health or money promises, sourced facts only).

## 7. What this means for NANDI (recommendations)

Shortlist for a first life-skill batch, weighing demand and fit:

| Priority | Track | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Talk to anyone (conversation and listening) | Top employer ask (S15, S16). Covers "fun to talk to" and the honest half of "sell anything". Job 5 is drafting it. |
| 2 | Spot the scam, earn safely | Takes the #2 Seekho demand (S1) and makes it safe (S12, S13). No competitor in my sources sells this angle. |
| 3 | Money you will actually use (budget, discount, loan, interest) | Huge gap (S9: 11% can work out a loan repayment). Extends the existing Money topic. |
| 4 | Market sense without tips | High interest among young people (S10, S11). Base rates are a strong, sourced hook. |
| 5 | First job (interview answers, CV lines, internship hunting) | High seeker and need signals (S6, S14, S16). |
| 6 | Focus and phone habits | Need is clear (S22). Matches the app's reason to exist. Seeker demand is unproven, so test it. |
| 7 | Using AI well | Highest search signal (S8). Teach offline: how to ask, how to check, what not to paste. |

Hold for later:
- Sleep (need is strong, but the no-health-promise rule makes copy hard; wait for Job 2).
- Creating (keep, but as a making habit, not growth).
- Photos (body-image risk; needs careful framing).
- Hackathons, detective reasoning, puzzles (niche or no demand data; use as hooks inside other tracks).

Skip:
- Coding (poor fit for 2-minute offline steps).
- Exam prep (owned by Unacademy, PW and others; S4).

Language: Seekho's content is in "Hinglish and regional languages" (S1). English-only titles may undersell the work-and-money tracks. Job 4 covers Hinglish variants.

Kill test for this ranking: if a 2-week test of the top 3 tracks shows lower start or finish rates than the current Psychology and Money topics, demand in Seekho's audience does not carry over to NANDI's users. Drop to the need-based tracks (focus, money basics) and re-rank.
