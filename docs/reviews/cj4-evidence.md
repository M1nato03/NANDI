# CJ4 evidence: what research supports for 8 life-skill tracks

Cloud job batch 4, Job 2. Branch `ideas/cj4-evidence`. Written 23 Oct 2026.
Research only. No app code or content changed.

## How this was done

- Abstracts pulled from PubMed (NCBI E-utilities) and Europe PMC, plus publisher,
  university and government pages. Quotes are copied from what was opened.
- Where only a search snippet was seen, the source is listed under
  "Could not open" and is **not** used as a quote.
- YouTube titles from the batch brief are treated as inspiration only.
- Evidence grade used in the tables:

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Meta-analysis, large preregistered study, or repeated in several labs |
| B | Several peer-reviewed experiments, or one large field experiment |
| C | One small study, a model, or correlational data only |
| X | Failed to replicate, or openly contested |

## Summary

| # | Track | Evidence base | Can 2-min lessons + real practice move it? | Main risk | Call |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Conversation, being fun to talk to | Strong (A/B) | Yes. Behaviours are small and easy to try today | Social anxiety, "charm scripts" | Ship first |
| 7 | Reasoning like a detective | Strong (A/B), incl. field transfer | Yes. Short debiasing training has lasting effects | Stereotyping, "lie-spotting" | Ship first |
| 6 | Focus and "cheap dopamine" | Mixed: tools strong (A/B), popular claims weak (X) | Yes for planning and friction tools | Self-diagnosing "addiction", guilt | Ship, myth-busting angle |
| 5 | Energy and rest | Strong on facts (A/B) | Partly. Knowledge yes; behaviour needs a routine | Health promises, medical advice | Ship with care (info, not advice) |
| 2 | Selling and persuasion, ethically | Moderate (B/C) | Partly. Concepts yes; skill needs role-play | Manipulation, scams | Ship as "negotiate and spot tactics" |
| 3 | Creating, not just consuming | Moderate to weak (B/C/X) | Partly. Starting a habit yes; quality needs output | Creator-income hype, comparison | Small track, honest framing |
| 8 | Presenting yourself | Mixed, several famous claims failed (X) | Partly, and risky | Body image for 18-25s | Only with strict guardrails |
| 4 | Winning projects and hackathons | Weak. No peer-reviewed study found on what wins | Little. Real events needed | All-nighters, prize hype | Fold into other tracks |

Three cross-track findings:

1. **Short training can transfer** when it includes practice and feedback
   (Morewedge et al. 2015; Sellier et al. 2019), and when learners compare two
   worked cases instead of one (Loewenstein et al. 2003). This supports NANDI's
   "Show me" + guided try + spaced practice format.
2. **Several famous self-help claims failed to replicate**: power posing on hormones,
   "willpower runs out", the phone "brain drain", dopamine fasting. These make
   good honest hooks ("what the viral video got wrong").
3. **Most studies are from US/UK/European students or online panels.** None of the
   key findings below were tested in Indian 18-25 settings (hostels, family
   functions, seniors). Lessons should say "studies found", not "always".

## 1. Conversation and being fun to talk to

### Teachable claims

| # | Claim (plain) | Source quote | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Asking follow-up questions makes people like you more | "people who ask more questions, particularly follow-up questions, are better liked by their conversation partners" | Huang et al. 2017, JPSP, [PMID 28447835](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28447835/) | B |
| 2 | People don't expect this, so they under-use it | "people do not anticipate that question-asking increases interpersonal liking" | Same | B |
| 3 | Questions work because they show you are listening | "responsiveness, an interpersonal construct that captures listening, understanding, validation, and care" ... "responsiveness explains the effect of question-asking on liking" | Same | B |
| 4 | After a chat, the other person liked you more than you think | "people systematically underestimated how much their conversation partners liked them and enjoyed their company, an illusion we call the liking gap" | Boothby et al. 2018, Psych Sci, [PMID 30183512](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30183512/) | B |
| 5 | Deeper talk is less awkward than you expect | "Participants were especially prone to overestimate how awkward deep conversations would be compared with shallow conversations" | Kardas, Kumar & Epley 2022, JPSP, [PMID 34591541](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34591541/) | B |
| 6 | Talking to a stranger nearby usually feels better than staying silent | "participants reported a more positive (and no less productive) experience when they connected than when they did not" | Epley & Schroeder 2014, JEP:General, [PMID 25019381](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25019381/) | B |
| 7 | Small chats with classmates count too | "students experienced greater happiness and greater feelings of belonging on days when they interacted with more classmates than usual" | Sandstrom & Dunn 2014, PSPB, [PMID 24769739](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24769739/) | C (correlational) |
| 8 | Conversations rarely end when both want them to; you can't read it, so end kindly and clearly | "conversations almost never ended when both conversants wanted them to" ... "Conversants had little idea when their partners wanted to end" | Mastroianni et al. 2021, PNAS, [PMID 33649209](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33649209/) | B |
| 9 | Humour is high-reward, high-risk | "The successful use of humor can increase status ... but unsuccessful humor attempts (e.g., inappropriate jokes) can harm status" | Bitterly, Brooks & Schweitzer 2017, JPSP, [PMID 27831701](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27831701/) | B |
| 10 | Good listening lowers the speaker's anxiety and defensiveness | "high quality listening decreases speakers' social anxiety, which in turn reduces defensive processing" | Itzchakov, Kluger & Castro 2017, PSPB, [PMID 27856728](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27856728/) | B |

Note on claim 1: one author (Gino) later faced data-integrity findings on other
papers. This paper was audited: "The audit confirmed that all the conclusions in
the paper are valid" ([2025 correction, PMID 40111841](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40111841/)).
A 2019 critique of Study 3 (Kluger & Malloy) was answered by the authors
([PMID 31714109](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31714109/)). Treat as solid but
say "studies found".

### Myths to correct

| Myth | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| "Be interesting: talk about yourself" | People already spend "30-40% of speech output solely to informing others of their own subjective experiences", and disclosure is rewarding to the speaker ([Tamir & Mitchell 2012, PNAS, PMID 22566617](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22566617/)). Letting them talk is the gift. |
| "They didn't like me" | Liking gap (claim 4). Your read after a chat is usually too harsh. |
| "Deep questions are weird with new people" | Overestimated awkwardness (claim 5). |
| "Small talk is pointless" | Weak-tie chats track with better days (claim 7). Correlational only. |
| "Fun = jokes" | Failed jokes cost status (claim 9). Questions are the safer lever. |

### Risks

- **Social anxiety.** Lessons should not imply "anxiety is a skill gap". Missions
  must be tiny and skippable. No streak pressure.
- **Interrogation.** Studies tested more questions in normal chats. Nobody tested
  "ask 20 questions". Teach follow-ups, not quizzes. (Not tested; design guess.)
- **Charm as a tactic.** Responsiveness works because it is real listening.
  Copy should avoid "make anyone like you" promises.
- **Culture.** Talking to seniors, elders and family hierarchies in India are
  untested by these studies.

### Can 2-minute lessons move it?

Yes, likely. The behaviours are discrete ("ask one follow-up"), and in Huang et
al. people were simply *instructed* to ask more questions, and it worked.
Pair each lesson with a one-line mission and next-day reflection.

## 2. Selling and persuasion, done ethically

### Teachable claims

| # | Claim (plain) | Source quote | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Know your walk-away option before you negotiate | "BATNA stands for Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement." "In plain terms, it is what you will do if you do not reach a deal." "Misjudging your alternative can lead you to reject a favorable agreement." | Subramanian, Harvard PON, [page](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/batna/translate-your-batna-to-the-current-deal/) | Expert page |
| 2 | Ask what people need, not just what they demand | Key lessons: "Separate the people from the problem;" "Focus on interests, not positions;" | Fisher & Ury, *Getting to Yes*, PON [book page](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/shop/getting-to-yes-negotiating-agreement-without-giving-in) | Expert book |
| 3 | The first number on the table pulls the deal toward it | "whichever party, the buyer or seller, made the 1st offer obtained a better outcome" and "1st offers were a strong predictor of final settlement prices" | Galinsky & Mussweiler 2001, JPSP, [PMID 11642352](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11642352/) | B |
| 4 | You can resist an anchor by thinking of your own target or alternatives | Thinking about "one's opponent's alternatives", "reservation price", or "one's own target ... all negated the effect of 1st offers on outcomes" | Same | B |
| 5 | Understanding how they think beats just feeling for them | "perspective taking increased individuals' ability to discover hidden agreements" ... "empathy did not prove nearly as advantageous and at times was detrimental" | Galinsky et al. 2008, Psych Sci, [PMID 18399891](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18399891/) | B |
| 6 | You don't need to be loud to sell; balance talking and listening | "Ambiverts achieve greater sales productivity than extraverts or introverts do" (one study of "340 outbound-call-center representatives") | Grant 2013, Psych Sci, [PMID 23567176](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23567176/) | C (single study) |
| 7 | Listening makes you liked, but is not a persuasion hack | "The listening manipulation also successfully improved perceptions of the persuader ... Surprisingly, however, the listening manipulation did not enhance persuasion" | Santoro et al. 2025, PNAS, preregistered, N = 1,485, [PMID 39977324](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39977324/) | B |
| 8 | Much influence works below awareness, so learn to spot it | Social influence processes are "subtle, indirect, and outside of awareness" | Cialdini & Goldstein 2004, Annu Rev Psychol, [PMID 14744228](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14744228/) | Review |

Design note: comparing two negotiation cases side by side helped teams transfer a
strategy to a new negotiation; "analyzing cases separately was no better than no
training at all" ([Loewenstein, Thompson & Gentner 2003, AMLE, PDF](https://loewenstein.web.illinois.edu/papers/Loewensteinetal%20AMLE03.pdf)).
Show-me cards should pair two contrasting examples.

### Myths to correct

| Myth | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| "Great salespeople are extroverts" | Inverted U in one study (claim 6). Weak evidence either way. |
| "Never name a number first" | First offers often anchor in your favour (claim 3), if you have done your homework. |
| "Listening is a trick to get a yes" | Preregistered field test: no persuasion gain (claim 7). Listen because it is respectful. |
| "Empathy wins deals" | Perspective-taking helped more than empathy (claim 5). |

### Risks

- **Manipulation.** Anger and pressure tactics can work: people "conceded more to
  an angry opponent than to a happy one" ([van Kleef et al. 2004, PMID 14717628](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14717628/)).
  Teach this as *defence* ("spot it"), never as a tactic to use.
- **Scams.** "Sell anything to anyone" overlaps with MLM and fake-income pitches
  common in India (see Job 1). Frame the track as "negotiate fairly and spot
  pressure", with a buyer's side in every lesson.
- **Not checked:** Neil Rackham's *SPIN Selling* (question types in large sales)
  is widely cited but was not verified here. Do not cite it as evidence.

### Can 2-minute lessons move it?

Partly. Concepts (BATNA, anchors, interests) fit 30-word cards well and have
everyday uses: buying a phone, rent, freelance rates, internship stipend.
Skill needs rehearsal. Missions like "before your next purchase, write your
walk-away price" are realistic.

## 3. Creating, not just consuming

### Teachable claims

| # | Claim (plain) | Source quote | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Habits form by repeating a small action in the same daily context | Participants did a chosen behaviour "in the same context" daily; "automaticity increases following an asymptotic curve" | Lally et al. 2010, Eur J Soc Psychol, [ISPA record](https://repositorio.ispa.pt/entities/publication/fae72a1c-b2b6-4991-9951-5d20b607f688/full) | C (96 people, self-report) |
| 2 | How long it takes varies a lot | "The time it took participants to reach 95% of their asymptote of automaticity ranged from 18 to 254 days." | Same | C |
| 3 | Missing one day does not reset you | Paraphrase of the abstract (verbatim text not opened): missing a single opportunity did not meaningfully change habit formation | Same | C |
| 4 | Making something today goes with a better next day | "people felt higher activated PA and flourishing following days when they reported more creative activity than usual"; reverse direction "was not supported" | Conner, DeYoung & Silvia 2018, J Positive Psych, [UMN page](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/everyday-creative-activity-as-a-path-to-flourishing/) | C (diary, n = 658) |
| 5 | Beginners do better asking "what worked?"; experienced makers seek "what's wrong?" | "Novices sought and responded to positive feedback, and experts sought and responded to negative feedback." | Finkelstein & Fishbach 2012, J Consumer Res, [Stony Brook page](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/tell-me-what-i-did-wrong-experts-seek-and-respond-to-negative-fee) | B |
| 6 | Feedback can backfire, especially when it is about you, not the work | "over 1/3 of the FIs decreased performance"; effectiveness falls as attention moves to the self | Kluger & DeNisi 1996, Psych Bull, [HUJI record](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-effects-of-feedback-interventions-on-performance-a-historical/) | A |
| 7 | Practice matters, but less than the "10,000 hours" story says | "deliberate practice explained 26% of the variance in performance for games, 21% for music, 18% for sports, 4% for education, and less than 1% for professions" | Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald 2014, Psych Sci, [PMID 24986855](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24986855/) | A |
| 8 | An if-then plan ("After dinner, I open my sketchbook") helps you start | "implementation intentions had a positive effect of medium-to-large magnitude (d= .65) on goal attainment" | Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, [KOPS record](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f) | A |

### Myths to correct

| Myth | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| "21 days (or 66 days) makes a habit" | Range 18 to 254 days; 66 was an average from the subset with good model fit (claim 2). |
| "One missed day breaks the chain" | Not supported (claim 3). Good news for a no-streak-guilt app. |
| "10,000 hours of practice is the secret" | Practice explains a share, not all (claim 7). |
| "Scrolling passively is bad, posting actively is good" | An early experiment found passive Facebook use lowered mood via envy ([Verduyn et al. 2015, PMID 25706656](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25706656/)). A later review of 40 studies found "Most studies did not support the hypothesized associations of ASMU and PSMU with well-/ill-being" ([Valkenburg et al., PsyArXiv preprint](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/j6xqz)). Contested (X). |
| "All feedback helps" | A third of feedback interventions hurt (claim 6). |

### Risks

- **Creator-income hype.** "Switch from watching to creating" slides into
  "Instagram growth" and income promises. Keep the track about making, not
  followers or money.
- **Metrics and comparison.** Public posting brings likes and comparison; see
  Track 8 for body-image findings on selfies.
- **Privacy.** Sharing first projects publicly is optional; missions can be
  "show one friend" or "keep it private".

### Can 2-minute lessons move it?

Partly. Lessons can start the habit (if-then plan, tiny daily output, how to ask
for feedback). Quality only comes from real output over weeks. Evidence that
"creating" beats "consuming" for wellbeing is thin; don't promise it.

## 4. Winning projects and hackathons

**Headline: no peer-reviewed study was found on what predicts winning a
hackathon.** "How I won 11 hackathons" is anecdote. Adjacent research below.

### Teachable claims

| # | Claim (plain) | Source quote | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | You will underestimate how long your own project takes | "Ss' predictions of their completion times were too optimistic for a variety of academic and nonacademic tasks" | Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994, JPSP, [PDF](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/buehler-et-al-1994.pdf) | B |
| 2 | Fix: plan from your last real project, not your hopes | "In Study 4, the optimistic bias was eliminated for Ss instructed to connect relevant past experiences with their predictions." | Same | B |
| 3 | Teams learn better when it's safe to say "I'm stuck" | "team psychological safety-a shared belief held by members of a team that the team is safe for interpersonal risk taking"; "learning behavior mediates between team psychological safety and team performance" | Edmondson 1999, ASQ, 51 teams, [PDF copy](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Group_Performance/Edmondson%20Psychological%20safety.pdf) | C (correlational) |
| 4 | Equal turn-taking may matter more than having the smartest member | "not strongly correlated with the average or maximum individual intelligence of group members but is correlated with ... the equality in distribution of conversational turn-taking" | Woolley et al. 2010, Science, [PMID 20929725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20929725/) | X (see myth table) |
| 5 | A clear pitch helps a good idea; it doesn't rescue a weak one | Model predicts training "will have a more positive effect on upper quantiles of the quality distribution than lower quantiles"; "The data support all four hypotheses" | Clingingsmith & Shane 2018, Management Science, field experiment at 4 pitch competitions, [PDF](https://files.cercomp.ufg.br/weby/up/1267/o/Clingingsmith_and_Shane_-_2017_-_Training_Aspiring_Entrepreneurs_to_Pitch_Experienced_Investors_Evidence_from_a_Field_Experiment_in_the_United_States.pdf?1656363197) | B |
| 6 | Say your pitch out loud; voice signals competence | Evaluators "rated a candidate as more competent, thoughtful, and intelligent when they heard a pitch rather than read it" | Schroeder & Epley 2015, Psych Sci, [PMID 25926479](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25926479/) | B |
| 7 | Projects that last need mixed skills, not just a sprint | "long-term continuation is predicated on skill diversity among team members"; "intensive short-term activity" linked to "lower likelihood of long-term project continuation" | Nolte, Chounta & Herbsleb 2020, PACM HCI (CSCW), [NSF PAR copy](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10284449) | C |

### Myths to correct

| Myth | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| "Pull an all-nighter to win" | 6 h or less per night for two weeks "produced cognitive performance deficits equivalent to up to 2 nights of total sleep deprivation" ([Van Dongen et al. 2003, PMID 12683469](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12683469/)). Lab study, not hackathon-specific. |
| "Group smarts is one measurable factor" | Reanalysis: "the empirical support for the construct is generally weak" ([Credé & Howardson 2017, PMID 29016184](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29016184/)). Teach turn-taking as good practice, not as proven science. |
| "Pitch polish wins on its own" | Training sharpens the signal; it helps strong ideas more (claim 5). |
| "We'll finish in time this time" | Planning fallacy (claim 1). |

### Risks

- All-nighters and stimulant use (health). Do not frame sleep loss as grit.
- Prize and "startup" hype; cost of travel and fees for students.
- Hackathon rules on pre-built code: never teach shortcuts that break them.

### Can 2-minute lessons move it?

Little on its own. Planning from past data, safe-to-speak team habits and saying
a pitch out loud are teachable, but "winning" needs real events. Better as 2-3
lessons inside a "work smart" or "projects" track than a standalone track.

## 5. Energy and rest ("8 hours ≠ rested")

All items here are **information, not medical advice**. No health promises.

### Teachable claims

| # | Claim (plain) | Source quote | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Adults 18-60 need 7 or more hours | "7 or more hours" per day for ages 18-60 | CDC, [About Sleep](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html) | Gov guideline |
| 2 | Young adults may need more than 9 hours | "Adults should sleep 7 or more hours per night on a regular basis"; "Sleeping more than 9 hours per night on a regular basis may be appropriate for young adults" | AASM/SRS consensus 2015, [PMC4434546](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4434546/) | Expert consensus |
| 3 | You can't feel how short sleep is affecting you | "Sleepiness ratings suggest that subjects were largely unaware of these increasing cognitive deficits" | Van Dongen et al. 2003, Sleep, [PMID 12683469](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12683469/) | B |
| 4 | Regular timing matters, not just total hours | Irregular sleep "associated with delayed circadian rhythms and lower academic performance"; "A positive correlation (r = 0.37 ...) between academic performance and SRI" | Phillips et al. 2017, Sci Rep, 61 students, [PMID 28607474](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28607474/) | C (correlational) |
| 5 | Big data backs regularity as a strong signal | "Sleep regularity was a stronger predictor of all-cause mortality than sleep duration" | Windred et al. 2024, Sleep, UK Biobank, mean age 62.8, [PMID 37738616](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37738616/) | B (observational, older adults) |
| 6 | A weekend lie-in does not fully undo short weekday sleep | "weekend recovery sleep is not an effective strategy to prevent metabolic dysregulation associated with recurrent insufficient sleep" | Depner et al. 2019, Curr Biol, small lab study, [PMID 30827911](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30827911/) | C |
| 7 | Grogginess after waking is normal and fades | "Sleep inertia, or the grogginess felt upon awakening, is associated with significant cognitive performance decrements that dissipate as time awake increases"; worse "by prior sleep loss" | Hilditch & McHill 2019, Nat Sci Sleep, [PMID 31692489](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31692489/) | Review |
| 8 | Bright screens in bed can shift your body clock later | Reading on a light-emitting device: "took longer to fall asleep and had reduced evening sleepiness, reduced melatonin secretion, later timing of their circadian clock, and reduced next-morning alertness than when reading a printed book" | Chang et al. 2015, PNAS, [PMID 25535358](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25535358/) | C (small lab study) |
| 9 | Same bed and wake time daily is the standard advice | "Going to bed and getting up at the same time every day"; "Turning off electronic devices at least 30 minutes before bedtime" | CDC, [About Sleep](https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html) | Gov guidance |

### Myths to correct

| Myth | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| "8 hours, exactly, for everyone" | Guideline is "7 or more"; young adults may need over 9 (claims 1-2). |
| "I've adapted to 6 hours" | Performance kept dropping while felt sleepiness levelled off (claim 3). |
| "8 hours at any time is the same" | Irregular timing goes with later body clocks and worse grades (claim 4). |
| "I'll catch up on Sunday" | Not fully, in one lab study (claim 6). |
| "Groggy morning = bad sleep" | Sleep inertia is normal (claim 7). |

### Risks

- **Health promises.** Never say "sleep more and you'll live longer". Claim 5 is
  an association in older UK adults. Use it only as "researchers found timing
  mattered", if at all.
- **Medical issues.** Insomnia, apnoea, depression: lessons must not diagnose.
  Add a plain "if this lasts, talk to a doctor" line and the educational footer.
- **Real constraints.** Hostel noise, shared rooms, exam weeks, night shifts,
  family schedules. Missions should be choices, not rules.
- **Shame.** No "you're ruining your brain" copy.

### Can 2-minute lessons move it?

Knowledge, yes. Behaviour change is harder: it needs a fixed wake time and a
wind-down routine. This fits NANDI's existing night mode and wind-down content.
Missions: "same wake time for 3 days", "phone outside reach tonight".

## 6. Focus and "cheap dopamine"

### Teachable claims

| # | Claim (plain) | Source quote | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | You can't "fast" from dopamine | Dopamine "doesn't actually decrease when you avoid overstimulating activities, so a dopamine 'fast' doesn't actually lower your dopamine levels" | Grinspoon 2020, Harvard Health Publishing, [page](https://www.health.harvard.edu/blog/dopamine-fasting-misunderstanding-science-spawns-a-maladaptive-fad-2020022618917) | Expert page |
| 2 | The useful core is stimulus control from CBT | Original idea: "a method, based on cognitive behavioral therapy, by which we can become less dominated by the unhealthy stimuli" | Same | Expert page |
| 3 | Change the situation, not just your willpower | Situational strategies "can nip a tempting impulse in the bud" and "may be especially effective"; example: "the student who goes to the library without a cell phone" | Duckworth, Gendler & Gross 2016, Perspect Psychol Sci, [PMID 26817725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26817725/) | Review |
| 4 | Urges are common and mostly harmless; the setting shapes whether you act | "desires are frequent, variable in intensity, and largely unproblematic"; "situational factors showed relatively more influence on components later in the process (resistance and behavior enactment)" | Hofmann et al. 2012, JPSP, 7,827 reports, [PMID 22149456](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22149456/) | B |
| 5 | If-then plans work | "implementation intentions had a positive effect of medium-to-large magnitude (d= .65) on goal attainment" (94 tests) | Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, [KOPS record](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/2e749bfb-8533-437c-8203-7e788c910c5f) | A |
| 6 | Hard things can become rewarding | "effort can also add value"; "sometimes we select options precisely because they require effort" | Inzlicht, Shenhav & Olivola 2018, Trends Cogn Sci, [PMID 29477776](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29477776/) | Review |
| 7 | Rewarded effort builds a taste for effort | "reinforcement for increased ... performance ... conditions rewards value to the sensation of high effort and thereby reduces effort's aversiveness" | Eisenberger 1992, Psych Rev, [PMID 1594725](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1594725/) | Theory + review |
| 8 | Batch notifications instead of killing them all | Batched "three-times-a-day felt more attentive, productive, in a better mood"; no notifications at all: "higher levels of anxiety and "fear of missing out" (FoMO)" | Fitz et al. 2019, Computers in Human Behavior, n = 237, [Duke record](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1402953) | C (one field experiment) |
| 9 | A break from one app can stick | Deactivating Facebook "increased subjective well-being" and "caused a large persistent reduction in post-experiment Facebook use" | Allcott et al. 2020, AER, [AEA page](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20190658) | B (large RCT, US) |

### Myths to correct

| Myth | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| "Dopamine detox resets your brain chemistry" | Wrong chemistry (claim 1). Keep the practical part: friction and cue control. |
| "Willpower is a battery that runs out" | 23-lab replication: "(d = 0.04, 95% CI [-0.07, 0.15])" ([Hagger et al. 2016, PMID 27474142](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27474142/)); 36-lab test: "preregistered analyses did not find evidence for a depletion effect" ([Vohs et al. 2021, PMID 34520296](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34520296/)). X. |
| "A phone on the desk drains your brain" | Two direct replications of Ward et al. 2017 failed ([FLoRA replication atlas](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1086/691462/)); a meta-analysis of 22 studies found "a significant overall negative effect of smartphone use and presence" with high heterogeneity ([Böttger et al. 2023, PMID 37754029](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37754029/)). Contested (X). Putting the phone away is still a cheap, sensible step. |
| "Good self-control = resisting harder" | Situation design does much of the work (claims 3-4). |
| "Delete every app / turn off every alert" | Full switch-off raised anxiety and FoMO in one study (claim 8). |

### Risks

- **Self-diagnosis.** "Dopamine addict" language medicalises normal use. Don't
  use "addiction" for everyday scrolling.
- **Guilt.** Focus copy can turn into shame about screen time. Keep it about
  tools, not character.
- **NANDI itself.** Fitz et al. compare notifications to slot machines. This
  track only works if NANDI keeps its own rules: no random rewards, no endless
  feed, a stopping screen.

### Can 2-minute lessons move it?

Yes. If-then plans and friction steps are short, concrete and testable tonight
("charge the phone outside the bedroom", "if I open Instagram after 11, then I
open NANDI wind-down instead"). Strong fit for NANDI's purpose.

## 7. Reasoning like a detective

### Teachable claims

| # | Claim (plain) | Source quote | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mental shortcuts are usually fine but err in predictable ways | Heuristics "are highly economical and usually effective, but they lead to systematic and predictable errors" | Tversky & Kahneman 1974, Science, [PMID 17835457](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17835457/) | A |
| 2 | Turn % into "out of 100 people" to use base rates right | Difficulties "can be considerably reduced by communicating the information in terms of natural frequencies rather than in terms of probabilities" | Hoffrage et al. 2000, Science, [PMID 11188724](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11188724/) | A (see next row) |
| 3 | This holds across 20 years of studies; pictures help too | Meta-analysis of "35 articles representing 226 performance estimates"; "visual aids demonstrated some of the strongest moderation effects" | McDowell & Jacobs 2017, Psych Bull, [PMID 29048176](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29048176/) | A |
| 4 | "Consider the opposite" beats "try to be fair" | "the induction of a consider-the-opposite strategy had greater corrective effect than more demand-laden alternative instructions to be as fair and unbiased as possible" | Lord, Lepper & Preston 1984, JPSP, [PMID 6527215](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6527215/) | B |
| 5 | Nobody reads liars well | "people achieve an average of 54% correct lie-truth judgments"; and "individuals regard their interaction partners as honest" | Bond & DePaulo 2006, PSPR, 24,483 judges, [PMID 16859438](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16859438/) | A |
| 6 | One short training session can reduce bias for months | "a single training intervention can improve decision making"; effects "persisted at least 2 months later"; "Games, which provided personalized feedback and practice, produced larger effects than did videos" | Morewedge et al. 2015, PIBBS, [City open access](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/12324) | B |
| 7 | It can transfer to a real decision later | "Trained participants were 19% less likely to choose the inferior hypothesis-confirming solution than untrained participants" | Sellier, Scopelliti & Morewedge 2019, Psych Sci, N = 290, [PMID 31347444](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31347444/) | B |
| 8 | Start from "how often does this usually happen?" (reference class) | "Probability training corrected cognitive biases, encouraged forecasters to use reference classes" | Mellers et al. 2014, Psych Sci, [PMID 24659192](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24659192/) | B |

### Myths to correct

| Myth | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| "A sharp detective reads lies from eyes and fidgeting" | 54% accuracy, close to chance (claim 5). TV mentalists are fiction. |
| "Deduction gives certainty" | Real-world inference is about likelihood and base rates (claims 2, 8). |
| "Just try harder to be unbiased" | Generic "be fair" instructions did less than a concrete strategy (claim 4). |
| "Bias training never leaves the classroom" | One field test says it can (claim 7). Still a single study. |
| "Snap judgments of faces are accurate" | Faces are judged in 100 ms, and extra time "may simply boost confidence in judgments" ([Willis & Todorov 2006, PMID 16866745](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16866745/)). Speed is not accuracy. |

### Risks

- **Stereotyping.** "Base rates" about groups of people can turn into prejudice.
  Use base rates for events and situations (exam results, phone scams, delays),
  not for judging individuals by caste, religion, region or looks.
- **Accusation.** "Spot the liar" games can damage trust with family and friends.
  Teach the 54% finding instead.
- **Fiction framing.** Drishyam/Mentalist-style hooks are fine as hooks; the teach
  card must say what real research found.

### Can 2-minute lessons move it?

Yes, and this is the best-evidenced case for short lessons: Morewedge and
Sellier used one short session, and practice with feedback beat passive video.
NANDI's daily mystery and puzzles already fit. Add explicit "consider the
opposite" and "out of 100" steps to mystery solutions.

## 8. Presenting yourself (posture, light, confidence)

### Teachable claims

| # | Claim (plain) | Source quote | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Close-up selfies distort your face; step back | "selfies increase nasal size by 30% in males and 29% in females compared with an orthographic projection"; "an image taken at 5 ft, a standard portrait distance, results in essentially no difference in perceived size" | Ward et al. 2018, JAMA Facial Plast Surg, [PMC5876805](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5876805/) | C (mathematical model) |
| 2 | People notice your look far less than you think | "people overestimate the extent to which their actions and appearance are noted by others, a phenomenon dubbed the spotlight effect" | Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky 2000, JPSP, [PMID 10707330](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10707330/) | B |
| 3 | You come across better than you fear | Liking gap: people "systematically underestimated how much their conversation partners liked them" | Boothby et al. 2018, [PMID 30183512](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30183512/) | B |
| 4 | Open posture may change how you *feel*, not your hormones | "our results showed a significant effect of power posing on self-reported feelings of power. However, we found no significant effect of power posing on hormonal levels or in any of the three behavioral tasks" | Ranehill et al. 2015, Psych Sci, N = 200, [PDF](https://www.zne.uzh.ch/dam/jcr:e5fc4c3c-d50e-4aa9-96ab-4389e426d20d/Psychological%20Science-2015-Ranehill-0956797614553946.pdf) | B (feelings), X (hormones) |
| 5 | Let them hear you, not just read you | Evaluators "rated a candidate as more competent, thoughtful, and intelligent when they heard a pitch rather than read it"; "Adding visual cues to audio pitches did not alter evaluations" | Schroeder & Epley 2015, [PMID 25926479](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25926479/) | B |
| 6 | Taking and posting selfies can make you feel worse | Women who posted selfies "reported feeling more anxious, less confident, and less physically attractive afterwards"; "even when participants could retake and retouch their selfies" | Mills et al. 2018, Body Image, n = 110, [PMID 30149282](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30149282/) | B (one experiment) |
| 7 | Heavy photo editing goes with more body worries | Higher "manipulation of and investment in these photos ... were associated with greater body-related and eating concerns" | McLean et al. 2015, Int J Eat Disord, girls aged ~13, [PMID 26311205](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26311205/) | C (cross-sectional) |
| 8 | "This image was edited" labels don't protect viewers | Review "concludes that disclaimers are an ineffective means of protecting body satisfaction" | Tiggemann 2022, Body Image, [PMID 35259655](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35259655/) | Review |

No peer-reviewed source was found or checked for lighting tips (window light,
golden hour). Treat lighting as photography craft, not research claims.

### Myths to correct

| Myth | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| "2 minutes of power posing boosts testosterone" | No hormone effect in N = 200 (claim 4). Lead author Carney later wrote "I do not believe that 'power pose' effects are real" ([NPR via VPM](https://www.vpm.org/npr-news/2016-10-01/power-poses-co-author-i-do-not-believe-the-effects-are-real)). Cuddy's reply argues postural feedback effects remain ([Cuddy et al. 2018, PMID 29498906](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29498906/)). Contested on feelings; failed on hormones. |
| "Everyone noticed my hair/skin today" | Spotlight effect (claim 2). |
| "A selfie shows what you really look like" | Close range distorts (claim 1). |
| "Filters/edits are harmless fun" | Editing links to body concerns (claim 7); selfie-posting lowered mood (claim 6). |

### Risks

- **Body image is the main risk for 18-25s.** "Look good in every picture" can
  push appearance focus, editing and comparison, which the evidence links to
  worse body image (claims 6-8).
- **Guardrails if shipped:** no body-shape, skin-colour, weight or face-shape
  advice; no "fix your flaws"; no before/after images; no filters or editing
  tips. Frame as "the camera lies, people notice less, your voice and words
  count" (claims 1, 2, 5).
- **Colourism.** In India, "look better" content often drifts into fairness
  ideals. Ban skin-tone advice outright.

### Can 2-minute lessons move it?

Partly. The safe, evidenced lessons are short and reassuring (step back from the
camera; spotlight effect; say it out loud). The appearance-improvement framing
of the original video is the riskiest of all 8 tracks. Recommend reframing as
"Show up with confidence" with zero appearance-rating content.
