# cj4 · Hobbies to try off the phone

Job 6 of research batch 4. Written 23 Oct 2026.
Audience: NANDI content and product editors.
Question: which beginner hobbies can NANDI hand to Indian 18-25s so they put the phone down and do something real?

Status: research only. No app code or content JSON changed.

---

## 1. Short answer

- 23 candidates ranked on the six criteria below. Writing, hand lettering, reading, drawing and origami come out on top: ₹0, silent, a first win in 10 minutes, and fine on a tired day.
- Only **6 of the 23** have a peer-reviewed or university source showing visible beginner progress within about 2 weeks. They are juggling, singing pitch, a first tune on an instrument, microgreens, a gentle stretching routine and a first magic trick. For the other 17, NANDI must not promise "you'll see progress in a week". It can let people compare their own day 1 and day 7 instead.
- Of Jenil's three examples, drawing ranks high (4th). Singing (16th) loses points for noise in a shared room. Videography (17th) loses points for phone pull and privacy, and no source shows beginner progress for it.
- Wellbeing findings are real but modest, and most are correlational. Report them as research findings, never as promises.
- Seven hobbies have a market that pushes gear or paid courses: calligraphy, photography, videography, instruments, magic, knitting and stretching or yoga. Every one of them has a ₹0 start, and NANDI should teach only that.

### 2a. Creative activity and wellbeing

|---|---|---|
| Conner, DeYoung & Silvia 2018, *J Positive Psychology* 13(2):181-189. ### 2b. What helps a beginner keep going

| Idea | Source (quoted) | What it means for NANDI |
|---|---|---|
| Fun now beats benefits later | Woolley & Fishbach 2017, *PSPB*. [doi:10.1177/0146167216676480](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167216676480); [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27899467): "immediate rewards are more strongly associated with actual persistence in a long-term goal." Also "immediate rewards (e.g., enjoyment) predicted current persistence at New Year's resolutions whereas delayed rewards did not" | Pick the hobby people *enjoy* on day 1. Make the first session fun. Do not sell a hobby on its long-term benefits. |
| Small near steps | Bandura & Schunk 1981, *JPSP* 41:586-598 ([PDF read](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/59faaf5b01b9500001e95457/5bc552d85141987915dab842_Bandura%20&%20Schunk,%201981.pdf)): "Under proximal subgoals, children progressed rapidly in self-directed learning … and developed a sense of personal efficacy and intrinsic interest in arithmetic activities that initially held little attraction for them. Distal goals had no demonstrable effects." | Each "Try it" day is one small, clear target, such as "one clean throw-and-catch". Avoid far goals like "learn guitar". Note that this study was with children and arithmetic. |
| Feedback beats repetition | Berglin, Pfordresher & Demorest 2022, *Psychology of Music* 50(4). [doi:10.1177/03057356211026730](https://doi.org/10.1177/03057356211026730); abstract at [Buffalo](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/the-effect-of-visual-and-auditory-feedback-on-adult-poor-pitch-re/): "the effect of training was only significant for participants who received visual feedback training" | Every Try-it needs a way to check yourself: a reference photo, a recording, a count. Repetition without feedback did much less in this study. |
| Practice matters, but it is not everything | Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald 2014, *Psych Science*. [doi:10.1177/0956797614535810](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614535810); [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/24986855): "deliberate practice explained 26% of the variance in performance for games, 21% for music, 18% for sports … We conclude that deliberate practice is important, but not as important as has been argued." | Do not use "10,000 hours" copy. Do not promise that everyone reaches the same level. Do promise that people beat their own day 1. |
| Mindset messages are weak | Sisk et al. 2018, *Psych Science*. [doi:10.1177/0956797617739704](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617739704); [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/29505339): "Overall effects were weak for both meta-analyses." | A pep talk will not keep people going. A visible small win will. |
| "Tone deaf" is usually not true | Cuddy et al. 2005, *Ann NY Acad Sci*. [doi:10.1196/annals.1360.026](https://doi.org/10.1196/annals.1360.026); [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/16597781): "Nearly 17% … self-reported tone deafness" but "many individuals who consider themselves 'tone-deaf' may not, in fact, have perceptual difficulties". Berkowska & Dalla Bella 2009, *Adv Cogn Psychol*. [doi:10.2478/v10053-008-0068-2](https://doi.org/10.2478/v10053-008-0068-2): "Most occasional singers can sing in tune and in time, provided that they perform at a slow tempo." | A strong hook for singing: "Think you can't sing? Most people can at a slow tempo." |
| "I can't draw" is mostly about seeing | Cohen & Bennett 1997, *JEP:HPP*. [doi:10.1037/0096-1523.23.3.609](https://doi.org/10.1037//0096-1523.23.3.609); [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/9180037): "motor coordination is a very minimal source of drawing inaccuracies … the artist's misperception of the object is the major source of drawing errors." | Teach looking (measure, compare, draw what you see) before hand skill. Do not frame drawing as a gift you either have or lack. |
| A popular drawing trick did not hold up | Ostrofsky et al. 2016, *Empirical Studies of the Arts*. [doi:10.1177/0276237416634851](https://doi.org/10.1177/0276237416634851); [PDF read](https://oudev.stockton.edu/social-behavioral-sciences/documents/ostrofsky2016inversion.pdf): "This result fails to empirically validate the effectiveness of drawing models upside-down for the purposes of facilitating drawing accuracy." | Do not teach "draw it upside down" as a proven method, and do not use "right-brain" copy. |
| "Chess makes you smarter" is a myth | Sala & Gobet 2017, *Learning & Behavior*. [doi:10.3758/s13420-017-0280-3](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13420-017-0280-3); [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28646322): "These results suggest that the effects (if any) of chess instruction, when rigorously tested, are modest". Sala & Gobet 2020, music training meta-analysis, *Memory & Cognition* ([doi:10.3758/s13421-020-01060-2](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-020-01060-2); [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32728850)): "Once the quality of study design is controlled for, the overall effect of music training programs is null". Both studies were with children. | Offer chess and music for their own sake. Never claim they raise IQ or marks. |

### 2c. Low-energy fit

| Source (quoted) | Our reading |
|---|---|
| ten Brummelhuis & Bakker 2012, *J Occup Health Psych*. [doi:10.1037/a0029213](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0029213); [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/22799771): "leisure activities (social, low-effort, and physical activities) increased next morning vigor through enhanced psychological detachment and relaxation. High-duty off-job activities (work and household tasks) reduced vigor" | On a low day, a hobby has to feel like leisure, not like another duty. Low-effort counts. Cooking for the hostel or a "must practise" task may feel like duty. |
| Sonnentag & Fritz 2007, *J Occup Health Psych*. [doi:10.1037/1076-8998.12.3.204](https://doi.org/10.1037/1076-8998.12.3.204); [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/17638488): "four recovery experiences can be differentiated: psychological detachment from work, relaxation, mastery, and control" | Our inference: relaxation and mastery are different things. On low days, offer *relaxation-type* sessions: repeat something you already know, like folding the same crane or a familiar doodle. Keep *mastery-type* sessions (a new step) for normal days. |
| Ohly et al. 2016, systematic review of Attention Restoration Theory. [doi:10.1080/10937404.2016.1196155](https://doi.org/10.1080/10937404.2016.1196155); [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/27668460): "Meta-analyses provided some support for ART … The remaining 10 meta-analyses did not show marked beneficial effects." | Nature-noticing hobbies (birds, windowsill plants) are pleasant and low effort. Their "restores your focus" evidence is mixed, so do not claim it. |

## 3. Scoring

Each criterion is scored 0, 1 or 2, for a maximum total of 12.

| Criterion | 2 | 1 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost | ₹0, uses what is at home or in a hostel | small one-off buy (editor estimate under about ₹300) | needs real spending |
| Space and noise | silent, fits on a bed or desk | needs floor space, a window, or makes some sound | loud, or needs a kitchen or outdoors |
| First win in 10 min or less | yes | within one session (about 30 min) | takes days |
| Low-energy fit | works lying or sitting when tired; no new learning needed | OK if the step is small | needs energy or focus |
| Phone needed | no | camera or a tool (tuner, timer) | needs apps or a screen to do it |
| Safety | negligible | small risk, manageable with a rule | real risk; needs rules |

Cost bands are editor estimates, not sourced prices.

Evidence grade for 1-2 week beginner progress:
- **A**: a peer-reviewed or university source shows visible progress within about 2 weeks.
- **B**: a source shows progress, but over a longer period or with no duration stated.
- **C**: no source found. NANDI must not promise quick progress for these.

### Why some scores are what they are

- **Singing** loses on noise: a shared hostel room is a real barrier. Humming and singing quietly are fine. Singing when the room is empty is better.
- **Videography** loses on phone pull, not on cost. The camera is fine. The risk is drifting into editing apps and posting. It also raises consent questions when filming people.
- **Cooking** is useful but scores lowest. Many hostels have no kitchen or ban appliances. It also involves heat and knives, and on a tired day it can feel like a chore, which ten Brummelhuis & Bakker found drains vigor.
- **Microgreens** have the clearest visible progress of all (sprouts in days, a harvest in about 2 weeks). The first win, though, is "you sowed it", not "you made something".
- **Reading** scores 12 but is not a skill with steps. Treat it as a low-day rest option, not a Try-it track.

### B-grade sources (progress shown, but not in 1-2 weeks)

- **Drawing**: Vodyanyk & Jaeggi 2025, *Research on Aging*. [doi:10.1177/01640275251321813](https://doi.org/10.1177/01640275251321813); [Europe PMC / PMC12102519](https://europepmc.org/article/PMC/PMC12102519). "10 online lessons and daily practice in which participants acquired drawing techniques that improved their drawing skills by > 0.5 SD (d = 1.27)." From the full text: "Participants selected a 10- or 5-week schedule". The participants were aged 65-87, and the study reports "considerable attrition (47%)". **No 1-2 week drawing source was found.**
- **Origami**: Liu et al. 2026, *Journal of Intelligent Medicine* 3(2):97-113. [doi:10.1002/jim4.70029](https://doi.org/10.1002/jim4.70029); abstract at [journal.hep.com.cn](https://journal.hep.com.cn/jim/EN/10.1002/jim4.70029). ### C-grade: no source found

These hobbies have no source showing beginner progress in 1-2 weeks: writing, hand lettering or calligraphy, reading, paper puzzles or chess, rangoli or kolam, a new script, phone photography, bird noticing, mehendi, mending, videography, knitting or crochet, Rubik's cube, dance and cooking.

Calligraphy studies found were cross-sectional (experts compared with beginners), for example [Frontiers in Psychology 2026](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1932736). Cooking studies were multi-week, and Reicks et al. 2018 ([doi:10.1016/j.jneb.2017.08.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneb.2017.08.004)) note "Less than half of the studies included a control group".

For C-grade hobbies, NANDI should not claim quick progress. It can use a self-check instead: "Put day 1 and day 7 side by side. Which is closer to the real thing?"

## 6. Gear and paid-course flags

| Hobby | What the market pushes | ₹0 start NANDI should teach |
|---|---|---|
| Calligraphy or hand lettering | nib sets, brush pens, paid courses | any ballpoint, with thick downstrokes drawn by hand ("faux calligraphy") |
| Drawing | art kits, sketch-tablet apps, courses (paid drawing courses showed up in our searches) | one pencil, any paper, one object on the desk |
| Phone photography | lens clip-ons, presets, editing apps, paid courses | the phone's built-in camera only, with one composition rule a day |
| Videography | gimbals, mics, editing subscriptions | a 3-shot sequence (wide, medium, close) filmed handheld, with no edit |
| Cheap instrument | ukuleles, keyboards, subscription apps | start with humming or a 5-note tune. If anything, buy a harmonica or bansuri (editor estimate, a few hundred rupees). Never push a purchase. |
| Singing | vocal-coach apps and courses (singing-app company blogs showed up in our searches; their data is not peer-reviewed) | hum one note and match it; a free tuner display is optional |
| Magic | trick shops, paid "secret" courses (the study's trick was bought apparatus) | coin or card tricks that need only an ordinary coin or deck |
| Knitting or crochet | branded yarn kits | one ball of yarn and one hook. Small spend: score 1. |
| Stretching or yoga | mats, paid apps, classes | a towel on the floor |
| Rubik's cube | "speed cubes" | any basic cube, or skip it |
| Microgreens | grow kits, special trays | a cut milk packet or berry box with drainage holes (OSU suggests "recycled berry, milk or mushroom containers") and kitchen seeds |

Rule for editors: a Try-it must be completable with what a hostel student already has. Any purchase is optional and comes after day 7.

## 8. Low-day sort

This is our inference from Sonnentag & Fritz 2007 and ten Brummelhuis & Bakker 2012 (section 2c).

| Fine on a low day (repeat, relax) | Save for a normal day (new step, mastery) |
|---|---|
| Reading paper books | Juggling, first days |
| Folding a crane you already know | A new tune on an instrument |

## 9. Recommendation for Job 7 (Try-it tracks)

1. **Pilot set.** Start with hobbies that have both a high total and A-grade evidence: **juggling, singing, microgreens, gentle stretching and a cheap instrument (or humming a tune)**. Add **drawing** (B-grade, rank 4) because Jenil named it and it scores 11.
2. **Videography.** Keep it, as Jenil asked, but make it camera-only and no-post. Say plainly in copy that progress is measured by self-check, not by a study.
3. **Progress copy.** Only the six A-grade hobbies may say something like "in one study, beginners did X within a week". Everything else gets a day 1 vs day 7 self-check, with no promise.
4. **Wellbeing copy.** Use "in a study of 658 young adults, people felt better the day after doing more creative things than usual" (Conner 2018). Do not say "hobbies reduce depression", because Mak 2023 is 65+ and observational.
5. **Talent myths to bust in hooks.** "Most people who think they're tone-deaf aren't" (Cuddy 2005). "Drawing errors come from seeing, not your hand" (Cohen & Bennett 1997). "Chess and music won't raise your IQ, so do them for fun" (Sala & Gobet).
6. **Fun first.** Pick the day-1 task for enjoyment, not benefit (Woolley & Fishbach 2017). Make every day one small, near target (Bandura & Schunk 1981).
