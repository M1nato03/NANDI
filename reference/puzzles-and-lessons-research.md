# Expand Prime Pocket, Then Make It Stop

Prime Pocket should grow along four tracks, but one rule holds them together: every session has to end. For puzzles, add five small-grid types: 6×6 Mini Sudoku, a Queens-style "Crowns" board, a binary sun-and-moon grid, a Zip-style "Number Path", and 5×5–6×6 Calcudoku. All five can be generated in the browser with one recipe. You plant a random solution, derive clues from it, then remove clues only while a solution counter still finds exactly one answer and a human-style logic solver still finishes without guessing. The Bhagavad Gita supplies an 18-lesson arc on effort over outcome, emotional steadiness and attention (2.47, 2.62–63, 6.5, 6.35, 18.37). Those lessons can use verse texts checked across two open datasets and meanings written in our own words. The Charaka Samhita supplies about 15 lessons on sleep, eating and habit change, but only if each lesson shows its modern evidence grade. Its advice on sleep and food quantity holds up. Dosha theory, the incompatible-food rules and two of its daily rituals (lead-based kohl and herbal smoking) do not. Among other life topics, money for Indians (personal finance plus scam defence) has the strongest evidence, a 76-trial meta-analysis, and the most urgency, since 91% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY25. Game-style bias training and probability for traders come next. The design layer matters most. Each lesson should open with a guess, show one idea per screen and close with an if-then plan. The app must also limit itself, because screen time in bed is tied to insomnia whatever the content. A finite daily set of 10–15 minutes, forgiving streaks, no infinite feed and a hard night cap are what make Prime Pocket a replacement for Reels rather than another version of them.

## Five small grids share one generator that never guesses

### Puzzles sized for three minutes and a 390-pixel screen

LinkedIn has already tested the format at scale. Its Tango is a **6×6** grid, Zip is **7×7**, Mini Sudoku is **6×6 with 2×3 boxes and digits 1–6**, and Patches is **7×7** ([hintsheet](https://hintsheet.com/linkedin-games)). One fan guide estimates all seven LinkedIn games take **12–20 minutes together**, about two to three minutes each ([pinpointdaily](https://pinpointdaily.org/linkedin-games/)). Simon Tatham designed his classic puzzle collection for "two or three minutes" of play per puzzle ([Tatham](https://www.chiark.greenend.org.uk/~sgtatham/puzzles/)). LinkedIn reports **84% of players return the next day and 80% within a week** ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-adds-zip-puzzle-game/742847/)). These are company figures with no stated base. LinkedIn's puzzles are also "handcrafted by humans, not AI-generated": three-time World Sudoku Champion Thomas Snyder makes or edits each one and says "AI is not yet there" ([Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/puzzles/connections/articles/meet-three-time-world-sudoku-092201785.html)). That hand curation is the hardest part to copy. A browser generator has to replace it with solver-based checking. India is LinkedIn's second-largest market, with **over 16 crore members** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/india-could-be-linkedin-s-top-market-in-2-3-yrs-country-head-pattabiraman-125090700258_1.html)), but no India-specific games data turned up.

The rules of the strongest candidates are short. Binary grids (Takuzu) need equal 0s and 1s per line and no three equal digits in a row ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takuzu)). LinkedIn's Tango adds "=" and "×" edge clues and drops Takuzu's unique-rows rule ([tango-unlimited](https://www.tango-unlimited.com/rules)). Queens places one crown per row, column and colour region, with no two crowns touching, even diagonally ([hintsheet](https://hintsheet.com/linkedin-games)). It is the classic one-star Star Battle ([Star Battle FAQ](https://www.puzzle-star-battle.com/faq.php)). Zip asks for "a single line that connects the number in sequence, while also filling every cell" ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-adds-zip-puzzle-game/742847/)). Calcudoku (the KenKen family) is a Latin square, meaning each digit appears once per row and column, plus arithmetic targets for small groups of cells called cages ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KenKen)). Patches is the Japanese rectangle-splitting puzzle Shikaku ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-adds-seventh-in-app-puzzle-game/815147/)).

| Puzzle (app name) | Rule in one line | Solve time | Skill it trains | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mini Sudoku 6×6 | 1–6 once per row, column, 2×3 box | 1–4 min | Elimination, scanning | **Build** |
| Crowns (Queens / 1★ Star Battle) | One crown per row, column, region; none touching | 1–4 min | Spatial pigeonhole reasoning | **Build** |
| Binary 6×6 (Tango/Takuzu) | Three of each symbol per line; no three in a row; =/× clues | 1–3 min | Local pattern inference | **Build** |
| Number Path (Zip) | One path through every cell, hitting 1, 2, 3… in order | 0.5–3 min | Planning, lookahead | **Build** |
| Calcudoku 5×5–6×6 | Latin square plus cage arithmetic | 2–8 min | Arithmetic plus logic | **Build** |
| Nonogram 8×8–10×10 | Row/column run lengths reveal a picture | 3–8 min | Line logic | Sixth type; picture reveal rewards |
| Equation Guess (Nerdle-style) | Guess a hidden equation in six tries | 2–5 min | Arithmetic deduction | Strong non-grid option |
| Futoshiki, Rectangles (Shikaku), static Mastermind | Inequalities / area rectangles / code from clues | 1–6 min | Ordering, factorising, elimination | Later variety |
| 9×9 Sudoku, Kakuro, Slitherlink, Masyu, Nurikabe | — | 5–20+ min | — | Too long, dense or hard to generate for daily phone play |

Solve times are the research team's estimates, not measured medians. Nerdle shows the daily format also works for maths: it reached **a million players in under a month** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerdle)). The five "build" types cover number logic (Sudoku, Calcudoku), spatial logic (Crowns), pattern logic (Binary) and planning (Number Path), and they share code. Sudoku, Calcudoku and Futoshiki run on one Latin-square solver. Crowns and two-star Star Battle run on one region solver. Rotating one featured type per weekday keeps the daily puzzle to a single three-minute decision. Run difficulty from easy on Monday to hardest at the weekend, and keep unlimited practice seeds visually secondary.

### One recipe guarantees one answer and a fair path to it

Tatham's developer notes give the standard. A fair puzzle is one a player "can only fail to complete through demonstrable lack of skill." Generating a solved grid and then "gradually remov[ing] clues for as long as the solver reports that it's still soluble" is a strategy that "cannot fail." And "your algorithm must never produce false positives" ([Tatham](https://www.chiark.greenend.org.uk/~sgtatham/puzzles/devel/writing.html)). The key point is that **a unique answer and a no-guess path are different properties**. Batenburg and Kosters show a uniquely solvable 2×4 nonogram that line-by-line logic cannot finish ([Batenburg & Kosters](https://liacs.leidenuniv.nl/~kosterswa/nonodec2012.pdf)). Peter Norvig's random-fill Sudoku generator produced puzzles where "many have multiple solutions, and a few (about 0.2%) have no solution" ([Norvig](https://www.norvig.com/sudoku.html)). So every generator needs two checks running together:

```
seed = hash(local date + puzzle type)          ← same board for anyone on the same day
  │
  ▼
1. PLANT    a random valid solution (filled grid, queen permutation, Hamiltonian path)
  │
  ▼
2. DERIVE   the full clue set from it (givens, regions, cages, =/× signs, waypoints)
  │
  ▼
3. STRIP    remove one clue at a time; keep the removal only if
              count(puzzle, limit = 2) === 1          → exactly one answer
              logicSolve(puzzle, maxLevel) succeeds   → no guessing needed
  │
  ▼
4. GRADE    difficulty = hardest technique used + number of hard steps
  │
  ▼
SHIP        ~300 ms budget per attempt; on failure try a new seed;
            after 3 failures use a small list of known-good seeds
```

The counter is a brute-force search that stops once it finds a second solution. The logic solver applies human techniques from cheapest to most expensive and records which ones it needed. One logic solver therefore does three jobs. It enforces fairness, grades difficulty and powers the hint button. Three engineering rules follow from this. First, seed the random number generator from the local date, so the daily board is reproducible and friends get the same puzzle. Second, check the player's grid against the rules, not against the stored solution, so a correct alternative answer is still accepted if a generator bug ever ships. Third, run generation in a Web Worker so the UI never stutters. Speed is not a concern at these sizes. Norvig's Python solver averages about **0.01 s per easy and 0.04 s per hard 9×9** ([Norvig](https://www.norvig.com/sudoku.html)). A pruned C++ Zip solver finishes the hardest 8×9 board in about **21 ms** ([Medium](https://medium.com/@ashwin5059198/beating-linkedins-zip-puzzle-in-c-a-deep-dive-in-optimized-search-99523797adde)). An ILP Queens solver takes **4–7 ms** from 6×6 to 12×12 ([aryanbpatel](https://github.com/aryanbpatel/queens-solver)). No phone-browser benchmarks were found, so time the generators on a mid-range Android before trusting the budget.

### Each grid type needs its own repair trick

| Type | How to plant | How to force uniqueness | Pitfall to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mini Sudoku | Pattern fill `v(r,c) = (bc·(r mod br) + ⌊r/br⌋ + c) mod N`, then shuffle digits, rows in bands, bands, columns in stacks | Remove givens (in symmetric pairs for looks) while count = 1 and the logic solver succeeds; for easy days allow only singles and stop removing early | Never transpose a 2×3-box grid (it changes the box shape); never ship without the counter |
| Crowns | Column permutation with neighbouring rows' columns at least 2 apart; then grow connected regions by random multi-source flood fill | While a rival solution exists, move one of the rival's crown cells into a neighbouring region, keeping the planted crown and region connectivity ([aryanbpatel](https://github.com/aryanbpatel/queens-solver)) | Random "Voronoi" regions are mostly multi-solution at N ≥ 8; big blobby regions make boring or guessy boards. Small skinny regions create the opening moves |
| Binary 6×6 | Only **14 of the 20 balanced 6-cell lines** avoid three in a row; build rows from these by backtracking while checking columns | Clue pool = 36 cell givens + 60 edge signs; remove while a line-pattern solver still finishes | Don't mix in Takuzu's unique-rows rule; pick one rule set per mode |
| Number Path | Warnsdorff-style random path with a snake fallback ([4rknova](https://www.4rknova.com/blog/2026/04/24/number-trail)) | Count with waypoint-order and connectivity pruning; when a rival path exists, add a wall it uses but the planted path doesn't, or a waypoint where they diverge; then strip redundant walls and waypoints | The cited generator does **no uniqueness check**, and many clones skip it |
| Calcudoku | Random Latin square, cages of 1–4 cells, ÷ or − for pairs, + or × for larger cages | Precompute each cage's valid digit tuples; if two solutions exist, split the cage where they differ or change its operation | "KenKen" is a trademark (see below) |
| Nonogram | Random image at **density about 0.5–0.6** with smoothed blobs, or curated pixel art | A line solver that determines every cell proves both uniqueness and no-guess; otherwise flip a cell in the stuck area and retry ([errata-nonogram](https://github.com/ikorfale/errata-nonogram)) | At 10×10 and 30–50% density, **under 2%** of random nonograms are line-solvable ([Batenburg & Kosters](https://liacs.leidenuniv.nl/~kosterswa/nonodec2012.pdf)) |

The QueensGamers engine shows the counting logic in its simplest form: "Finding one solution proves the puzzle is solvable. Failing to find a second solution proves it is unique" ([dev.to](https://dev.to/alwaysaladdin/building-queensgamers-1800-free-puzzles-and-an-open-source-typescript-engine-2ddj)). For a weekend 9×9 mode, note that **no uniquely solvable 9×9 Sudoku has 16 or fewer clues**, proven in 2012 with about 7 million CPU hours ([UCD](https://www.ucd.ie/news/2012/01JAN12/100111-There-is-no-16-clue-or-less-Sudoku-mathematician-proves.html)).

### Grade by technique, because clue count barely predicts difficulty

In Pelánek's analysis of 1,700+ Sudokus, the **number of givens correlated only r = 0.25–0.27** with human solve time. Constraint-propagation metrics reached **r = 0.68–0.86**, and a combined metric reached **0.84–0.95**. The dependency structure between steps mattered on its own, at r ≈ 0.67–0.69 ([Pelánek](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/sudoku-arxiv.pdf)). Tatham's Solo levels follow the same idea. Easy levels always offer "a square you can fill in with a single number", and harder levels need partial deductions ([Solo manual](https://www.chiark.greenend.org.uk/~sgtatham/puzzles/doc/solo.html)). Give each type an ordered technique ladder and score each puzzle as `maxLevel × 100 + sum of step costs`. For daily 6×6 Sudoku, cap the ladder at pairs. For Crowns: easy boards use basic elimination and "region confined to one row"; medium boards add pigeonhole reasoning, where k regions are confined to k rows; hard boards need a one-step contradiction. No published grading study exists for Queens, Tango or Zip, so log each solve time locally and re-fit the difficulty bands to the one person actually playing.

### Borrow MIT code, rename LinkedIn's games, and claim only practice

Tatham's collection is **MIT-licensed** and covers Sudoku, Keen (Calcudoku), Unequal (Futoshiki), Pattern (nonograms) and Unruly (binary), all built around no-guess solvability. Its C solvers can be ported to JavaScript if the MIT notice is credited ([Tatham](https://www.chiark.greenend.org.uk/~sgtatham/puzzles/)). QQwing is **GPL-2.0-or-later** ([qqwing man page](https://man.archlinux.org/man/qqwing.1.en)): read it, but don't paste it into a non-GPL app. The aryanbpatel Queens generator and the errata nonogram generator are MIT. QueensGamers states no licence. Nextoy holds the "KenKen" and "KenDoku" trademarks, so use **Calcudoku** or **Mathdoku** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KenKen)). Treat Queens, Tango, Zip and Patches as LinkedIn product names. "Crowns", "Binary", "Number Path" and "Rectangles" are neutral, and the app should use its own colours and symbols rather than LinkedIn's sun-and-moon art.

Be equally careful about what puzzles do for the mind. A major review found "extensive evidence that brain-training interventions improve performance on the trained tasks… and little evidence that training enhances performance on distantly related tasks" or "everyday cognitive performance" ([Simons et al. 2016](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1529100616661983)). A meta-analysis is bluntly titled "Working Memory Training Does Not Improve Performance on Measures of Intelligence" ([Melby-Lervåg et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691616635612)). The much-quoted ACTIVE result, **25% lower dementia incidence** over 20 years, came from computerised *speed-of-processing* training with boosters in adults over 65. Its memory and reasoning arms showed no reduction ([Johns Hopkins](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/news/newsroom/news-releases/2026/02/cognitive-speed-training-linked-to-lower-dementia-incidence-up-to-20-years-later)). Lumosity paid the FTC **$2 million** over brain-training claims ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges-its-brain-training-program)). The honest in-app line is: you get faster at the puzzles you practise, the evidence for boosting general intelligence is weak, and the real benefit is an active three minutes instead of a passive scroll.

## The Gita lands best as a performance-psychology dialogue

### A gifted performer freezes, and his mentor talks him through it

The Gita is a dialogue in 18 chapters set in the Mahabharata's Bhishma Parva. The usual count is 700 verses. Both open datasets used here count **701**, because they number Arjuna's opening question in chapter 13 as 13.1 ([gita/gita chapters](https://github.com/gita/gita/blob/main/data/chapters.json)). The setting is a ready-made hook for a student under pressure. In 2.7 Arjuna says his mind is "confused about dharma" and asks, "I am your student; teach me." In 2.3 Krishna tells him to "cast off this petty weakness of heart and stand up" ([vedicscriptures 2.7](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vedicscriptures/bhagavad-gita-data/main/slok/bhagavadgita_chapter_2_slok_7.json)). The teachings that carry best into a student's day fall into six themes: effort over outcome, emotional steadiness, attention and impulse control, self-leadership, lifestyle balance, and the ethics of speech and desire. A sceptical engineering student responds better to "a 2,000-year-old performance-psychology dialogue — test it on yourself" than to anything that asks for belief first. Eknath Easwaran's three-volume verse-by-verse *Bhagavad Gita for Daily Living* ([BMCM](https://www.bmcm.org/about/news/new-edition-bhagavad-gita-daily-living/)) and Chinmaya Mission's youth study groups ([CHYK](https://chykwest.org/study-groups/)) both use this application-first approach.

A few pairings stop the most common misreadings. Put 2.47 next to 18.14, which says every action depends on five factors, with *daiva* (chance or providence) as the fifth. The text gives the reason outcomes are not fully yours, and 2.47's second half warns against "attachment to inaction." So the lesson is to let go of what you can't control, not to stop caring. Teach 6.34–35 as a pair. Arjuna complains that the mind is as hard to hold as the wind, and Krishna answers "without doubt", then gives the method: *abhyāsa* (practice) and *vairāgya* (dispassion). The teacher agreeing first is what wins over a sceptic. Engineering metaphors fit well: 2.62–63 as a positive-feedback cascade (dwelling → attachment → desire → anger → delusion → memory loss → loss of judgement), the guṇas as clarity, drive and inertia, and 18.14 as a system with five inputs, only some of them controllable. All verse texts were checked against both datasets ([gita/gita](https://github.com/gita/gita/blob/main/data/verse.json); [vedicscriptures](https://github.com/vedicscriptures/bhagavad-gita-data)).

### Verified text, own-words meanings, public-domain flourishes

The research team compared the Devanagari of 83 verses character by character across the two independent open datasets. **74 matched exactly**, and the rest differed only in spelling conventions, apart from one real reading variant (2.64: *viyuktais* or *vimuktais*, same meaning) and the chapter-13 numbering offset ([gita/gita](https://github.com/gita/gita/blob/main/data/verse.json); [vedicscriptures](https://github.com/vedicscriptures/bhagavad-gita-data)). Cite verses as chapter.verse, and add "(13.8 in some editions)" for chapter 13. On the main card, show a plain romanisation (for example *karmany evadhikaras te ma phaleshu kadachana*) and put the exact IAST spelling behind a "precise pronunciation" tap. Write English meanings fresh from the Sanskrit. Indian law protects translations as works in their own right ([SpicyIP](https://spicyip.com/2017/02/printing-the-bhagavad-gita-copyright-protection-of-translations.html)). Being bundled in an open dataset does not put Prabhupada, Sivananda, Chinmayananda or Purohit Swami into the public domain. Easwaran's work is actively published under copyright ([BMCM](https://www.bmcm.org/store/bhagavad-gita-for-daily-living-volume-1/)). Edwin Arnold's 1885 verse translation ([Gutenberg #2388](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2388)) and Annie Besant's translation ([Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Bhagavad-Gita_(Besant_4th))) are safe for an occasional "classic rendering" card, but their thee-and-thou style should not stand in for the literal meaning. The team could not reach Gita Supersite (IIT Kanpur), so spot-check six anchor verses (2.47, 2.62, 2.63, 6.5, 6.35, 18.63) there before launch.

### Modern parallels, not scientific proof

Several Gita ideas line up with real findings. Students who mentally rehearsed the *process* of studying studied better and scored higher than students who pictured the *outcome* ([Pham & Taylor 1999](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/59faaf5b01b9500001e95457/5bc55b26e337d65684a37d55_Pham%20&%20Taylor,%201999.pdf)), which supports 2.47. Habitual reappraisal goes with better wellbeing than habitual suppression ([Gross & John](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Individual-differences-in-two-emotion-regulation-Gross-John/18533cefd4f0ff44d0e3c1e1b9f90f0c7826a330)), which is why equanimity should be taught as non-reactivity, never as bottling up. Ruminating on anger increased anger compared with distraction ([Bushman 2002](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167202289002)), which matches 2.62–63. New habits took **about 66 days** to become automatic, with a wide range ([Gardner, Lally & Wardle](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3505409/)), which fits 6.35's emphasis on practice. The Gita-specific research, though, is mostly correlational self-report scales of *anāsakti* (non-attachment) and karma-yoga in Indian samples ([Mulla & Krishnan](https://rkvenkat.org/zubin_pds12.pdf)), plus conceptual papers that present the text as a complement to therapy ([Bhatia et al. 2013](https://journals.lww.com/indianjpsychiatry/fulltext/2013/55002/the_bhagavad_gita_and_contemporary_psychotherapies.33.aspx)). Two warnings apply. Meditation research is prone to overclaiming ([Van Dam et al., "Mind the Hype"](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/files/56912114/Mind_the_Hype.pdf)), and a 23-lab replication found the "willpower runs out" ego-depletion effect close to zero ([Hagger et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616652873)). The card line that stays honest is "Ancient insight, modern parallel: not the same thing, but pointing the same way." If a learner shows signs of real distress, the app should point to professional help, not to a verse.

### Handle caste, gender, war and devotion directly

Verse 4.13 ("the fourfold varṇa was created by me according to guṇa and karma") and 18.41–48 divide duties by varṇa. They should never be lesson content. Teach svadharma (3.35, 18.47) as "your own responsibilities and strengths: better your own path done imperfectly than someone else's done well." Then say plainly that birth-based readings exist, that Gandhi re-read varṇa as qualities and work ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7473828/)), and that Ambedkar criticised the text for legitimising caste ([Barua 2019](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0971685818805328)). Render 2.3's *klaibya* ("unmanliness") as "cowardly weakness" and note the change. Keep the battlefield honest: the text is set at a real war, and reading it as an inner battle is one interpretation. Don't extract "fight" as advice. Leave out 18.66 ("abandoning all dharmas, take refuge in me alone") from a practical course. Flag 5.10 and 12.15 as devotional while offering a secular reading. Pair 4.40 ("the doubter perishes") with 4.34 (learn by questioning) and 18.63 ("reflect on it fully, then do as you wish"), so the course never discourages questioning. Ending each lesson with a 24-hour micro-experiment, such as counting how often you bring the mind back during one study block (6.26), matches the spirit of 18.63.

## Charaka earns trust only by grading its own advice

### A layered, roughly 2,000-year-old text, not a 5,000-year-old one

The Charaka Samhita began as Agnivesha's work. Charaka revised it, which Meulenbeld dates to **about 100 BCE–200 CE**. Dridhabala later wrote about **a third** of it to replace lost portions ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charaka_Samhita); [Maas](https://www.academia.edu/247343/On_what_became_of_the_Carakasa%E1%B9%83hit%C4%81_after_D%E1%B9%9B%E1%B8%8Dhabala_s_revision)). Sources put Dridhabala around the 5th or 6th century CE. The text has **8 sections and 120 chapters** and, with the Sushruta Samhita and Ashtanga Hridaya, forms classical Ayurveda's "great three." Lessons should say "written down roughly 2,000 years ago, revised over centuries" and "the Charaka Samhita says", not "Charaka wrote", and should avoid the marketing claim of "5,000 years." Verse numbers follow the free *Charak Samhita New Edition* ([New Edition](https://www.carakasamhitaonline.com/index.php/Charak_Samhita_New_Edition)) and can be off by one or more in other editions, so cite "Sutrasthana 7.34 (New Edition numbering)."

Three ideas from Sutrasthana 11 are the most useful for a student. The **three pillars of life** are food, sleep and *brahmacharya* (Su 11.35) ([Tistraishaniya](https://www.carakasamhitaonline.com/index.php/Tistraishaniya_Adhyaya)). The New Edition translates *brahmacharya* as "celibacy". Present it as self-control over indulgence and note the literal translation, rather than prescribing celibacy. ***Prajñāparādha***, the "error of judgement", is wrong action that comes from failures of understanding, restraint and memory (Sh 1.99–102) ([New Edition](https://www.carakasamhitaonline.com/index.php/Prajnaparadha)). It is a 2,000-year-old name for the gap between intention and behaviour: knowing you should sleep and scrolling anyway. It also links naturally to the bias track below. ***Sattvāvajaya*** means "withdrawal of mind from harmful objects" (Su 11.54). It sounds like what psychologists call self-regulation, but it is not equivalent to CBT. Tridosha and *agni* (digestive fire) run through the whole text. Teach them as how ancient physiology explained things, because critics describe doshas as "a fictional concept" ([Wikipedia: Ayurveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayurveda)).

The text contains some striking gems. Su 7.36–38 prescribes swapping a bad habit for a good one **a quarter at a time**, completing the swap by day seven ([Navegandharaniya](https://www.carakasamhitaonline.com/index.php/Naveganadharaniya_Adhyaya)). Su 7.34 says staying awake at night should not be overdone even by people used to it. Su 8.27 says "one should not be in a habit of postponing things… should not lose spirit" ([Indriyopakramaniya](https://www.carakasamhitaonline.com/index.php/Indriyopakramaniya_Adhyaya)). Vi 1.24–25 gives eating rules with a reason for each, and Vi 2.3 says to fill one-third of the stomach with food, one-third with liquid and leave one-third empty ([Rasa Vimana](https://www.carakasamhitaonline.com/index.php/Rasa_Vimana); [Trividhakukshiya](https://carakasamhitaonline.com/index.php?title=Trividhakukshiya_Vimana)). Sh 2.46 sums up the healthy person in one verse, including *samīkṣyakārī*, one who acts after thinking ([Atulyagotriya](https://www.carakasamhitaonline.com/index.php/Atulyagotriya_Sharira)). One popular attribution needs correcting. **"Exercise at half your capacity" is Sushruta's line** (Chikitsa 24.47). Charaka says only "in moderation" and lists signs for when to stop ([New Edition: Vyayama](https://www.carakasamhitaonline.com/index.php/Vyayama)).

### Health claims carry legal weight in India

A JAMA study found **20.7%** of Ayurvedic medicines bought online contained lead, mercury or arsenic, rising to **40.6%** for metal-based rasa shastra products. Claims of good manufacturing practice made no difference ([Saper et al.](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/182460)). The Drugs and Magic Remedies Act, 1954 bans advertising remedies for listed diseases. The Supreme Court's contempt action against Patanjali, which ordered suspended products off shelves in May 2024, shows the law is enforced ([SC Observer](https://www.scobserver.in/journal/patanjali-contempt-case-blatant-violations-expanded-scope-repeated-scoldings-and-the-flip-flip-on-rule-170/)). When the Court let AYUSH's deletion of the Rule 170 pre-approval requirement take effect in August 2025, it told states to enforce the 1954 Act "in letter and spirit" ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/ayush-ads-get-green-light-supreme-court-lifts-ban-on-pre-approval-rule/)). The lessons should never say a practice treats, cures or prevents a named disease. They should recommend no herbs or formulations and carry a standing "educational, not medical advice" footer. The same discipline applies to the Ayurvedic products venture: lines like "head oil prevents greying" are the text's claims, not evidence, and must not become product copy. ## Money and clear thinking beat every other topic on evidence

### A ranked shortlist built on outcome evidence and his own goals

Financial education is the rare life topic with strong trial evidence. A meta-analysis of **76 randomised trials (about 160,000 people, 33 countries)** found it improves both knowledge and behaviour, "especially when it comes to budgeting, savings, and credit", with effects "three to five times greater than reported in previous research" ([GFLEC](https://gflec.org/metaanalysis/)). India's starting point is low: only **27% of adults** are financially literate, though Gujarat is at 34% and 18–29-year-olds are the most literate group ([NCFE](https://ncfe.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ExecSumm_.pdf)). For a student who builds algo-trading systems, SEBI's data turns the trading interest into a teaching hook. **Over 91% of individual equity F&O traders lost money in FY25**, with aggregate net losses of **₹1,05,603 crore**, up 41% from the year before ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html)). Bias training is the second-best bet, and its evidence matches Prime Pocket's format. One interactive game session cut six biases by **at least 31.94% immediately and 23.57% two months later**, beating instructional video ([Morewedge et al. 2015](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/12324/)), and a follow-up reports the training carries over to real decisions ([Sellier et al. 2019](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0956797619861429)).

## Hook with a guess, end with a plan, then let go

### Open every lesson with a question he can almost answer

Asking for a guess before teaching works even when the guess is wrong. In Richland and colleagues' study, pretested items were recalled at **75% versus 56%**, even though only **5%** of pretest answers were correct. The advantage persisted after a week (55% versus 45%) ([Richland, Kornell & Kao](https://learninglab.uchicago.edu/Pre-Testing_files/RichlandKornellKao.pdf)). A review adds the condition: it works when the learner then gets to study the correct answer ([Pan & Carpenter 2023](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1393374)). *Predictions* add surprise. Answers that people predicted wrongly *with high confidence* were retained best, provided the correct answer arrived shortly afterwards ([Brod 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-021-01904-1)). Higher curiosity predicted better recall of surprising answers one to two weeks later ([Kang et al. 2009](https://neuroecon.berkeley.edu/papers/papers_files/Kang_PsychSci_2009.html)), and the memory boost from curiosity spilled over to material shown during the wait for the answer ([Gruber et al. 2014](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/10/141002123631.htm)). So the guess screen should carry a confidence tap (Sure / Think so / Guessing), and the reveal should arrive on the very next screen, with the lesson's one key picture and nothing in between.

Stories carry ideas, but only when the story *is* the idea. Narratives beat expository text on memory and comprehension by **Hedges' g = 0.55** across 33,078 participants, though with signs of publication bias ([Mar et al. 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-020-01853-1)). Willingham's four Cs (causality, conflict, complications, character) give the structure ([Willingham](https://mrbartonmaths.com/resourcesnew/8.%20Research/Explicit%20Instruction/The%20Privileged%20Status%20of%20Story2.pdf)). Interesting but irrelevant details reliably hurt learning across 58 studies, especially when they sit next to a key diagram or stay on screen ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2020/03/200319103223.htm)). Removing extraneous material improved learning in **23 of 23** tests ([Mayer & Fiorella](https://edtechuvic.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2022/09/principles-for-reducing-extraneous-processing-in-multimedia-learning-coherence-signaling-redundancy-spatial-contiguity-and-temporal-contiguity-principles.pdf)). A simple test for every card follows: if you delete it, does the learner lose the idea? If not, cut it. Multimedia research points the same way and fits a learner who needs the picture before the term. Labels placed *inside* a diagram (spatial contiguity, **d = 1.10**), teaching key terms before the explanation that uses them (pre-training, **d = 0.75**) and one idea per learner-paced screen (segmenting, **d = 0.79**) all have large lab effects ([Cambridge Handbook](https://cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-multimedia-learning/principles-for-managing-essential-processing-in-multimedia-learning-segmenting-pretraining-and-modality-principles/DD24C2F48B9B1277CE59F78276110258)). A meta-meta-analysis of 1,189 studies adds a caveat: good design matters less in self-paced formats ([Noetel et al. 2022](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1338120)). On a tap-to-advance phone lesson, the bigger lever is the activity (guess, predict, explain), not visual polish.

| # | Screen (≈ seconds) | What he sees (example: Gita 2.47) | Evidence behind it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scene (15) | 1:40 AM, refreshing a placement-results page; one line of text | Open with conflict and a character |
| 2 | Guess (20) | "What actually helps tonight?" 3–4 options plus a confidence tap | Pretesting (+19 points); confident predictions create surprise |
| 3 | Reveal + one picture (20) | Answer at once; two circles, "Effort (yours)" vs "Outcome (not yours)", labels inside, one highlight | Curiosity benefit is time-locked; spatial contiguity; signalling |
| 4 | Name it (10) | "This split is *karma* vs *karma-phala*", with an icon and a plain gloss | Picture before jargon; pre-training |
| 5 | Source line (15) | 2.47 in plain romanisation with our own gloss; IAST and full verse behind a tap | Authenticity without overload |
| 6–7 | Story beat (40) | Arjuna's version of the same dilemma in two cards | Narrative g = 0.55; every card carries the idea |
| 8 | Principle (10) | One-sentence rule; the diagram fades to icons | Concreteness fading ([Fyfe et al.](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1036777)) |
| 9 | Transfer check (25) | New situation (cricket trial, startup pitch) plus one item from an earlier lesson | Retrieval, transfer, interleaving |
| 10 | Why? (20) | Pick 1 of 3 explanations; free text optional | Self-explanation g = 0.55 ([Bisra et al.](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1186664)) |

The if-then plan on screen 11 is the lesson's real payload. If-then plans have a **medium-to-large effect on goal attainment (d = 0.65 across 94 tests)** ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/handle/123456789/10973)). Asking "Did your plan fire yesterday?" at the next session gives him a reason to come back that isn't a streak. Tone matters as much as structure. Teenagers made healthier real-world food choices (**+7 points** choosing water, **+11 points** healthy snacks) when the message framed resisting food marketers as autonomy and status, rather than as health advice ([Bryan, Yeager et al. 2016](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2016/09/160912173844.htm)). The sample was 13–14-year-olds, so treat the transfer to a 20-year-old as plausible but untested. Translated into lesson copy: "Reels are engineered by teams to hold your attention at 2 AM; 6.26 is a 2,000-year-old field guide to taking it back." Never "you should." Occasional "Do you buy this?" prompts with Agree / Partly / No give back the choice that lecturing takes away.

At the course level, apps converge on concepts under two minutes ([Nibble on Kinnu](https://nibble-app.com/blog/kinnu-app-review)) stacked into short sessions. Brilliant builds each introductory course from **50+ concepts with 20+ problems each** ([Brilliant](https://blog.brilliant.org/hand-crafted-machine-made/)). That ratio means each idea needs five to ten *different* scenarios, so spaced reviews show new situations rather than the same card again. A three-week "Gita for a Restless Mind" course fits this shape. It has three chapters of four or five lessons, each ending in a story chapter with inline questions and a match-the-pairs review (the Duolingo Stories model; [Duoplanet](https://duoplanet.com/duolingo-stories-the-complete-guide-what-you-need-to-know/)). Light days hold only reviews and plan check-ins, a mid-course mastery check mixes scenarios across chapters, and a branching capstone story ends with a surprise collectible verse card. Concepts move through mastery tiers, Seen → Familiar → Solid → Mastered, modelled on Khan Academy's 50/80/100-point levels, and they can decay ([Khan Academy](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/360007253831-What-is-self-paced-Mastery)).

Puzzles follow the same rules with a few additions. For a new puzzle type, let him try a small, intuitive board first and teach the technique afterwards. Problem-solving before instruction improved conceptual understanding and transfer across 53 studies ([ETH Zurich on Sinha & Kapur](https://ethz.ch/en/news-and-events/eth-news/news/2021/09/produktives-scheitern.html)), though the press framing is promotional. Hints should climb a three-rung ladder, all driven by the grading solver: a nudge ("check row 3"), then the named technique on a 4×4 mini example, then one move only. Aim practice puzzles at roughly 70–85% first-try success. The "85% rule" was derived for simple learners on binary classification ([Wilson et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-12552-4)), and a pre-registered study found difficulty-skill balance did not change enjoyment ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9890114/)), so track whether he returns rather than tuning to a number. Puzzles can also carry lesson content, for example a logic-grid mystery whose clues paraphrase the day's verse.

### Build the stopping point before the hook

The central risk is that a better app still wins the wrong time slot. In **45,202 Norwegian students aged 18–28**, each hour of screen use after going to bed was associated with **59% higher odds of insomnia and 24 fewer minutes of sleep**. Social media was no worse than other screen activities: "the type of screen activity does not appear to matter as much as the overall time spent using screens in bed" ([Hjetland et al. 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1548273/full)). The study is cross-sectional, but a meta-analysis of 125,198 young people found the same pattern, with bedtime device use linked to inadequate sleep (**OR 2.17**) ([Carter et al. 2016](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/fullarticle/2571467)). For someone with a baseline of about six phone hours a day, mostly at night, and a 1–2 AM bedtime, a Gita lesson at 1 AM is still screen time in bed. Prime Pocket's success metric therefore has to be **Reels minutes replaced and sleep protected**, not minutes spent in Prime Pocket.

The habit evidence shows what keeps people coming back without hooking them. Wordle's creator limited it to one puzzle a day because "having one puzzle per day creates a sense of scarcity" and keeps play to about three minutes ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordle)). Recommendations, autoplay and ads reduce users' sense of agency, while playlists, search and play controls increase it by letting people plan ahead ([Lukoff et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.11778)). Cliffhangers do not work the way the folklore claims. A 2025 meta-analysis found **no reliable Zeigarnik memory advantage** for unfinished tasks, but a robust urge to *resume* them, with **66.79%** resumption ([Ghibellini & Meier 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05000-w)). A teaser should point to tomorrow's scheduled lesson, never to an endless next card. Broken streaks cause a "double failure" that sharply cuts motivation, and streaks that can be kept across several activities motivate more ([Silverman & Barasch](https://phys.org/news/2024-03-streaks.html)). An AI-generated summary of Duolingo's retention team reports that **two streak freezes beat one and three were no better**, that earning back a lost streak helped, and that the flame icon did not resonate with Indian users ([podcast summary](https://www.getrecall.ai/summary/lennys-podcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-or-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team)). Treat these as indicative only, and don't copy Duolingo's 10 PM streak-saver notification. Rewards undermine motivation when they are expected and tied to completion (**d = −0.44**), while informational praise raises intrinsic motivation, especially for college students (**d = +0.43**), and *unexpected* rewards do no harm ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2001_DeciKoestnerRyan.pdf)). A 12-stamp card with 2 stamps pre-filled was completed faster than a 10-stamp card ([Kivetz et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2733214)), so progress bars should start partly full.

```
DAYTIME SESSION (finite, ~10–15 min, ends on a "Done" screen)
  1 new lesson ................ 3–5 min   (one per track per day)
  due flashcard reviews ....... ~2 min    (Greene, Gita, Charaka, money cards)
  today's puzzle (one type) ... 1–5 min   (weekday rotation, easy Mon → hard Sun)
  logic-grid mystery .......... existing
  → "Come back tomorrow"  (next lesson unlocks 7 AM)

NIGHT MODE (user-set; default begins right after the planned ~11 PM study session)
  puzzle + reviews only, 10-minute hard cap
  → "Phone down" screen with one verse card to think about, lights off
  no new lessons · no notifications after ~9 PM · day boundary at 4 AM
```

The remaining rules follow from the evidence above. Count *any* lesson, review or puzzle toward the streak, and start with two freezes plus earn-back. Never show a shame screen; "Welcome back — your 23-day best is saved" is enough. Pick a non-flame symbol such as a diya or lotus. Praise specific competence ("you spotted the attachment trap in three new situations") and make collectibles surprises. Keep the timer quiet, with a zen toggle, and no countdowns. Reset puzzles at local midnight or a chosen morning hour; LinkedIn resets at midnight Pacific, which is 12:30 or 1:30 PM in India ([pinpointdaily](https://pinpointdaily.org/linkedin-games/)). The share card can be plain text in the Wordle style, such as "Prime Pocket #142 · Crowns ★★ · 1:47 · no hints". Because boards are date-seeded, friends with the app get the same puzzle and can compare without a server. An optional two-tap self-report ("What did this replace tonight?" and "Lights out by?") measures the thing that actually matters.

## Conclusion

The research changes how the hard problems in this expansion should be ranked. In puzzles, uniqueness is the easy part: a counter that stops at two solutions handles it in milliseconds. Fairness is the hard part, and the logic solver that guarantees it is also the difficulty grader and the hint engine. Writing one good solver per family therefore stands in for the human curator LinkedIn pays for. In the lessons, the ancient texts become *more* convincing to a sceptical engineer when the app shows where they fail. Grading Charaka's "eat slowly or you'll overeat" as contradicted is what makes "sleep is a pillar of life" believable, and the convergence card (Gita 2.47, Epictetus, outcome bias and trading discipline) is the most valuable unit across all the tracks. Among life topics, the evidence points away from more philosophy and toward money, bias training and probability, the areas where a 20-year-old's choices compound fastest and where India's scam and F&O-loss numbers are worst.

The largest unknown is whether the substitution works at all. No study shows that replacing short-form video with a learning app improves sleep or wellbeing. Flow and difficulty-matching findings are shakier than ed-tech folklore suggests. There are no phone benchmarks for these generators and no India-specific puzzle data. That argues for building measurement in from day one: local solve times, if-then follow-through, and self-reported bedtime and "what this replaced." Treat the first month as an experiment he runs on himself, in the spirit of 18.63: reflect on it fully, then do as he wishes. If bedtime moves earlier and Reels minutes fall, the expansion is working, even if time in Prime Pocket stays small.
